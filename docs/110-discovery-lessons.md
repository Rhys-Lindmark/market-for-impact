## What the first100 change about the next ten shots

1. **Cheap access is not automatically cheap additional health.** ReCARES, hearing and eye-care leads motivate looking for a removable access obstacle, but fitting, sustained use and otherwise-available care determine the gain. Look for completion and use evidence, not distribution totals.
2. **Keep the entire ordinary gift in scope.** The pending GLIDE/Breathe and published PVF corrections show how a favorable small program can misrepresent an unrestricted organization gift. A candidate with a clean single-purpose operating model is easier to estimate; complexity is a uncertainty flag, not evidence of low value.
3. **Existing public support changes the counterfactual.** A waitlist or an expired historical contract does not establish a current funding gap. Look for additional staff/slots/transport/repair capacity and who pays for the necessary complements.
4. **Use unique people and finite trajectories.** HEPPAC's rescue correction cautions against counting each reversal as an independent lifetime saved. Apply the same discipline to recurring contacts, treatments and devices.
5. **Do not extrapolate every short effect over a life.** Earlier care often gains weeks or months rather than all remaining healthy years. Childhood can support long horizons only where the causal pathway and persistence justify them.
6. **Donated resources are not free resources.** Track donor cash and full resource costs separately. Test whether additional donated clinical time, equipment or inventory can actually expand.
7. **Do not screen out policy solely because its health evidence is harder.** Citywide changes may have large effects, but exposure, passage, implementation, attribution and displacement multiply. Require a coherent causal chain and null/harm cases; count housing units as an intermediate, not QALYs.
8. **Bay attribution is part of selection.** A globally excellent charity can have negligible local effects. The new ten should principally compete in this Bay-focused product; use international findings for calibration rather than filling the list.
9. **The published order is not a common-strength evidence hierarchy.** Existing100 reports share evidence and priors. A low central estimate driven by a highly favorable scenario needs stronger challenge, not extra confidence from its numerical rank.
10. **Negative findings improve the search.** Candidates should enter with a plausible advantage, but evidence may reject that advantage. Replace a duplicate or non-existent candidate; do not replace an honestly unfavorable finished report just to manufacture a winner.

## Cheap threshold checks before spending a report slot

For a direct-care mechanism, test the dimensions explicitly: cost per additional recipient / (incremental utility × supported duration) = dollars/QALY. Adjust for counterfactual care and attribution once, not repeatedly. An illustrative $1,000 recipient cost with0.1 utility for5years and50% additionality yields0.25QALY and$40,000/10QALYs; halving duration and additionality raises this to$160,000. These are arithmetic screens, not estimates for any organization.

For prevention: gift cost / (additional adverse outcomes prevented × finite health gained per outcome). For systems work: gift cost / (affected population × incremental outcome probability × health effect × counterfactual contribution), with implementation risk and harms explicit. Use no assumed policy victory as certainty. A pathway that requires every factor at its optimistic edge is not a leading shot.

##100 →25 →10 selection record

Each candidate must have a unique ID and a real-organization verification field. Record source lead, mechanism, geography, ordinary-gift scope, plausible advantage, principal failure mode and duplicate/sponsor relationship. Hypotheses without an implementer can inspire candidates but cannot occupy the final ten.

Screen qualitatively on: plausible health-per-dollar advantage; causal evidence and transfer; available marginal delivery mechanism; Bay reach; tractability of a useful15-minute first report; and information value relative to already researched organizations. Do not invent calibrated probabilities or hide value judgments inside a summed score.
