## Decision first

**After the Rule 6-5 scale correction, prioritize Alameda Health Consortium over CBE for scarce full research.** AHC has the strongest observed service denominator. CBE remains an enforceable, high-variance implementation comparison, but this particular mortality pathway does not support a favorable whole-gift central or optimistic estimate. Neither is a verified favorable gift. **PSE Healthy Energy is the strongest excluded alternative to retain**, because technical targeting can change the health yield of public spending, but its generic population/risk arithmetic below is unanchored and cannot now be used to outrank CBE numerically. **Justice in Aging and Silicon Valley Bicycle Coalition remain real upside hypotheses, not central-case top-10 picks.** NICJR remains worthy of a causal check, but a whole-national-organization cost boundary makes its Bay-only central case considerably less attractive than quoting Oakland's homicide decline.

This ordering is a research judgment, not a claim that only measurable services work. Large system benefits remain possible. Missing attribution is explicitly modeled below—not equated to zero. Conversely, an optimistic world is not evidence that the required scale actually exists.

## What a gift would have to change

### Justice in Aging

**Transmission:** expert technical assistance / administrative challenge → correct an erroneous enrollment, care denial, or implementation rule → an eligible Bay older adult actually obtains additional care → finite utility or survival improvement. The ordinary gift supports the entire national portfolio; health advocacy alone reported $4,901,804 and litigation $661,649, but those are not the cost denominator.

The current [California page](https://justiceinaging.org/issues/california/) identifies a dedicated Medicare/Medicaid and long-term-services team and 2026 implementation resources. The [DHCS buy-in program](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medicare-premium-payment-buy-in-program/) confirms automatic Part A buy-in began January 1, 2025. [DHCS county instructions, 2024](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal/eligibility/letters/Documents/ACWDL-24-20.pdf) establish the publicly administered mechanism. This means a new gift cannot claim all people newly eligible under the old reform. Residual implementation errors, missed take-up, appeals, and protection against actual service loss are the relevant margin. These are credible mechanisms, but no current Bay error cohort was located.

The [litigation inventory](https://justiceinaging.org/our-work/litigation/) is important disconfirmation of careless geography: Marsters concerns **Massachusetts**, not California. National SSA litigation may benefit Bay residents but requires both a Bay share and incremental legal contribution. State-funded legal assistance is also a baseline: the [toolkit page](https://justiceinaging.org/category/toolkit/) explicitly names California Department of Aging support. Additional lawyer work must be beyond that funded service. Public care, county eligibility workers, local legal aid and pro bono partners are complementary resources, not free donor-created health.

Decisive question: how many Bay people face one remediable current administrative failure, for how long before alternative correction, and what extra JIA capacity changes that delay? A credible cohort can rescue the economics; merely assigning a national population share cannot.

### Silicon Valley Bicycle Coalition

**Transmission:** technical design / implementation engagement → a safer corridor is built sooner or with better protection than otherwise → fewer serious injuries and/or sustained additional cycling → finite health benefit net of crash exposure. No electoral persuasion modeled.

The original return separates $826,683 regional event work, $350,419 advocacy, $141,675 education; 354 students and 24 classes are outputs, not prevented injuries. It reports 160,000 regional challenge miles, which are not necessarily incremental miles. The [2025 highlights](https://bikesiliconvalley.org/news/2023/11/svbcs-year-in-review-2023-zh8j2) credit collective advocacy for roughly 14 miles of lanes, but [Caltrans October 6, 2025 completion notice](https://dot.ca.gov/caltrans-near-me/district-4/d4-news/2025-10-06-sr82-safety-features) establishes a **$55M completed project and 13.5 miles of Class II/IV lanes**. Do not call all of those protected Class IV, or credit their completed benefits to a new gift.

The [VTA 2025 implementation update](https://www.vta.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/Bike%20superhighway%20Implementation%20Plan%20Update_2025.pdf) is a useful next corridor-stage source. Need an actual unfunded design, protection upgrade, or construction acceleration—not another plan adoption count. Public capital and road maintenance remain in the resource ledger. Physical-activity gains require retained mode change; school injury results from infrastructure cannot simply be assigned to adult classes.

Decisive question: identify the next corridor, expected users/injury baseline, counterfactual construction date/design, and SVBC's incremental influence. Unknown capacity is a prior, not rejection, but the existing celebrated project is an unusually strong funded-baseline caveat.

### PSE Healthy Energy: strongest excluded alternative

**Transmission:** missing neighborhood exposure data → a real agency/implementer changes control or mitigation targeting → actual dose falls → finite health gains. The January 2026 [Contra Costa factsheet](https://www.psehealthyenergy.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Factsheet-English.pdf) documents 50 deployed sensors, combined with 700 privately owned sensors, covering September 2023–May 2025. It identifies local exposure gaps and stresses that only regulatory-grade monitors establish violations. The factsheet recommends feasible interventions; it does **not** establish purchased filters, pollution controls, or a measured dose reduction. The [refinery map](https://rrm.psehealthyenergy.org/) is a tool, not additional deaths already prevented.

The strongest next lever is a named public allocation/design decision for which PSE's analysis changes targeting or timing; ordinary money could retain research/translation capacity. PSE's [Spring 2024 account](https://www.psehealthyenergy.org/pse-quarterly-newsletter-spring-2024/) explicitly collaborates with CBE/APEN. These are separate recipients but overlapping causal chains: do not award each all downstream benefit. CBE has the nearer enforcement pathway; PSE may win if a funded implementer is constrained specifically by missing technical analysis. Public monitors, industrial controls, household equipment, operation and maintenance must be costed or identified as already-funded counterfactual resources. Current Bay allocation remains unknown.

## Finite signed central comparison—not just kill gates

These are **subjective screening central scenarios**, not fitted estimates, probability-weighted expectations, or recommended published prices. Every population, risk change, utility, duration, contribution and marginal-response coefficient below is a judgment except AHC's observed 16,662 enrollment/renewal transactions, the corrected CBE historical modeled mortality-reduction range, and the finance anchors. Population means a hypothesized Bay cohort in the named pathway, not a measured organization reach. There is no extra Bay-share multiplier after an already Bay-only population; whole costs retain all non-Bay work. Unquantified programs are omitted benefits, not claims of zero value.

Convention: estimate the finite net Bay benefit associated with one annual whole-cost unit; an ordinary $100k gift scales by $100k/whole expense. This **linear marginal response is itself an uncertain assumption**, represented explicitly by m. Contribution a concerns organization-versus-partners causation; m concerns whether new unrestricted money expands/retains useful work instead of replacing other money/reserves. They are different, but could be dependent. Do not add another vague attribution discount. Costs below are nonprofit whole-cost diagnostics, **not total social cost including induced public/private complements**.

Nevertheless, the numerator must pay the whole portfolio cost. These priors are intentionally inspectable and replaceable, not tuned to make a threshold.

## Evidence that could actually change the ordering

1. **CBE:** quantify one still-open control delay and the fraction of the modeled reduction still unrealized. The executed refinery settlement and November 2025 Air District modeling in the prior memo support enforceability, not the previous generic mortality assumptions. Do not claim historical rule adoption again. Check incremental industrial/public costs and acceleration versus eventual compliance. This pathway is now lower priority than AHC, not a supported $157k/10Q optimistic opportunity.
2. **AHC:** deduplicated successful coverage-years and actual incremental assistance after the RECESS award ended June 2025. Its 7,323 enrollments + 9,339 renewals are not unique people or retained coverage. Training and integrated behavioral-care benefits remain unquantified and potentially material; do not double-count clinic outcomes. Insurance's mixed [Oregon randomized evidence](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1212321) does not validate the chosen .02 utility.
3. **PSE versus CBE:** identify a named funded implementer who uses technical analysis to change a decision. A data gap alone is weaker than an enforceable deadline; a uniquely decisive technical bottleneck could reverse that view.
4. **NICJR:** Oakland-specific staffing/grant share and residual change beyond CAPS, city DVP, Ceasefire, and the already announced housing pilot. CAPS city trends are uncontrolled; the original Oakland evaluation transfers only partially to current TA. Federal CVIPI cancellation is real in its return, but Bay loss is not established.
5. **JIA / SVBC:** current issue/corridor with an implementation counterfactual. Do not substitute old statutory wins or completed lanes. Their upside warrants retaining hypotheses; their central inputs are not sufficiently anchored for a literal ranking against corrected CBE or AHC.

## Material correction: Rule 6-5 mortality scale

Correction interval began September 11, 2026 at 14:51:11 UTC. Root's visually checked `work/market-for-impact-110-discovery/docs/110-discovery-air-scale-check.md` identifies [March 2021 Air District staff report](https://www.baaqmd.gov/~/media/dotgov/files/rules/reg-6-rule-5-particulate-emissions-from-refinery-fluidized-catalytic-cracking-units/2020-amendment/documents/20210330_sr_0605-pdf.pdf?la=en), Tables 11–12, printed pages 39–40: Chevron baseline 5.1–11.6 deaths/year with 23% reduction; Martinez 2.8–6.3 with 50% reduction. Therefore combined Scenario B reduction is **2.573–5.818 deaths/year**, not baseline burden. I read the root evidence memo; I did not independently repeat its visual table inspection.

The former 20/80 deaths-per-year CBE assumptions exceeded this actual intervention scale and are withdrawn, along with $4.379M central/$157k optimistic outputs and the resulting preference for CBE. Corrected central uses the range midpoint 4.1955; optimistic uses 5.818. Residual fractions .5/.75 are explicitly unsupported judgments for how much of the historical reduction remains available to accelerate beyond current funded compliance. Contribution and marginal-response factors are retained separately; finite 1/2-year acceleration avoids crediting permanent benefits after counterfactual compliance. The unchanged 2/10-Q harm stress assumptions produce .09775/17.9264 net Q, respectively. The central nearly cancels and should be read as approximately null, not as a precise $806M estimate. Zero-harm central gross benefit is only 2.09775Q, also unfavorable at $37.58M/10Q. Neither scenario clears $1M/10Q.

This correction does not prove all CBE work low value: morbidity and independent Bay pathways remain unquantified. It does mean that those pathways require actual evidence before rescuing the selection. PSE's generic mortality priors also do not establish an alternative supported headline; if using the same refinery intervention, it must inherit the same achievable reduction ceiling and share attribution with CBE and others. Revised evidence-informed order: AHC first; CBE a bounded implementation research question; JIA/SVBC/PSE unresolved scale hypotheses; NICJR weak on the present whole-cost Bay-only pathway. No unsupported alternative is promoted merely because CBE was corrected.
