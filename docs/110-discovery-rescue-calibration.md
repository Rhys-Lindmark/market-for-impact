## Decision

Micah's low cost remains a credible research lead, but none of the three has established rescue-cohort throughput. NEED's larger cost requires substantially more incremental coverage. HOPE's attractive screen depends on an entirely assumed expense denominator as well as assumed unique coverage. Do not promote distribution units into the selected rescued-person cohorts used by HEPPAC or Face to Face. Doing so improves apparent productivity by roughly 56–91 times before other differences and would be a material category error.

The earlier Micah/NEED screen specified cost hurdles, not locked best-estimate rescue priors. Only HOPE had an explicit numerical community-coverage illustration. Thus this is a prospective common-prior diagnostic, not evidence that previous Micah/NEED estimates were incorrectly calculated.

## Numerical common-prior illustration

Hold Bay share at 100%, cash-to-additional-coverage response at 0.5, one active year, and unique at-risk people at 200. These are comparison assumptions, not measured capacities. The community family retains the HOPE planning inputs exactly: baseline hazard .070 versus supported .068, common subsequent hazard .07, horizon 10 years, utility .65, annual discount 3%. Integrated finite gain is .007611190493 Q per covered person before funding response. Annual Q = people × .007611190493 × .5. Price = 10 × full expense / annual Q. These are central illustrations without a scenario distribution; not expected-value rankings.

| Candidate | Full annual expense | Price at 200 unique people | Unique people needed for <$1M/10Q | For <$100k/10Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

Thresholds round upward. Halving either incremental hazard reduction or funding response approximately doubles the required people (hazard nonlinearity makes the first relation approximate). Funding null yields zero; harmful response yields signed negative benefit, not an attractive positive price. The .5 response is a generic illustrative replacement, not added on top of each accepted model's existing additionality discounts.

## Why direct rescue-prior transfer is invalid

Unchanged central biological trajectories extracted from accepted models give .428826289280 Q per synthetic rescued-person equivalent for F2F and .691034692454 for HEPPAC v2, before their organization-specific credit/funding/overlap factors. F2F uses 2 opportunities × .15 fatality, other hazard .20, post hazard .25, horizon 5, utility .7, continuous discount .03. Their cohort definitions select hypothetical missing-rescue opportunities; they are NOT generic distribution recipients.

For diagnostic clarity only, replacing community Q/person with these trajectories while retaining the same .5 response would reduce the required unique people at the $1M threshold to:

Multiply these unrounded figures by ten for the $100k threshold. The last two columns are deliberately **not candidate estimates**. This table does not reproduce their whole-gift estimates. Survival differences remain subjective, not proven population differences; matching them is appropriate sensitivity, not a reason to count every distributed dose as a rescue.

## Source-grounded scaling and counterfactual checks

- **Micah:** [original 2025 IRS filing](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/full_text/202621969349200007/IRS990EZ) reports $61,176 expense plus $4,900 netted fundraiser cost, 15,000+ doses distributed throughout Sonoma. [Schedule O](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/full_text/202621969349200007/IRS990ScheduleO) identifies only $815 harm-reduction supplies, substantial officer donated hours, no salaries, and additional sober-living/scholarship spending. This is not $815 whole program cost. Existing volunteers and likely contributed inventory are complements; neither is demonstrated to scale with an ordinary gift. Cash/savings $94,114 and CD $34,690 are not evidence of a gap. Fifteen thousand doses cannot establish 174 distinct people with the assumed incremental hazard benefit; refill/repeat doses, secondary distribution, reserve stock and alternative free supply all matter. It is nevertheless a materially stronger volume anchor than no annual count.
- **NEED:** [FY2025 original filing](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/full_text/202621309349300527/IRS990) gives $357,858 expense, $445,659 government revenue, two employees and 28 volunteers. Approximately 20,000 primary/secondary exchangers and one million syringes are neither annual unique people nor naloxone rescues. [Official donation page](https://www.berkeleyneed.org/donate/) says syringe donations are not currently needed. [Official service/history source](https://www.berkeleyneed.org/) documents existing naloxone services and HEPPAC collaboration. Government funding is not an automatic impact discount, but the next private gift must be distinguished from already financed operations and HEPPAC's same outcomes. No naloxone throughput or spare staffed capacity established.
- **HOPE:** [official locations](https://www.hope4change650.org/narcan-locations-information) and [homepage](https://www.hope4change650.org/) advertise differing cumulative quantities (3,000/6,000) and five sites, not annual unique coverage. [County agreement](https://sanmateocounty.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=5FEEA3C1-A530-4DBF-9526-717613ED8645&ID=14759669&M=F) funded up to $10,000 through June 30, 2026, with reimbursement and stock-maintenance duties; volunteer-only operation and hardware do not establish recurring capacity. No original whole-expense filing was available in the prior identity check. The $40k assumption must remain visibly uncertain. [DHCS free naloxone program](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/individuals/naloxone-distribution-project/) means a private dollar need not buy the inventory itself. Its plausible role is otherwise unavailable location/refill/transport access, not gross drug provision.

## Actionable next evidence, not outreach authorization

None presently establishes <$100k as a best estimate.
