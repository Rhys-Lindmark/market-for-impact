## Selection rule

For whole annual expense E, need more than E/100,000 additional Bay QALYs per budget-equivalent to beat $1M/10, and E/10,000 to beat $100k/10. Apply whole-org dilution, cash response, Bay residence and best existing alternative before health credit. Donated labor/devices and reimbursed clinical care remain resources. Example break-even units below are **assumptions for screening**, not observed effects or prices. No probability weights or threshold-tuned model.

Best first screens: **Easy Does It, ILRCSF, HERS**, followed by **Women's Cancer Resource Center**. These target restoration of existing function or removal of a specific treatment barrier; superiority remains unproven. Exact expenses and unique clinically relevant throughput should be the first deepening gate, not reason to assume zero benefit when unknown.

## Selection challenge against the strongest alternatives

Read root's `work/market-for-impact-110-discovery/docs/110-discovery-lessons.md` after drafting; its ordinary-gift and resource requirements are preserved here.

- **EDI versus ILRCSF:** EDI has the clearest acute essential-care backup and relatively concentrated service mission. ILRCSF has a specific repair program but broader whole-org dilution. EDI's dedicated tax baseline is the stronger contrary fact; expense and unserved episode-days could reverse this order. No donated-parts leverage without parts availability, repair labor, dispatch and transportation.
- **ILRCSF versus CIL:** Field repair/loaners may restore function sooner than CIL's explicitly non-emergency one-month response. The latter delay is stronger demand evidence but not automatically a larger gift effect. Compare full recipient-level time restored, not visits or advertised waiting times; include insurance repair obligations.
- **HERS versus Cancer CAREpoint:** HERS offers a more specific prescribed-product-to-function bridge; CAREpoint might unlock much larger treatment benefit but the probability of changing treatment is unmeasured. Favor HERS for first screen, not because products are inherently superior. Include fitter time, clinician prescription, replacement garments, insurance payments and patient adherence.
- **WCRC versus Cancer CAREpoint:** WCRC's clinician-submitted active-treatment eligibility and two-county restriction give a cleaner clinical/Bay denominator. CAREpoint's bridge-until-other-resources mechanism could establish a shorter but more credible counterfactual delay. Neither can count all cancer survival; broad portfolio cost and alternative aid remain decisive.
- **CSS versus the two other crisis centers:** CSS already has a closer follow-up protocol anchor, so it dominates generic hotline replication for information value. Current protocol verification is still required. Treat all three as one correlated evidence lane, not diversification.
- **PDI versus additional general dentistry:** More concentrated severe disease improves the clinical-denominator hypothesis, but anesthesia/whole-facility expense, reimbursement and non-Bay catchment can erase it. Existing dental reports warn against optimistic all-patient pain duration; PDI is reserve, not automatic selection.

No new full report selected. Recommended immediate deepening is a comparative finance/unique-repair-episode check of EDI and ILRCSF, then HERS clinical product mix. This can eliminate weak candidates before committing one of ten slots.
