# Bay Area Legal Aid — dated source ledger

Retrieved 2026-09-10. Primary sources are preferred; modeled values are explicitly marked as analyst priors.

1. **FY2024 Form 990 / audited reconciliation (primary filing).** https://pdf.guidestar.org/PDF_Images/2024/941/631/2024-941631316-202543219349317404-9.pdf — EIN 94-1631316; $31,426,545 revenue; $30,238,844 Form 990 expense; $25,381,980 program expense; $35,286,276 audited-statement expense including $5,047,432 donated services/facilities; $28,500,264 government grants; $1,043,220 other contributions; $14,574,749 net assets without donor restrictions; $8,017,936 cash/temporary investments and $2,193,874 publicly traded investments. Program ledgers: health/economic/consumer $11,660,776 and 7,828 individuals; housing $7,888,871, including $3,896,303 granted to eight legal-aid organizations, and 9,719 individuals; domestic violence $3,740,661 and 4,637 survivors/children; other vulnerable-population work $2,091,672. The three population counts cannot be summed as unique people. The filing does not say whether subgrantee clients are included in 9,719; the model costs the grants but adds no partner outputs/QALYs.
2. **BayLegal official site.** https://baylegal.org/ — active organization domain; the server returned 403 to this research environment on 2026-09-10, so no official payment page, donation recipient EIN, current campaign or marginal quote was verified. The FY2024 filing independently evidences receipt of non-government contributions and the organization's EIN, but is not a current payment route. No donation button should be displayed.
3. **Seron, Van Ryzin & Frankel, Law & Society Review 2001.** https://doi.org/10.2307/3185408 — randomized NYC housing-court representation study, 134 treatment and 134 control assignments; reported final judgments against 22% represented versus 51% unrepresented. Legal outcome, not health outcome; old and selected context.
4. **Greiner, Pattanayak & Hennessy, Massachusetts housing-court RCT.** https://legalaidresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/greiner-massachusetts-housing-court-2010.pdf — full representation (about 12.4 lawyer hours) versus limited help (about 1.7 hours); did not find significant evidence of major substantive benefit. Transfer differs by baseline service and court.
5. **Cassidy & Currie 2023 / NBER 2022.** https://www.nber.org/papers/w29836 — NYC Universal Access rollout found fewer possessory judgments, smaller monetary judgments, fewer warrants and evictions. Quasi-experimental legal outcomes; not BayLegal outcomes or a health effect.
6. **Collinson et al., Quarterly Journal of Economics / NBER.** https://economics.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2023-09/qjad042.pdf — judge-leniency IV in Chicago/NYC: eviction orders increased homelessness and hospital visits over two years. Supports a health mechanism but does not directly estimate QALYs from legal aid.
7. **Cassidy et al., AEA Papers and Proceedings 2025.** https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fpandp.20251006 — NYC child linkage study: possessory judgments associated via Universal Access instrument with moves, homelessness and mental-health claims. Short paper and policy/context transfer uncertainty remain.
8. **Oregon Health Insurance Experiment, NEJM 2013.** https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1212321 — Medicaid lottery increased use/detection, reduced positive depression screens by 9.15 percentage points, but showed no significant two-year measured physical-health improvement. BayLegal only helps some reported health/economic/consumer clients obtain coverage, so the model applies additional outcome-share gates.
9. **Iverson et al., J Clin Psychiatry 2021.** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34813687/ — 60-person RCT of a psychosocial IPV intervention versus enhanced usual care; improved empowerment/self-efficacy, but depression and IPV change did not significantly differ. This is counterevidence against converting survivor service counts automatically to health gains and is not a legal-services trial.
10. **Historical 2017–18 BayLegal annual report.** https://baylegal.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Bay-Area-Legal-Aid-Annual-Report-2017-2018.pdf — reported 21% of cases in San Francisco. It is used only to inform a wide current SF prior, not as current residence measurement.

## Model-only judgments

- Ordinary gift follows FY2024 total-expense shares; no designation or incremental service offer was verified.
- Funding additionality 2%–20%; delivery 60%–100%; within-path uniqueness 80%–95%; cross-path overlap adjustment 70%–90%.
- Positive material-outcome shares: health/economic/consumer 5%–25%; housing 8%–30%; domestic violence 6%–25%.
- Every finite QALY is computed as analyst utility change × duration: harm −0.001×1 year for all three paths; null zero; cautious health .02×1, housing .02×1, DV .03×1; central health .05×1, housing .03×2, DV .05×2; favorable health .10×2, housing .05×3, DV .10×3. No mortality or benefit beyond three years is credited. These packages are not estimates from the cited studies.
- The historical audit ratio $35.286m/$30.239m = 1.1669 is a pro-rata resource sensitivity only. Verified marginal gross resources and complete societal gross resources are unknown.
- Bay share is 100% for the modeled direct service populations because the filing identifies them as Bay Area individuals. Current SF share is unmeasured; 15%–25% scenarios are subjective and historically informed.
