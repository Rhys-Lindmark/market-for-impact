## Selection and result

California Walks is the strongest remaining bounded research candidate in the prior three-organization screen after CCA. The current `work/market-for-impact-end-overdose/lib/*research-index.ts` files and backlog contain neither California Walks nor Public Health Advocates. CalWalks has a more inspectable future implementation unit than PHA's current broad policy portfolio: donor-supported community assistance that could change physical pedestrian-safety delivery. Walk San Jose is part of CalWalks, not another recipient. This selection concerns research value, not a probability-weighted expected-value claim.

For a hypothetical **$100,000 unrestricted gift**, central judgments produce **0.1583 US, 0.04317 Bay and 0.001583 SF QALYs**, all assumed California residents. Donor prices are **$6.32 million, $23.2 million and $632 million per 10 QALYs**. The central total net resource numerator is $162,000, increasing US price to $10.24 million/10 QALYs.

The favorable joint case produces **34.32 US, 15.69 Bay and 0.6864 SF QALYs**. Donor prices are **$29,137 US, $63,737 Bay and $1.46 million SF/10 QALYs**. Its $1.748 million total net resource numerator raises these to **$509,315 US, $1.114 million Bay and $25.47 million SF**. Favorable donor results cross the proposed research threshold but total-resource results do not. The case is an uncalibrated conjunction of favorable judgments, not a confidence bound or established high expected value. No input was solved backward from a threshold.

## Prospective work and baseline

The [current CPBST page](https://calwalks.org/cpbst) identifies the SafeTREC partnership, OTS/NHTSA support, and implementation follow-up for up to three communities. The [consulting page](https://calwalks.org/consulting) says donors and foundations support no-cost assistance. Neither establishes an unmet queue or gift-ready offer. The old `/tamenu` URL returned 404. Current services support modeling prospective additional capacity; they do not justify credit for historic awards.

[CTC's ATP page](https://catc.ca.gov/programs/active-transportation-program) describes an oversubscribed funding program and publicly supported technical assistance. Existing funding, projects, consultants and other advocates are baseline. The page records March 19, 2026 adoption of 2027 guidelines. This model makes no claim that 2027 applications remain open in September. It concerns later implementation capacity and does not assign a new gift earlier submitted applications.

Half the gift buys two hypothetical **$25,000 all-in implementation-support packages**; the other half remains in the denominator with unquantified portfolio health. Packages include staff, engagement, analysis and shared operations, not just wages. Central 50% funding additionality and a 20% incremental chance of changing a distinct project's implementation yield **0.2 expected changed projects**. These are analyst priors. The change probability already conditions on other advocates and public agencies; do not add a second coalition-credit multiplier. A project cannot appear twice as two applications, meetings or advocates' independent outcomes.

Each changed project covers a nominal six crossings. With 80% operational realization, the gift produces 0.96 expected crossings brought forward. A competing project loses **1.5 health-equivalent crossings per changed decision**, or 0.30 in expectation. This is independently specified lost alternative health, not a fraction of successful target installation. Failed implementation therefore causes net harm. An alternative need not use refuge islands literally; the equivalents express its finite health value on the same scale.

## Matched physical mechanism and health bridge

[FHWA guidance](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/pedestrian-bicyclist/background-resources/safety-benefits-raised-medians-and-pedestrian) reports a 46% pedestrian-crash reduction at marked crossings with medians/refuges. Its 2013-modified historical page is an engineering scale anchor, not an experiment on California Walks. The [current countermeasure page](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/proven-safety-countermeasures/medians-and-pedestrian-refuge-islands-urban-and-suburban-areas) identifies suitable multilane urban/suburban conditions. The model applies an explicit 70% central transfer for site design and injury-severity mismatch. No claim is made that a crash reduction directly establishes the same fatality reduction or applies to all projects supported by CalWalks.

Central baseline rates are **0.01 fatality and 0.10 serious nonfatal injured persons per crossing-year**. They are prospective high-risk-site judgments, not extracted observed local counts. The favorable 20-crossing archetype uses 0.02 fatalities and 0.20 serious injuries per crossing-year; pessimistic rates are fivefold below central. Site selection, regression to the mean, changing traffic, exposure and severity composition could materially alter these priors. An actual evaluation should use matched-site or empirical Bayes methods rather than simply extrapolate a short cluster of selected crashes.

Only **three earlier operational years after a two-year delay** count centrally. The no-gift infrastructure catches up afterward. Annual events are discounted at midyear at 3%. Fatalities and serious nonfatal injuries are disjoint people, avoiding adding serious injuries to a fatal-and-serious aggregate.

A prevented fatal event receives finite survival: utility 0.8, 2% competing annual mortality, 30 years, producing **12.156 QALYs at event**. A prevented serious nonfatal event receives a 0.2 average disability decrement for ten years with the same competing mortality and discounting, producing **1.537 QALYs at event**. These are health judgments, not validated clinical estimates or statistical-life-dollar conversions. One tail per distinct person; no extra lifetime multiplier. Short-tail and mortality-only cases test reliance on persistent injury deficits. The physical acceleration window and the subsequent saved health years have different roles.

## Geography and harm

[Walk San Jose](https://calwalks.org/walk-san-jose) establishes concrete Bay work. It does not show where next-gift beneficiaries reside. Central target health uses 25% Bay and 1% SF; displaced health uses 20% Bay and 1% SF. Favorable shares are 40%/2% target and 20%/2% displaced. All are residence-allocation judgments. SF nests within Bay within California before subtraction; regional net results need not share the target proportions after differently located harms. Street location and visitor residence are not interchangeable.

The model retains zero funding additionality, no changed decision, a common target-and-alternative engineering null, equal-benefit displacement, failed target implementation, no SF benefit, and positive-US/negative-SF cases. The common engineering null removes both projects' effect; target-only implementation failure separately retains displaced-alternative harm. An independent 0.01-QALY donor harm diagnostic survives zero funded additionality. These are stresses, not estimated harm frequencies. Walking-activity, climate, other injuries, community programs and other portfolio health remain unquantified. Cross-charity aggregation must deduplicate crossings and people with Walk SF, SPUR or other coalition partners.

## Real public and private resources

Per changed project, central hypothetical public real-resource PV is **$600,000 target versus $300,000 alternative**. Expected public net cost is therefore $60,000. Private real-resource PV is **$20,000 versus $10,000**, giving $2,000 net. The $100,000 gift plus these differences is $162,000; the separate **$224,000 gross target envelope** does not subtract the alternative. Grants themselves are transfers and excluded. Assets paid publicly are excluded from private costs.

The priors represent gift-date incremental present values relative to each project's own no-gift schedule, including capital timing, design/construction and maintenance. They are not asserted face-value project awards or independently priced procurement budgets. When construction is only earlier, its full asset cost is not automatically all additional; project-specific timing and downstream maintenance/savings must determine the PV difference. Resource assumptions are the weak point, openly labeled rather than hidden behind donor leverage.

Favorable projects use public $2 million versus $1 million and private $50,000 versus $20,000. At 1.6 expected changed projects these yield $1.6 million public net, $48,000 private net, and **$1.748 million net including gift**, against a $3.38 million gross target envelope. Actual resources can dominate nonprofit cash. No monetized health is subtracted from the numerator. Specific alternative health is already subtracted; a generic extra public-budget health penalty would require a distinct nonoverlapping opportunity cost.
