## Decision

The explicit central assumptions yield **13.109 net overseas QALYs per $100,000 unrestricted gift**, **$76,286 donor / $101,944 gross resources per 10 QALYs**. The corrected whole-resource central estimate narrowly exceeds $100k; it remains a potentially valuable research candidate, not a validated sub-threshold offer. Current room for funding is unknown. No assumptions were retuned after seeing the threshold. Fifteen joint/one-way diagnostics include rapid alternative treatment, low financial additionality, weak preference-utility bridge, zero and harm.

SF and Bay direct modeled health are both zero; neither has a finite positive cost/QALY ratio. Indirect local effects are unknown/uncredited rather than measured zero. San Jose administration does not create SF/Bay patient QALYs. Overseas health is not nested inside US/Bay/SF health and must not be merged into those totals.

## Outcome and evidence before arithmetic

Severe persistent urinary/fecal leakage is the health problem. Repair can close a fistula but anatomical success is not full continence or social recovery. The external [Nielsen follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19459865/) located 38 of 44 women at 14–28 months: the abstract reports 21 dry, 13 residual incontinence, three persistent fistula, totaling 37 classified urinary outcomes. We use 21/44 dry and 13/44 partial: all 10 remaining operated women (three persistent, six lost and one unclassified) receive zero durable health. This independent-audit correction increased total central above the threshold; there was no compensating retuning. The small uncontrolled cohort cannot isolate gift causality, eliminate survivor bias or measure preference-valued QALYs.

[Aynalem's survivor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35178255/) classified 84.1% of 182 respondents as having poor overall quality of life. That is neither a treatment effect nor proof repair does not help; it strongly cautions against equating closure with complete mental/social recovery. Therefore the utility gains .25 for dry and .08 for partially incontinent states are explicit priors, not copied questionnaire scores. Failed/unobserved states get no durable gain. Signed negative utility diagnostics remain possible.

The [Uganda economic model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6263022/) uses historical microcosts and lifetime DALYs versus no surgery. Infrastructure/indirect costs were excluded. Its favorable cost/DALY estimate is not our whole-organization cost/QALY estimate and supplies no hidden lifetime benefit here.

## Costs, allocation and complete boundary

The [2024 annual report](https://fistulafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/FF_Annual-Report_2024_PR5_24-Pg_Reader-Spreads_250708_Reference-1.pdf) reports 16,587 surgeries and $19,726,107 whole expenses. The [2024 Form 990](https://fistulafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/Fistula-Foundation-2024-Form-990-for-Public-Inspection.pdf), pp.1,2,10, corroborates $16,533,204 program + $1,490,119 management + $1,702,784 fundraising. These sum exactly. Thus historical whole cost is $1,189.251 per reported surgery. Grant accrual and surgery timing may differ; it is a calibration reference, not an incremental causal quote. A labeled 1.25 current/marginal uplift gives $1,486.564, not an estimated inflation statistic.

Every dollar remains in the numerator; no second program-spending fraction or overhead haircut. Training, outreach, fundraising and non-fistula cases are included in that historical cost. A .85 unique-episode factor discounts repeat counts without refunding their costs. The [current FAQ](https://fistulafoundation.org/about/faqs/) says 20% of cases are severe perineal tears. We use .8 fistula share provisionally, excluding tear efficacy while retaining cost and procedural harm. Its advertised $624 covers treatment-level care, not this whole-organization denominator. Provider reporting is quarterly; that does not establish independently observed marginal outcomes. EIN is 77-0547201.

Another $500 per nominal surgery is the central prior for unreimbursed public hospital infrastructure/staff, patient travel/time and family care; $0–$1,500 is explored. Apply it to all nominal procedures, including failed/repeat/non-fistula care, not only successes. It adds $33,634.61, producing a **gross** resource envelope $133,634.61. This is deliberately not net induced societal expenditure: no counterfactual cost savings or public-resource offsets have been measured. In the donor-pays-all case the extra $500 moves into complete cash cost, activity falls, and donor/total prices coincide at $101,944. No duplicate overhead or outside resource addition.

## Public baseline, finite health and harms

Existing government hospitals, NGO repair services, other donors and reserves continue without the gift. Funding additionality .5 (range .15–.85, explicit zero) removes replaced financing. Conditional on a genuinely additional offer now, later alternative treatment can still erase its health advantage. A five-year cap (range1–10, zero diagnostic) models this separate patient counterfactual. Five years is a labeled judgment about otherwise delayed care, not an observed waiting list or life expectancy. These factors must not both describe the same replacement event.

QALYs use expected mutually exclusive state utility × finite time. The stream starts after .5-year delay, survives mortality hazard .01/year, and decays .03/year after the long-term outcome anchor; all are priors. The central utility average is .1429545 across the entire operated anchor cohort. Annual discount is 3%, implemented with log(1.03); no double discount. The hard five-year advantage gives 4.140417 present-value health-years. There is no 40-year cure, maternal/newborn survival credit or income conversion. Decay represents subsequent recurrence/deterioration, not another initial surgical-failure multiplier.

Acute intervention losses .015 QALY per added unique care episode (including tears) and independent loss zero centrally are gift-date present values, not rates. They cover discomfort/complication or mortality harm outside durable-state utility; do not subtract the same worsening twice. Independent harm can remain when incremental activity is zero. The episode harm includes all repeat operations within that unique episode, not just one procedure. Charging full present surgical harm without deducting harms of delayed no-gift repair is conservative. These placeholders are genuine empirical gaps, not evidence the harms have those observed magnitudes.

## Decision-changing tests

At central duration/utility/cost, donor break-even requires funding additionality about .381; the gross-resource boundary requires about .510. Those thresholds are derived diagnostics, not fitted assumptions. One-year alternative-care catch-up yields only 2.668 QALYs and $500,923 total per 10 QALYs. Low utility (.1 dry, zero partial) yields $326,669 total; .15 additionality yields $339,813. The cautious joint case is $25.49m and favorable $8,229, not a confidence interval. A negative-health scenario never gets an attractive negative cost ratio.

The cheap next test is a dated marginal budget reconciling restricted/unrestricted funds, committed grants, genuinely unfunded distinct patients, procedure repeats and capacity over the next year. Obtain patient-level untreated waiting-time/alternative provider information, plus outcome follow-up including failures and a preference-valued health instrument. If most additional recipients would already receive equivalent care within a year, or financial additionality is below .510 at central clinical assumptions, the gross-resource central threshold fails. Request outside resource accounting to replace the $500 prior. The [2025 announcement](https://fistulafoundation.org/news/advances-womens-global-health/) reports 19,577 surgeries and $28m raised; growth supports current activity but raised funds are not matched expenses or proof of room.
