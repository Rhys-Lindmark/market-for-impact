## Household mechanism, incidence and overlap

The candidate imports the shared signed income bridge: $50,000 reference income and $100,000 healthy-year comparison value, using logarithmic total household income changes and own delay/discount schedule. This is welfare equivalence, not measured clinical QALYs. The $50,000 baseline is a reference, not the measured income of patients. Each added care pathway has a one-year net household change: after-tax/benefit-adjusted earnings plus unreimbursed care savings less fees, travel and treatment resources paid by the household. Components are combined before taking the logarithm. Zero-valued inputs have householdStatus unknown, and negative remaining income is rejected.

CPAP purchase replacement is a separate financing cohort. Someone who would buy the same machine without the gift may save cash but has zero incremental clinical treatment from this purchase; someone gaining otherwise unavailable treatment may gain health but must not also receive the full avoided-purchase benefit. The candidate bounds the nominal financing-replacement share plus clinical additionality at1. The illustrative replacement share .25 and $350 net saving are judgments after $500 avoided purchase less $100 fees and $50 travel, not observed prices. The replacement cohort has no added QALY. Government reimbursements and household fees are transfers in a societal ledger: count household incidence once, do not add the payment again as a real resource consumed. Organization donor cost is not reduced by patient savings.

Tobacco savings have no measured local quantity or welfare netting, so their reference is0/unknown. The +$1,500 and−$500 annual cases use extra initial quits and finite common-survivor abstinence exposure, attenuated by background quitting, relapse and mortality; they exclude wages/consumption of extra survivors. Net annual tobacco input must already subtract replacement products and any foregone consumption value the evaluator elects to include. It is not the gross cigarette bill treated as free welfare. To avoid summing logarithms for the same household, joint nonzero cessation acquisition cash plus tobacco savings are rejected pending an integrated household distribution. Across pathways, the reference assumes distinct recipient households; the nonoverlap share can reduce shared portfolio credit to0, while independent harms persist. A verified person/household crosswalk is required to replace that assumption.

Partial resource envelope retains the original nominal-offer complementary inputs ($100 cessation, $300 asthma, $400 CPAP), regardless of health success. They are allowances, not audited invoices. Household cash welfare is not itself a societal resource saving. Fees/transfers must be reconciled against these costs before claiming a net social-resource ICER; the candidate deliberately reports a gross partial resource ratio. No automatic positive donor welfare, staff-wage benefit, or baseline earnings of surviving people is counted.
