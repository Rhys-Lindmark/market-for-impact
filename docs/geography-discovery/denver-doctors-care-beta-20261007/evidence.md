# Doctors Care: beta source and decision checkpoint

Original FY2025 IRS return 202631609349300808 was retrieved successfully: $3,393,823 full expenses, 2,569 clinic clients, 7,920 visits, and a separate 898 coverage clients with 1,228 appointments. These client groups may overlap. The $2,790,718 integrated clinic/client program cost is not an allocated clinic-only cost. The other full-year expenses are $3,231,491 (2024) and $2,989,134 (2023), mean $3,204,816. Noncash contribution revenue is not an automatic additional expense.

Current original new-patient and sliding-scale pages describe newborn–64 eligibility, Medicaid, CHP+, Anthem, Cigna and uninsured patients. The fee table effective June 1, 2026 lists all-visit fees $20/$25/$55/$80/$135/$160 and medical-assistant fees $5/$10/$20/$30/$50/$60; the older example disagrees with the table. Patient mix, unique households, completed treatment and incremental care are not measured by these pages. The own get-involved page embeds https://www.coloradogives.org/forms/Doctorscare/?embed=embeddable_form; a bounded GET returned HTTP 200, without a transaction.

The original Oregon clinical trial author-hosted PDF https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/nejmsa1212321.pdf was read, including table 4: annual out-of-pocket spending fell $215.35 (95% CI $21.95–$408.75), depression prevalence fell 9.15 percentage points, but measured physical outcomes did not significantly improve over two years. SF-8 points are not QALYs. This is insurance coverage for low-income uninsured adults, not Doctors Care's mixed-age clinic versus alternative providers. The original labor paper's official NBER abstract https://www.nber.org/papers/w19547 reports no significant employment or earnings effect. Root independently read the full author paper. Neither study estimates Doctors Care's clinical utility or net earnings.

Decision: separate observed clinical patients from judged household grouping. Retain the transparent .03 utility/.4 response/one-year clinical hypothesis, not claim calibration by Oregon. Replace unsupported central $500 earnings with zero earnings and a separately judged $200 otherwise-paid medical spending avoidance, before net additional access fees/time. Positive and negative earnings remain sensitivities. Medical savings require incremental completed access but not symptom response; positive work gains additionally require clinical response and an adult/caregiver subgroup. All exposed household costs and patient harms survive failed benefit. Whole-recipient cost versus clinic-only benefits remains a conditional partial-channel reference, not whole portfolio or next-gift effectiveness.

The $200 transport, .3 resource success, .3 additionality, .8 household grouping, .95 Denver share and $75 net access cost remain judgments. The access cost is relative to insured/FQHC/other-provider alternatives, not all gross travel or copays. No extra provider opportunity cost is presumed absent: a $4 million full-social-cost and external-payer scenarios test unrecognized costs. Public costs already recognized in recipient spending are not counted a second time; unrecognized external incidence is separately tested.
