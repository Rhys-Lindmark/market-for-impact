# Friends of the Urban Forest: independent source challenge

1 October 2026. The author proposal is accepted for implementation and further arithmetic review, not yet as a published recalibration.

The original [Portland study](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/65639.pdf) was independently read. Its annual nonaccidental-mortality associations are 0.154, 0.262 and 0.306 fewer deaths per 100,000 tract residents per planted tree in the preceding 1–5, 6–10 and 11–15-year bands. Mean tract population is 4,318; the young-tree confidence interval includes zero. These are observational, ecological associations, not local causal effects. Counts describe planted rather than surviving trees, and tree deaths were unavailable: adding an independent establishment and annual-survival haircut would require a justified relative-cohort adjustment. Cardiovascular deaths must not be added to all nonaccidental deaths. The paper does not estimate remaining quality-adjusted life years.

The proposed ledger therefore correctly uses tract population, one nonaccidental-mortality pathway, a finite 15-year exposure window and no extra survival stack. Applying age-band associations prospectively to one incremental cohort remains a transport assumption. Five remaining quality-adjusted years per prevented death, 10% causal retention, 50% local transport and 50% net-new exposure are openly identified analyst judgments, not newly measured coefficients or calibrated probabilities. Their joint sensitivities must accompany the central estimate; the favorable case must not become a probability-weighted headline.

The workforce ledger must use ten Green Crew participants, not the eighteen participants across two different programs. A current six-month wage posting combined with an earlier annual participant count is a cross-vintage projection. Wages are labor compensation, not automatically additional household income: counterfactual earnings, taxes, benefits, work expenses and displacement need explicit treatment. The six-month net-resource flow is annualized only inside the logarithm, then credited for six months, not repeatedly for future years. No durable earnings effect is identified. Existing staff payroll is not a separate benefit. Tree mortality effects and temporary participant resources remain separate; no extra mental-health or financial-distress credit is stacked.

Whole organizational expense is retained in the donor allocation. Public grants, downstream city maintenance and already planned replacement complicate donor additionality; retrospective expense divided by tree output is not a marginal quote. Signed tree harms and incurred participant burdens must survive null-benefit cases when physical or applicant exposure remains. A zero funded implementation removes implementation-related harms but does not automatically remove incurred application costs.

Acceptance conditions: independent equation and boundary tests, explicit source-versus-judgment inputs, current report/API/ranking consistency, distinct historical calculations, closed research provenance and responsive report checks. No count increment before verified publication.
