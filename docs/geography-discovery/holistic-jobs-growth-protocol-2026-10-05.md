## Every initial and deep review

1. Identify native outcomes: completed care, actual unique trainees, sustained job access, additional homes or implemented policy—not visits, certifications, publicity or cumulative totals substituted for current beneficiaries.
2. Assess health and signed disposable-income/consumption effects together. Net resources exclude taxes, benefits withdrawn, childcare, commuting, training opportunity cost, displaced earnings and other costs. Salary levels, gross wages, job placement percentages and benefits obtained are not automatically causal income gains.
3. Establish an explicit alternative-provider/policy counterfactual. Distinguish treatment completion, incremental outcome, donor-funded capacity, geographic share and implementation probability. Do not apply several unexplained success multipliers to the same step.
4. For policy, separate enactment, implementation, behavioral response and downstream beneficiaries. Apply delay, finite duration, decay, displacement, harms and attribution. Do not multiply national population by a slogan. Keep national/CA/city nested benefits out of summed portfolio totals.
5. Use current `lib/income-health-equivalence.mjs`, inspired by [Coefficient Giving's comparison](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/cost-effectiveness/). Its published $50,000 reference income and $100,000 healthy-year valuation imply 0.5 × people × discounted years × log(after/before). Keep main $ per better life as ten equivalent healthy years. Income equivalents are not measured clinical QALYs/DALYs; expose both components.
6. Handle overlap explicitly. Do not add the same housing/security/mental-health welfare twice, retail service value plus resulting health, ordinary earnings of additional survivors, or earnings already counted elsewhere. Model net resources jointly within the same household; distinct pathways need disjoint people or a joint resource ledger. Positive overlap discounts do not remove unavoidable negative costs.
7. Keep a numerical conditional reference when defensible, labeling judgment inputs and the distinction from an empirically calibrated expected value. Missing controlled data are not evidence of zero effects; missing income must not automatically withdraw a valid conditional model. Include unknown, financial-only, clinical-null, zero-expansion and negative/adverse cases where relevant. Do not force a favorable price or a change.
8. Preserve the complete initial model and price as diagnostics. Explain what the deeper source checks actually changed—cost scope, clinical or economic coefficients, attribution, duration or uncertainty—and distinguish evidence-driven changes from judgment revisions.
