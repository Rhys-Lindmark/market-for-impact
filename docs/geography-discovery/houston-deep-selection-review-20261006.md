# Independent Houston deep-selection challenge

Decision: accept the ten-review phase with three material substitutions, not the proposed membership unchanged. This is a comparative research-priority judgment, not a claim that any ten have measured highest donation EV. Read all 25 current accepted Houston initial summaries, scopes, monitoring and funding passages in data/geography-reports.json; compared the accepted 100-to-25 discovery/selection metadata and independent acceptance. No new organization research, numerical recalibration, publication or per-organization research credit.

## Material substitutions

- Lone Star Legal Aid replaces TOMAGWA: matched FY 2024 cost and case/award reporting expose housing, safety and net-resource pathways worth testing, whereas TOMAGWA already has a source-checked narrow surgery illustration and existing dental grants without an established new-gift bottleneck.
- Santa Maria Hostel replaces Healthcare for the Homeless - Houston: ongoing maternal addiction treatment plus a concrete funding/capacity disruption can change both health and caregiver/work resources, whereas HHH's terminated funded dental project makes the current staffing/implementation bottleneck more specific and already investigated rather than a reason to repeat its narrow dental pass first.
- Daya replaces Christ Clinic: dated counseling, housing and economic-empowerment outputs and a reported local share allow a bounded safety/net-income comparison against AVDA, while Christ Clinic's current unique annual treatment cohort and donor-created capacity remain entirely unresolved in the skim.

These substitutions explicitly reject selection by the lowest initial price: Daya and Santa Maria have high conditional prices because unlike whole-recipient/partial-channel boundaries and unmeasured priors can conceal wider benefits. Missing evidence is unknown, not zero. This does not assert their whole portfolios are better; a deep review must test that possibility and can conclude otherwise.

## Recommended finite order and principal stop question

1. AVDA — org:avda. Refresh the historical household cohort and test safety, legal alternatives and full support-payer incidence before treating its attractive conditional price as real.
2. The Lighthouse of Houston — org:the-lighthouse-of-houston. Test measured training/low-vision outcomes, public workforce alternatives, full tuition/time burdens and consolidated donor capacity.
3. Santa Maria Hostel — org:santa-maria-hostel. Resolve the current operating plan and actual gift-sensitive completed treatment before translating recovery and net caregiver/work resources.
4. Lone Star Legal Aid — org:lone-star-legal-aid. Distinguish actual changed housing/safety outcomes from case volume and economic awards; trace payer incidence and Houston share.
5. Crisis Intervention of Houston — org:crisis-intervention-of-houston. Test unique distress improvements, 988 replacement and full volunteer costs; do not infer deaths prevented from calls.
6. Daya — org:daya. Deduplicate counseling/economic groups and compare culturally specific access against realistic alternatives; include safety harms and full burdens.
7. Meals on Wheels Montgomery County — org:meals-on-wheels-montgomery-county. Use the fresh unique recipient cohort and service months, then test causal nutrition/connection and actual net food/caregiver resources.
8. The Rose — org:the-rose. Navigation into treatment is a more tractable bridge than screening volume; test additional timeliness/stage, treatment costs and screening harms.
9. Air Alliance Houston — org:air-alliance-houston. Identify one implemented permit/exposure change with probability, coalition attribution, finite delay and signed compliance/job effects; sensors and stopped permits alone are insufficient.
10. Bayou City Waterkeeper — org:bayou-city-waterkeeper. Examine incremental implementation beyond existing consent-decree obligations and new funding opportunities, with finite local exposure/flood effects and payer burdens; do not credit the historical $2 billion decree as annual output.

Retained two policy candidates because a source-identifiable regulatory implementation could affect many people, not for a category quota. Conversely none has demonstrated such a causal effect yet. Fifteen-minute reviews should close with an explicit source-based estimate or bounded unknown, not serial polishing, forced improvement or expanded review budgets.

## Remaining candidates compared

Easter Seals is the first replacement: latest audited cost and a coherent child/caregiver mechanism are valuable, but historical ECI cohort, public entitlement substitution and whole multi-program costs presently make a donor-sensitive channel less identifiable than the selected candidates. Christ Clinic is next, with small operating cost and an access mechanism, but no verified current unique cohort. HHH and TOMAGWA remain legitimate broader clinical candidates, not low-EV conclusions; existing independently source-checked donor holds reduce the incremental value of repeating the dental analysis now.

HAWC and Fort Bend Women's Center retain plausible safety, counseling and earnings benefits, but current broad/overlapping outputs and capital/property or inventory boundaries make AVDA plus Daya the sharper first comparisons. West Street Recovery and Rebuilding Together retain repair and policy upside, but historical/cumulative output and city/partner baseline need a current completed cohort before inference; the Restoration Team also has a closed application queue, not proven marginal lack of funding. Brighter Bites has plausible diet/resource effects, but national/Houston cost-output mismatch, mixed household counts and funding disruption make the current research less decisive than Meals. Vecino, AccessHealth and San Jose retain general clinical upside, but insurance/charity substitution, repeated encounters or old full costs and unknown completed treatment make their marginal bridge weaker at present. Avenue 360 and Allies in Hope retain potentially important HIV/housing effects, but overlapping or undated cohorts and HOPWA/Ryan White/insurance baseline make a gift-sensitive incremental channel harder to identify in this finite pass.

All 25 were considered: the ten above plus Easter Seals, Christ Clinic, HHH, TOMAGWA, HAWC, Fort Bend, West Street, Rebuilding Together, Restoration Team, Brighter Bites, Vecino, AccessHealth, San Jose, Avenue 360 and Allies in Hope. The four features are not selected yet. Require independent review of each deep estimate and synchronize report/list/shortlist only after acceptance.
