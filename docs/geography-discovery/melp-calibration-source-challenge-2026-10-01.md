## Current delivery and the counterfactual

Independently reopened [AbleCloset instructions](https://ablecloset.com/how-it-works/). Pediatric equipment is free, generally a six-month loan, sometimes shorter, and intended for trial use or a wait for permanent equipment. Families may borrow several items and must arrange therapist safety and sizing checks. The permitted term does not establish the incremental duration of benefit; inventory items are not unique children. Extensions are possible but do not establish long-term developmental or caregiver effects.

The current [adult MELP FAQ](https://www.freemedequip.org/faq/) lists mobility/bathing devices and consumables, requires pickup, names alternative providers and supplies no universal numeric loan limit. Administrative hours and warehouse locations establish access routes, not residence shares or additional health. ## Clinical evidence and utility

The independently read [walking-aid randomized trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2841747) supports improvements in mobility and fear of falling after assessed/trained device provision. Its 90-day health-rating difference is −1.17 for walking aids versus control, with confidence interval −7.34 to 4.99. The 5.24 difference is telemonitoring plus aids versus aids, not versus control. Within-group improvement cannot substitute for a controlled comparison. The rating uses a 0–100 visual scale, not a preference-weighted utility tariff; division by 100 is not a QALY conversion. Neither this trial nor the local program identifies the generic utility coefficients in the old loan model.

The [COPD rollator trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17035442/) distinguishes acute walking assistance from home provision: the latter did not establish between-group quality-of-life improvement. That small, condition-specific trial does not refute all functional benefit, but it supports use/adherence and null-health stresses. The [BATH-OUT feasibility study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-018-6200-4) concerns installed bathing adaptations, not a portable chair. Its evidence cannot automatically transfer to a pediatric loan or provide an unadjusted general loan-closet utility effect.

A positive utility/duration bridge may remain an explicit judgment. It must not be presented as measured local effect, and the same shared adult clinical evidence should be treated consistently with the accepted ReCARES calibration. Differences in actual device mix, safe use, temporary need, alternative delivery, financial cost and marginal capacity can matter; lending status alone does not empirically justify a lower adult utility. Pediatric utility remains separately weakly identified. Independent clinical and geographic sensitivities are more defensible than arbitrary probability weights whose favorable tail determines a headline.

## Income, access and overlapping value

Health-access and counterfactual-purchase strata should be mutually exclusive within each deduplicated need episode. Someone unable to buy equipment may gain access, but does not thereby receive its full retail price in additional cash. Someone who would buy or rent equally useful equipment may save actual out-of-pocket resources without gaining its full access-related health effect. A loan bridging a delay should credit only the incremental interval before equivalent equipment or need resolution. Insurer/public-payer savings are not household income without demonstrated incidence.

Net savings must deduct actual substitute rental/purchase costs, transport, fitting, repairs, taxes or benefit offsets where relevant. Free equipment should not be labeled a cash transfer by default. Avoid recurring annual rental savings for an item borrowed briefly, repeated capital purchase savings after return, staff-payroll benefits, and unmeasured permanent wages/caregiver gains. The logarithmic income-health crosswalk is a normative comparison unit, not a clinical utility measure. Baseline household resources and recipient economic incidence are explicit assumptions if unmeasured.

Clinical utility may include financial distress or freedom to perform paid work. If such a broad proxy is retained, overlap with net-resource effects needs an explicit allocation or sensitivity. Otherwise the same economic mechanism can be credited in both ledgers. Actual health-function and independent cash savings for disjoint recipient strata avoid that automatic stacking. This distinction is not evidence that unmeasured channels have zero value.
