## What it does — three sentences

Operating NEMS delivers primary and specialty medicine, pharmacy, dental, behavioral and other care, with language access and coverage assistance. Its separate Foundation receives donations and supports clinical facilities and grants; the verified public giving route is not a restricted HBV navigation purchase. The conditional program examined here is repeated additional appropriate annual HBV monitoring for a closed diagnosed adult cohort, followed by clinically indicated care, rather than a hepatitis cure or a count of all NEMS patients.

## Three reasons to investigate

1. Navigation could help people complete care when language, scheduling, coverage and follow-up barriers remain despite available medical services.
2. NEMS already provides liver monitoring, ultrasound, fibrosis assessment, medication affordability assistance and clinical expertise, making implementation plausible while also strengthening the no-gift baseline.
3. The current campaign identifies actual philanthropic uses such as flu vaccination and mold mitigation, providing concrete alternative mechanisms to investigate rather than assuming every gift finances HBV.

## Three reservations

1. A Foundation gift's present allocation, Bay beneficiary share, additional HBV capacity and incremental clinical work remain unknown. Property support, technology and other clinical programs cannot be assigned the HBV coefficient.
2. The published lifetime HBV model has unresolved arithmetic/narrative inconsistencies and an unverified local causal calibration. A causal-transfer discount expresses judgment but does not repair the underlying evidence.
3. Twenty years of recurring capacity is a hypothetical commitment. Actual completed monitoring, appropriate subsequent treatment, finite timing, replacement funding, patient fees, travel, lost take-home pay and caregiving costs have not been measured for a marginal donor tranche.

## Signed household-resource assessment

There is no evidence here that NEMS navigation adds a fixed amount of actual household net income or consumption. The central cash ledger is therefore **unknown**, separately from the conditional numerical health component. Net resources can rise through lower out-of-pocket illness costs or saved take-home pay, or fall through new fees, travel, missed work and caregiver expenditure. Gross survivor earnings, GDP, provider payroll, reimbursed charges and the value of insurance are not household income benefits.

The executable packet allows a row-specific net resource change per additional monitoring person-year. Each mechanism must first be net of its associated costs. A positive row's independent share then removes overlap with the health pathway; negative rows remain fully counted regardless of that positive overlap factor. Only after that row-level crediting are dollars summed and passed once into the income-equivalence function. Thus a $100 net avoided-illness-cost hypothesis at 50% independent share, minus $10 travel and $20 lost take-home pay/caregiver burden, credits $20, not $35 or a separately transformed collection of nonlinear benefits. Broader consumption consequences are not appended without a distinct, nonoverlapping mechanism.

The existing shared comparison uses $50,000 annual baseline household resources and a $100,000 value per healthy-year equivalent. That baseline is a comparison prior, not the mean resources of NEMS patients. Cash flows occur annually only within the funded window, discounted annually; this prevents one care event buying lifelong earnings. The annual additional monitoring people are repeated recipients, so recurring cash consequences are flows rather than newly counted unique people. The diagnostic assumes the same recipient group faces the specified average net resource rows; actual heterogeneous recipient burdens require separate groups, not an average log treated as measured welfare. Cash welfare equivalents are not clinical QALYs.

Diagnostics include unknown cash, explicit zero cash, positive net cash, adverse cash (-$250), severe adverse cash (-$2,000), cash-only, complete null, replacement-associated harm, and negative effects with positive overlap set to zero. Dollar scenarios are decision hypotheses, not confidence intervals or observed household impacts. Negative health is subtracted after positive health overlap adjustment; negative cash is never discounted by a positive overlap factor. Financial distress severe enough to exhaust baseline resources is rejected rather than producing undefined logarithms.
