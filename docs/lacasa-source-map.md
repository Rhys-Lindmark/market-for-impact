# La Casa de las Madres — source map v1

Accessed 2026-09-10 unless stated otherwise. The model has no observed QALY or verified marginal-capacity input; every transfer from advocacy evidence into QALYs is labeled as an analyst prior.

## Organization identity, scope, outputs, and finances

- **Official 2025 annual report:** <https://www.lacasa.org/s/2025-Annual-Report.pdf>. Reports a broad umbrella of 1,384 Bay Area survivors of all ages receiving counseling, skill building, advocacy, and family-focused services; 885 survivors and their families using the Community Office/Drop-In Center; six co-locations serving 815 survivors and families; 6,151 crisis calls/texts; 2,226 police-response contacts; 231 people sheltered; 6,571 shelter service-days; and 5,230 prevention/outreach contacts. It reports $5,329,808 revenue/support and $4,525,216 expense, including $95,586 donated services/equipment. These groups overlap or use different units; the model uses 1,384 as the broad non-additive direct-service umbrella, retains 885 as a subset diagnostic, and adds no other pathway numerator.
- **IRS Form 990 mirror, EIN 94-2330864:** <https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/942330864>. FY2025 filing reports $5,062,993 revenue, $4,560,661 expense, $502,332 surplus, and $13,476,875 net assets. The model uses the larger IRS expense as the ordinary-gift denominator. Filed 2026-02-28 according to the filing index.
- **Official FY2024 audited financial statements:** <https://www.lacasa.org/s/La-Casa-Final-24-FS-1.pdf>. FY2024 expense was $4,761,718; program expense $4,121,299; government contracts/grants $3,239,538. Financial assets available for general expenditure within one year were $8,115,902 and policy called for at least 180 days of expense. Program allocations were residential $1,508,085, non-residential $2,445,185, and community education $168,029. The audited expense includes $98,957 of contributed legal services, while FY2024 Form 990 expense is $4,662,761—an exact $98,957 difference. This demonstrates that a GAAP in-kind amount cannot be assumed inside the Form 990 expense. For FY2025, the $95,586 reported in-kind input is not reconciled to the selected IRS denominator; it is not mechanically added, and the 1.10–1.50 gross sensitivities are intended to encompass it plus unpriced partner inputs.
- **Official current services:** <https://www.lacasa.org/>. Describes a 24/7 hotline/text line, emergency shelter, permanent supportive housing, counseling, advocacy, and prevention work.
- **Official promotional gift examples:** <https://www.lacasa.org/group-volunteering> and <https://www.lacasa.org/blog/2022/12/5/giving-the-gift-of-hope-amp-safety>. The organization says $100 can provide a night of safety and lists other illustrative amounts. These are not treated as restricted offers, incremental prices, or proof of capacity.

## Causal and health-transfer evidence

- **Sullivan & Bybee randomized trial, journal abstract:** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10028208/>. N=278 former shelter residents, randomized to a 10-week trained-advocate intervention or control; 95% retained across six interviews over two years. The authors report less violence, better quality of life, social support, and resource access; more than twice as many intervention participants experienced no violence throughout two years. This is an intensive post-shelter intervention, not an evaluation of La Casa's mixed portfolio.
- **Government-funded trial report:** <https://www.ncjrs.gov/pdffiles1/nij/grants/185728.pdf>. Gives the historical trial design, intervention and follow-up details. It is an author-submitted grant report and says publication does not imply DOJ endorsement.
- **Cochrane review of advocacy:** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9392211/>. Eleven randomized and two quasi-randomized studies. Intensive advocacy may improve short-term quality of life and reduce physical abuse one to two years after shelter recruitment; results were heterogeneous and often underpowered. Depression effects were compatible with no benefit. Two deaths reported across studies were not attributed to study participation.
- **Cochrane intensive-advocacy NNT:** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9392211/>. Very-low-quality evidence at 24 months includes OR 0.39 and NNT about 8 for cessation/reduction of physical abuse; the review reports no benefit at 12 months or three years. The central heuristic combines NNT 8 with the utility model below, not as a direct QALY observation.
- **IPV state-utility calibration, not an observed trial QALY:** <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6119435/>. A UK decision model used utility 0.63 for abused/unidentified women, 0.65 while seeing an advocate, and 0.85 when not abused. The central finite heuristic is `1/8 × (0.85−0.63) × 2 = 0.055 QALY`, rounded down to 0.05. It assumes the advocacy responder reaches the modeled not-abused state for two years and does not establish La Casa effects. The 0.15 favorable value is a deliberately wide 5%-weight tail allowing higher response and broader short-run mental-health/safety gains.
- **CTI randomized trial:** <https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00038-017-1067-1>. N=136 in nine Dutch shelters. At nine months PTSD symptoms improved, but primary quality of life and re-abuse/depression/distress outcomes were null. It is used as a reason for explicit evidence-null/downside mass, not as La Casa evidence.
