## Clinical and geography assessment

Positive pathways are mutually exclusive and deduplicated; consumables are separate. Harms are subtracted independently of safe-use benefit, including zero-benefit worlds. Pediatric benefit has no lifetime/developmental/caregiver bonus. Bay/SF shares are nested priors, not site-location facts. Foreign redirection is not quantified as a third of health: the packet's one-third concerns incoming items redirected for multiple reasons, not overseas beneficiaries.

The draft must make **effective years** mean incremental useful duration before need resolution, loan return, discontinuation or equivalent alternative equipment—not allowed loan length. `unmet` can be a fraction receiving no timely substitute over that effective period; explain this to avoid counting the same delay haircut twice. Adult MELP publishes no universal loan limit. AbleCloset usually permits six months, with extensions; favorable pediatric .75 year is therefore an extension/longer-use judgment, not the standard policy. Safe fitting requires more than inventory availability; volunteer OT training does not establish individualized clinical completion.

## Cross-organization consistency: material interpretive issue

MELP central adult mobility uses **.03 × .25 = .0075** QALY per otherwise-unmet safe-use episode; ReCARES uses **.05 × .5 = .025**, a 3.33-fold difference. Bathing is .005 versus .015 (3×); supplies .0001 versus .0004 (4×). The largely shared controlled walking-aid evidence does not empirically identify either set. Lending status alone does not establish shorter adult need or faster alternative access. ReCARES also may bridge temporary need and insurance delays.

The prior lock states lower MELP utility/duration partly reflects reused controlled disconfirmation. **New generic clinical evidence should be considered symmetrically**, not applied only to the later charity while leaving an earlier ranking implicitly more evidence-backed. Legitimate distinct factors include the more expensive contemporaneous cost base, funded maintenance baseline, forecast realization, device/population mix, location and demonstrable program-specific access differences. Those do not validate a lower utility coefficient for the same effective use.

Requested sensitivity, not retuning: evaluate both charities with a common explicit clinical parameter family for comparable adult mobility/bathing/supply groups, holding each organization's costs, scope, geographic/marginality inputs and own uncertainty structure fixed. Keep pediatric assumptions separate. Show the common low and common higher family in addition to original models; do not overwrite one model just to synchronize ranks.

A narrow diagnostic already executed read-only: replace **only MELP central** adult mobility/bathing/consumables utility and duration with ReCARES central values (.05/.5, .03/.5, .005/.08); keep pediatric, all MELP delivery inputs, every other scenario and all weights unchanged. Central Bay becomes **$572,533.20**, weighted Bay **$2,117,235.51**. This proves clinical-prior selection materially affects the central estimate, while the weighted result still does not establish a <$1M opportunity. It is not a harmonized forecast or justification for new coefficients. Preserve it as a sensitivity, not a recommendation.
