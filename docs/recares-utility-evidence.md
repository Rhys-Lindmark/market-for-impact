## Decision

The v2 mobility assumption **0.05 utility × 0.5 year = 0.025 QALY per successfully used, otherwise-unmet mobility device** remains an analyst judgment, not a measured ReCARES effect. Controlled studies support a possibility of functional benefit but do not validate this utility magnitude or six-month duration. Two directly relevant walking-aid trials include null quality-of-life findings. This is important disconfirmation, not proof of universal zero benefit. Do not retune scenario weights to preserve or defeat a threshold.

## Primary controlled evidence

### Walking aids prescribed in a geriatric emergency department

[Polesel et al., JAMA Network Open 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2841747): 75 Brazilian older adults, mean age 81.3, three equal groups; provision included physiotherapist assessment and training. At 90 days, walking-aid versus control life-space mobility difference was 12.77 (95% CI 1.06–24.54), and fear-of-falling difference −5.60 (−9.06 to −2.14). However, Table 2 reports the EQ-5D instrument's **0–100 health rating**, not a preference-weighted utility: adjusted between-group difference **−1.17 (−7.34 to 4.99), p=.70**. Significant within-group improvement is not the randomized treatment effect. Do not divide that rating by 100 to manufacture QALYs. Missing follow-up values were imputed for 3 walking-aid, 6 telemonitoring, and 7 control participants. Clinical selection/training, population, and 90-day follow-up differ from ReCARES' mixed donation service. Functional efficacy is plausible; 0.05 utility for six months remains unverified.

### Rollator provision after COPD rehabilitation

[Gupta et al., Chest 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17035442/): 31 post-rehabilitation COPD patients randomized to rollator (18) or usual care (13), eight weeks. Acute assisted walking distance improved, but home provision produced no significant between-group Chronic Respiratory Questionnaire effect (p>.08) or walking-capacity effect. **8/18 used the rollator less than three times weekly.** This is infrequent use, not a documented abandonment rate. No utility or QALY estimate is reported in the abstract. COPD-specific limited walking capacity and prior rehabilitation are not ReCARES' observed recipient mix. Nevertheless, device delivery alone cannot be assumed to create sustained health benefit; adherence and counterfactual access matter.

### Major bathing adaptation: feasibility randomized trial

[BATH-OUT, BMC Public Health 2018](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-018-6200-4): 60 older adults randomized to immediate adaptation or routine waiting. Table 6 reports a three-month EQ-5D-5L adjusted change difference **0.09 (SE .05; 95% CI −.003 to .18)**. Average adaptation cost was **£4,878.46**. This was an installed level-access shower, not a donated shower chair or transfer bench. The study was designed for feasibility, and controls subsequently received adaptations. It cannot establish a persistent six-month incremental effect or justify assigning this coefficient to ReCARES portable aids. It does show utility gains of this order are possible in a materially different, intensive intervention; its interval also includes zero.

### Larger bathing trial: primary funder findings, no numeric QALY coefficient retrieved

[NIHR BATH-OUT project findings](https://sscr.nihr.ac.uk/research/care-economics/bath-out/) report 296 randomized adults receiving expedited versus routine major bathing adaptations. First-follow-up SF-36 physical component difference was **2.5 points (95% CI .8–4.2)**. The funder reports significantly higher QALYs across the trial but supplies no numeric QALY difference on this page. Attrition was high and differential; later physical-component differences were not significant. Do not cite the protocol as results or map SF-36 points into QALYs. This corroborates a clinical mechanism for expedited substantial adaptations, not the scale or persistence of portable equipment effects.

## Implication for v2 and highest-value next evidence

Preserve explicit null and independent harm scenarios. Label all ReCARES utility and duration coefficients subjective, including supplies and bathing—not source-derived. No controlled wheelchair-repair or portable bathing-aid preference-utility coefficient was established in this bounded search; that is a search limitation, not a claim none exists. No new numerical mapping is recommended. The most valuable charity-specific bridge would be device-class counts plus prospective use, counterfactual acquisition delay, and baseline/follow-up preference-weighted health measurement with a credible comparison group. A small recipient follow-up can inform use and duration but alone will not identify causal utility.
