## Whole-cost anchor and current funding interpretation

The [FY2025 consolidated audit](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/19/2026/05/2025-KQED-FS.pdf), issued February 24, 2026, reports **$111.630 million total functional expenses** (printed p9): $111.188 million operating expenses plus $442,000 Studios Fund expenses. Do not use the rounded $111.2 million operating subtotal as whole cost. KQED and wholly owned Snap are consolidated with intercompany elimination. Depreciation/amortization is included, not free capital. Public and philanthropic complements outside these accounts would remain additional resources in any system-cost assessment.

Audit Note B reports $24.4 million operating reserves and $34.902 million available for general expenditures within one year. Board-designated endowment is not equivalent to spendable current cash. These dated balances neither prove no need nor establish today's marginal funding response.

The [July 2025 budget statement](https://www.kqed.org/about/18677/statement-about-2025-kqed-budget-reductions) describes 45 layoffs, 12 voluntary buyouts and about $13 million annualized expense reductions. Its prospective deficit is not interchangeable with audited FY2025 results. The funding shock is real evidence of pressure; it does not identify the program, additional output or health outcome purchased by the next unrestricted dollar.

## Outcome evidence and decisive disconfirmation

The [2025 annual report](https://cdn.kqed.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2025-KQED-Annual-Report.pdf) supplies accountability examples, not an estimated journalism-to-health effect. San Rafael's 45-household RV-park dispute remained litigation, not 45 observed evictions prevented. The 60-household park purchase was in Fresno County, outside the Bay, and relied on organizing and public/nonprofit finance. Neither can be credited wholesale to KQED.

The report also says foundations replaced the canceled education-project funding: this is contrary evidence to treating that canceled grant as a currently unfunded tranche. Audience is not an outcome: the HTML [community report](https://www.kqed.org/community-report) and PDF show different website audience figures, and channel reach is neither deduplicated nor Bay-resident health exposure. National distribution and education, Monterey and Sacramento-area broadcasting prevent an assumed 100% Bay allocation.

Cityside's independent screen `/private/tmp/mfi-gw-cityside-alpha-checkpoint.md` found no portable empirical journalism-to-health coefficient. Its Berkeley inspection reform has independent government implementation and cannot also be awarded to KQED. This is a shared calibration lesson, not a reason to presume journalism has zero value.

## Explicit finite model skeleton

For each nonoverlapping pathway: annual cohort population × incremental implemented outcome probability beyond existing media/public services × finite health gain per outcome × Bay share × KQED annual-cohort causal contribution × ordinary-gift funding response, less signed harms. Allow null implementation, displacement and adverse outcomes. Keep duration finite and avoid crediting one annual budget with the entire historical institution or indefinite future effects.

1. Service information: unique Bay people at relevant risk; additional verified protective behavior relative to other information; causal risk change; finite health duration. Page views cannot substitute for any of these.
2. Accountability: a concrete policy or service change actually implemented; affected population; residual health risk; timing; contribution relative to officials, litigants, organizers and other media. Passage or publicity alone is not health.
3. Education: additional retained behavior with a defensible health bridge. Civic knowledge and artistic value remain unquantified, not zero.

No values are filled for those unknowns. At the audited cost, beating $1 million per 10 Bay QALYs requires **more than 1,116.3 net Bay QALYs per annual whole-cost cohort**; beating $100,000 requires more than 11,163. These are break-even arithmetic, not estimated benefits, and omit unknown external resource costs. A marginal gift additionally requires an explicit funding-response model.

## Next information that would make a bounded model useful

- One current Bay health-relevant investigation or service-information product with unique affected population, implemented response and timing, plus an explicit counterfactual contribution judgment grounded in the decision record.
- The current ordinary-gift allocation and replacement-funding response: preserve which output, or expand what output, rather than assuming every dollar restores the eliminated federal grant.
- A primary causal bridge for the specific behavior or implemented service change, with overlap, finite duration, null and harm cases. A generic audience-to-QALY coefficient would not resolve the gap.
