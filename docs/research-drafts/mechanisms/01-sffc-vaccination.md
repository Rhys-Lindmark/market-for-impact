# SF Free Clinic: targeted seasonal influenza vaccination

Research date: 2026-09-07. This is an exploratory acute-illness-only model, not a verified funding offer or a comprehensive ranking of vaccines.

## Actual program and marginal unit

https://sffc.org/primary-care confirms uninsured-only primary care, including vaccination, at 4900 California St, SF. https://sffc.org/donate states no insurance payments or government funds; donations support care alongside volunteer clinicians and donated equipment/supplies. This is the operating clinic's own statement, not proof that every vaccine has been privately purchased. https://www.sfccc.org/san-francisco-free-clinic/ lists 1,642 clients/year, undated and organization-wide: DO NOT divide organization spending by that number.

Specific flu delivery is supported by SFDPH's dated 2020 list https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2020-11/FluShot-Eng-110420.pdf and GLIDE's current referral page https://www.glide.org/healthservices/ . Current SFFC page does not publish vaccine inventory, dose count, ages or stock price. Thus current influenza stock and any particular high-risk age group need confirmation. Shingles might have a stronger long-horizon benefit but actual SFFC Shingrix delivery was NOT verified; do not model it as established.

Candidate tranche: additional completed seasonal inactivated influenza doses to uninsured adults 18–64 who would remain unvaccinated elsewhere. Not a referral, appointment or all clinic clients. A one-season intervention requires repeat annual cost for repeat protection.

## Primary causal and utility anchors

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19909082/ (2009 RCT): 5,103 TIV and 2,549 placebo adults aged 18–64, Czech Republic/Finland, September 2006–May 2007. Placebo culture-confirmed influenza attack rate 3.2%; efficacy against any influenza strain 61.6%, 95% CI 46.0–72.8. External season and relatively healthy adults, not a 2026 SF effect. https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0808652 (2009 randomized 1,952 adults, 2007–08 season) found 68% efficacy, CI46–81, further showing meaningful but season-dependent prevention. These are relative efficacy estimates, not percentage-point reduction.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3047534/?pdf=1 (2011 prospective English 2009H1N1 study, full relevant text read via curl): measured EQ-5D mean loss .008 QALY/confirmed case (2.92 quality-adjusted days). Only114/186 confirmed respondents had complete information for total loss; selection and pandemic-to-seasonal transfer matter. This is measured illness burden, not a randomized vaccine utility effect.

Historical acquisition anchor https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-for-children/media/pdfs/2026/03/Vaccine-Price-List-01-26-v2-508.pdf: Jan2026 Fluzone single-dose syringe private-sector20.878/dose, CDC14.34. Contract endedFeb2026, NOT current quote. https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines-for-children/php/price-list/index.html describes current2026–27 pricing and explicitly says private providers cannot purchase directly under CDC contracts. Administration, recruitment, cold chain/waste and volunteer resource costs remain additional.

## Explicit model and calculations

Q/dose = seasonal symptomatic influenza risk × relative vaccine efficacy × QALY loss/case × counterfactual additionality − vaccine harmQ.

Favorable cost below historical private vaccine price requires donated/subsidized stock. It is a donor perspective, not free resources. Total-resource version must add donated doses and volunteer time. No monetized productivity savings; no speculative mortality, hospitalization or household-transmission benefit included. Hence this is a deliberately narrow acute-illness model, not evidence vaccination is clinically unimportant. A truly high-risk population could change results substantially but needs verified local severe-outcome risk and vaccine effect.

Null: vaccine mismatch/no incremental vaccination; Q=0 and no finite ratio. Harm: vaccine burden exceeds incremental prevented illness, Q<0; report as harm, not a negative attractive price.

## Decision

This tested narrow mechanism fails the <$100K/10Q threshold even favorably. At central assumptions a $40 dose requires >.004 Q/dose to pass, versus modeled.00006. Decisive next data: current vaccine/dose inventory, genuinely unvaccinated high-risk pool, donated versus purchased stock, incremental staffing/visit cost, alternative free vaccine access, and specific capacity a restricted gift can expand. Do not label an undifferentiated vaccination gift a proven bargain.
