# SFCCC SOS: sustained treatment of clinically eligible venous ulcers

Research handoff 2026-09-07. Actual wound care verified; the narrower protocol proposed here is NOT verified as an existing distinct SOS service. It is a conditional, measurable expansion hypothesis, not generic outreach effectiveness.

## Local program, entity and additionality

[Current SOS page](https://www.sfccc.org/street-outreach): medical van, doctors/nurses/outreach workers; on-site urgent/preventive services and separate referrals; over 1,000 people annually is NOT a wound-treatment denominator. [Provider-issued December 3, 2024 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sfcccs-street-outreach-services-transforming-lives-this-holiday-season-302321453.html) describes actual medication management and wound treatment for a client with swollen painful legs. That anecdote neither diagnoses venous ulcers nor demonstrates causal health gain.

Thus determine whether donor funds actually add clinical time/treatment beyond existing grants and billable care. The 2025 $35,000 National Health Corps education-award gap on donation page is NOT a wound-treatment funding offer. SFCCC-hosted [2026 alumni article](https://www.sfccc.org/blog/2026/2/12/nhc-sf-member-blog-alumni-spotlight-meet-vivian-yip-nhc-sf-2022-2023-alumna) describes DPH Shelter Health wound care, not SOS-only output; do not conflate those teams or count shared patients twice.

## Clinical hypothesis and evidence

Screen wound etiology and arterial circulation, then deliver clinically appropriate compression/dressings with scheduled follow-up to adults with venous leg ulcers who otherwise receive inconsistent care. Do NOT transfer venous-ulcer benefits to abscesses, diabetic ischemic feet or xylazine-related wounds. Compression can harm arterial insufficiency; clinical eligibility is essential.

[Morrell et al. BMJ May 16, 1998 primary randomized trial, inspected author PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/1568/1/dixons2.pdf): 233 patients, 120 weekly specialist-clinic four-layer compression versus 113 usual district-nurse home care. Over one year, mean ulcer-free weeks 20.1 vs14.2: incremental 5.9 (95% CI1.2–10.5), incorporating recurrences. SF36/EuroQol did NOT improve between groups; some pain measures did. Historical annual treatment cost £804.03 versus £681.04; total NHS cost £877.60 versus £863.09, not SF prices. Cannot isolate compression from trained nurses/setting. Does not compare outreach with no care.

[Wong 2023 primary prospective Singapore study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hsr2.1488): 28 analyzed, 17 lost (37.8%); healed utility .870 vs unhealed .812 (difference .058, p=.39). Observational health-state contrast, NOT causal utility gain from healing. [Cheng 2019 primary validation abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30778889/) analyzes 80 Australian patients; .89/.73 healed/unhealed values are reported secondarily by Wong and NICE, not full-read in Cheng during this handoff. Treat .16 as favorable model orientation pending primary-table verification, not established treatment gain.

[Harrison 2008 primary randomized health-services trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19036149/): clinic versus home delivery with the SAME evidence-based nursing care yields equivalent healing/HRQoL. Counterweight: location/outreach alone need not improve outcomes when usual care already supplies adequate treatment.

## Cost unit and explicit exploratory arithmetic

Unit: one eligible person's additional course of longitudinal wound care, with up to one year of observed ulcer-free time. No lifetime extrapolation, mortality, infection admissions or referrals added. Model Q = incremental ulcer-free weeks / 52 × causal utility difference × clinical-transfer factor × funding additionality − net incremental harm.

[SF official RN classification July 1, 2026](https://careers.sf.gov/classifications/?classCode=2320) gives $84.525–$113.125/hour public-sector base pay. This is a LOCAL LABOR PROXY, not SOS salary/quote. Central course cost $1,500 is JUDGMENT: 12 contacts × ($100 RN/hour × .5 hour ×1.3 benefits + $30 supplies)=$1,140, plus $360 allocated van, outreach, assessment/medical oversight/admin. Contacts/duration, salary point, burden and supplies are all assumptions; initial screening costs and failed-contact time must be included in a provider quote. Twelve contacts is a proposed minimum dose, not proof it matches a year of trial treatment; clinical-transfer penalty partly recognizes mismatch.

Favorable cost requires heavily shared or donated capacity and demonstrably adequate treatment, currently unverified. None passes $100K/10Q; favorable would need C below $181.73 at its health assumptions. Do not force it beneath threshold by treating volunteer labor as free in total-resource perspective.

Null: generic EQ5D improvement truly zero (consistent with trial), existing adequate care, no extra funded course or no clinically eligible patients. Harm: inappropriate compression, missed ischemia/infection, or replacement of superior care; retain signed Q and show no positive ratio when Q≤0. Positive scenarios set net harm=0 after assumed clinical safeguards, not evidence that complications never occur.
