# Child-health mechanism-first gap scan

Researched 2026-09-07; additional to NEMS Hep B Moms/Wu Yee and other agents' lead/asthma work. Two actionable mechanism leads plus one historical preschool provider lead. Do not inflate organization count by listing the same UCSF/coalition mechanism as multiple fully researched donors. All costs/QALY conversions are analyst priors except Vision To Learn's stated $150 donor bundle.

## 1. Vision To Learn — SF school exams, glasses and replacement/adherence

**Revised central $250,000 per10 incremental QALYs. This supersedes the earlier $79,365 central estimate following independent recheck on 2026-09-07.** Formula Q per completed clinically indicated glasses episode = a×w×u×T = .50 genuinely additional access ×.60 average wear/effective correction fraction ×.02 health utility gain while effectively corrected ×1 incremental year before counterfactual correction =.006 QALY. Cost c=$150; 10c/Q=$250,000. These are judgment assumptions, not newly measured SF inputs. Education/test-score benefit excluded from QALYs. The former central combination (a=.6,w=.7,u=.03,T=1.5; Q=.0189) is retained as a **favorable $79,365/10 scenario**, not the ranking estimate. Its simultaneous assumptions on additionality, wear, utility and duration were too favorable for the central case given their weak local support.

Optimistic c=$100,a=.9,w=.9,u=.08,T=3=>.1944 QALY,$5,144/10. Pessimistic c=$300,a=.2,w=.4,u=.01,T=.5=>.0004 QALY,$7.5m/10. Null if gift replaces funded visits, child receives timely glasses anyway or glasses are unused. Harm scenario subtract .02 QALY per revised central episode for missed pathology/delayed referral or other adverse effects, yielding -.014 net; not a finding of program harm. Referral-only examinations are not full specialist disease diagnosis. Break-even at c=$150 is >.015 QALY/episode; revised central .006 misses by a factor of2.5. At other revised central parameters need >2.5 incremental years before counterfactual correction, with any additional maintenance/replacement costs included.

Cost-denominator recheck: the organization's primary decade report https://visiontolearn.org/wp-content/uploads/VTL-A-Decade-of-Progress.pdf explicitly describes less than $150 per child covering screenings, examinations, providing and fitting glasses. The organization's 2022–23 annual report https://visiontolearn.org/wp-content/uploads/VTL_AnnualReport_2023_12_screen.pdf also reproduces a description of approximately $150 all-in per child provided glasses. This strengthens interpretation as a completed-glasses bundle rather than an arbitrary screened pupil. **Do not add a screening-yield multiplier to this stated bundle.** It remains an advertised average, not a verified current SF incremental delivery quote.

Utility/applicability recheck: no SF under13 preference-based utility estimate was verified. Tahhan et al.2013 studied adults aged40–65, including near-vision impairment/presbyopia, with specific acuity distributions: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23664469/ . Its utility deficits must not be imported as children's health gains. The revised .02 is an explicit age/acuity-sensitive judgment, not that study's estimate. Academic gains are not a health utility measure. Neither the Baltimore trial nor the preschool compliance study establishes incremental years until usual-care correction; reduced year2 academic effects do not establish disappearance of clinical benefit.

Primary local/donor evidence: SFUSD March8,2023 partnership notice https://www.sfusd.edu/employees/announcements/2023-03-08-free-vision-screening-eye-glasses-opportunity-vision-learn confirms free school service and capacity limits; current organization region page https://visiontolearn.org/where-we-work/northern-california/ identifies SFUSD and existing Golden State Community Foundation support. National homepage https://visiontolearn.org/ states $150 provides screening, exam and glasses and 501(c)(3) EIN45-3457853, with donation route. This is a credible actual donation recipient but **SF-only allocation/extra school queue not yet confirmed**. Existing regional grants, insurance, mandated school screenings, family optometry and in-kind glasses are counterfactual funding, not added benefits to credit twice.

Primary causal study: Neitzel et al.,2021, cluster randomized rollout in Baltimore, grades3–7 (2,304 analyzed students). Paper hosted by implementation partnership: https://visionforbaltimore.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/JAMA-2021-Report.pdf ; investigators' report https://hub.jhu.edu/2021/09/09/glasses-boost-student-performance-vision-for-baltimore/ . Reading improved at year1, broad gains not sustained at year2. Trial identifies school program→academic outcome, not health utility or lifetime eyesight. Wearing and replacement should be tracked directly. SFUSD screening policy describes opt-out mobile nondilated eye evaluations: https://www.sfusd.edu/zh-hant/node/7656 . A pediatric clinical assessment can be valuable without inferring lifetime blindness avoided for every corrected refractive error.

Public-resource caveat: the primary 2024–25 report https://visiontolearn.org/wp-content/uploads/VTL_AnnualReport_2025_20-ocr.pdf reports Medicaid/CHIP revenue, other public funding and in-kind contributions alongside private support. Consequently $150 must not be represented as an independently sufficient purchase of every resource. Nor should total annual funding divided by delivered glasses be called a marginal cost: funding, expenditure, growth investment and restricted grants differ. The reviewed estimate is conditional on donated/paid clinical capacity and SF allocation; public reimbursement may either complement an additional service or replace charitable need.

Marginal funding needed: SF clinic-day/full delivery cost, unfunded schools/children under13, proportion needing and receiving glasses, measured wear/loss/replacement at6/12months, existing reimbursements and mean delay in obtaining glasses without program. Worth prioritizing for concrete donor diligence because the mechanism is direct and local delivery verified, but **the revised central estimate does not cross the target**. An unrestricted national gift cannot inherit an SF-only score without an allocation assumption.

## 2. Preschool caries prevention/treatment access — CavityFree SF and UCSF Children's Night Clinic

**Central $500,000 per10 incremental QALYs** for an extra clinically indicated two-year prevention/access package c=$100. Formula Q=a×d×h=.5 additional treatment share ×.10 absolute probability reduction in consequential caries over two years ×.04 discounted health QALY per prevented consequential episode=.002. Cost per10=$500k. All parameters analyst priors; d is conservative relative to some trial contrasts but applies to modern incremental care, h includes pain/eating/sleep disruption, not an automatically counted lifetime tooth benefit. Hypothetical package, not a published clinic price.

Optimistic c=$40,a=.9,d=.20,h=.10=>.018 QALY,$22,222/10. Pessimistic c=$200,a=.2,d=.02,h=.01=>.00004 QALY,$50m/10. Null if already covered by Medi-Cal Dental/school varnish, no clinical effect atop existing prevention or children not reached. Harm if adverse effects/delayed treatment exceed .002 QALY/central package; no related adverse events in cited trial. At c=$100 target requires >.01 incremental QALY, or h>.20 with central a,d—five times central h. Treat an active painful cavity with real treatment access as a separate potentially better-yield mechanism; preventive varnish alone does not cure that unmet need.

Especially relevant primary trial: Weintraub et al.,J Dent Res February2006, 376 initially caries-free young children from low-income SF Chinese/Hispanic families, randomized caregiver counseling alone vs annual/semiannual varnish. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16434737/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2257982/ . Intention-to-treat caries protection; protocol deviation led to fewer active applications, and follow-up attrition limits precision. Oral disease outcomes, not QALYs. Modern counterfactual can be stronger: Athens preschool randomized trial (2014) found little additional varnish effect atop supervised fluoridated brushing https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25123352/ . Do not reuse old trial's untreated-control risk for already-served SF children.

Actual programs: https://www.cavityfreesf.org/ describes citywide ages0–10 strategy co-led by SFDPH/UCSF and school/clinic varnish integration. This is **a coalition/program, not yet a verified standalone tax-deductible donor vehicle**. Its self-reported historical 45% decline is NOT causal impact of the next donation. SFUSD2026 Give Kids a Smile notice verifies current free preventive care event https://www.sfusd.edu/announcements/2026-03-01-give-kids-smile-2026-free-dental-care-kids-ucsf . UCSF's official Children’s Night Clinic organization https://ucsf.campusgroups.com/CNCO/ confirms caregiver/insurance work; SFUSD2023 clinical description https://www.sfusd.edu/announcements/2023-01-15-free-dental-screening-ucsf describes fluoride/sealants/SDF and transition into Denti-Cal. Official school donation route https://dentistry.ucsf.edu/donate supports patient/children/family care but **CNC/prevention-specific acceptance unverified**. Existing clinical volunteering, school programs and public dental payer are substantial overlap. Best next step is donor earmark to genuinely uncovered access/restorative care and full cost per added treated child, not paying again for a reimbursable varnish already scheduled.

## 3. See Well to Learn — preschool vision/amblyopia lead, current vehicle unresolved

SF-specific primary observational study in2021 reports the Prevent Blindness Northern California See Well to Learn program examining 3–5-year-olds in51 Head Start preschools, free glasses and ophthalmology referral: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7893547/ . Cross-sectional wear-compliance study is not a trial of health outcomes. Program's historical parent charity launch/fundraising page (2017,updated2020) https://advocacy.preventblindness.org/prevent-blindness-northern-california-launches-new-campaign/ validates historical delivery, not2026 operations or donation acceptance. Current searches did not locate a sufficiently current primary operating/donor page. Retain as **historical provider lead; do not publish active Donate CTA or claim present-day room for funding**.

Mechanism prior if an active comparable local route is confirmed: c=$300 per additional clinically indicated glasses+followup episode, a=.6,w=.7,u=.03,T=2 =>.0252 QALY, central **$119,048/10**. Optimistic c150,a.9,w.9,u.08,T5=>.324,$4,630/10; pessimistic c500,a.2,w.4,u.01,T.5=>.0004,$12.5m/10; null if no active service or no incremental correction; harms could outweigh correction if referral missed. Do not model whole-life amblyopia prevention unless diagnosed subgroup risk, incremental treatment success, adherence and unilateral-vision utility are separately established. This is a mechanism comparator, not a current organization score or recommendation.

## Scope decision

The most useful newly surfaced under13 lead is Vision To Learn, now **$250,000/10 QALYs central; $79,365 favorable and explicitly superseded as central**. It is not a robust under-$100k finding. Strong SF-specific preschool intervention evidence exists for dental varnish, but marginal payer overlap and smaller health burden weaken central donor-QALY value. Preschool sight care remains promising but historical entity evidence is insufficient for an active donation card. These findings supplement rather than duplicate lead/asthma work. No outreach, donations or site edits performed.
