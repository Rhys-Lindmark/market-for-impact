# GrowSF and Housing Action Coalition donor handoffs

Accessed 2026-09-07. Monetary figures below are explicit analyst best-guess priors for hypothetical incremental grants, NOT measured organizational QALY estimates. No election outcomes, endorsements or voter persuasion receive health credit. Policy spillovers count only SF resident health; construction capital and government costs excluded from donor-dollar numerator but disclosed.

## GrowSF

Central **$10 million per 10 incremental QALYs** on $100,000 general support under a narrow non-electoral housing-policy pathway: Q=.1. Formula Q = grant-share for non-electoral housing implementation (g=.10) × conditional additional SF homes (H=1,000) × implementation/construction probability (p=.20) × grant-induced causal share (a=.10) × discounted net QALY/completed home (h=.05). This simplified allocation assumes work scales linearly with g; no verified budget allocation exists. Best guess is deliberately low-confidence and not calibrated from reported wins.

Optimistic g=.5,H=5,000,p=.5,a=.2,h=.1 =>25 QALYs, $40,000/10; pessimistic g=.02,H=100,p=.1,a=.02,h=.01 =>.00004 QALYs,$25 billion/10. Null if no incremental non-electoral policy work or implementation. Harm if induced policy has net adverse health: central parameters but h=-.005 =>-.01 QALY. For $100k grant, target requires >10 QALYs; central .1 misses by factor 100. A confirmed restricted non-electoral grant could remove uncertain g but must not be represented as currently available.

Actual boundary: https://growsf.org/about/ and https://growsf.org/donate/ (latter redirects to about) explicitly identify **501(c)(4)** and gifts as not federally deductible charitable contributions. Page includes research/newsletter work, housing supply, protected bike lanes and crosswalks, but a substantial described mission is elections; footer identifies GrowSF Voter Guide FPPC #1433436. No verified c3 vehicle or SF housing-implementation restricted fund found. The portal must distinguish c4 general support from a charity and avoid treating its self-reported electoral impact as health evidence.

Mechanism evidence: Asquith/Mast/Reed Philadelphia Fed primary research (2020) supports local construction→lower relative nearby rents, not advocacy attribution or health: https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/housing-and-neighborhoods/supply-shock-versus-demand-shock-the-local-effects-of-new-housing-in-low-income-areas . The health conversion h and donor additionality a have no direct GrowSF evidence. Safer-street mechanisms are real possibilities but are not added here because no donor-funded incremental project, injury exposure or resulting implementation was established; avoid stacking speculative pathways. Existing city planning work, other advocates and existing funding are counterfactual. Practical assessment: poor fit for a health-QALY charitable shortlist; positive but extremely speculative health expected value, with a wide upside tail, not evidence of ineffectiveness at its own civic goals.

## Housing Action Coalition (HAC)

Central **$1 million per 10 incremental QALYs** on a hypothetical $100k SF non-electoral project-support/implementation package. Formula Q = H×p×a×h = 500 conditional extra completed SF homes ×.40 realization probability ×.10 causal share attributable to extra gift ×.05 discounted net health QALY/home =1 QALY. Optimistic H=3,000,p=.6,a=.3,h=.1 =>54 QALYs, **$18,519/10**. Pessimistic H=100,p=.1,a=.02,h=.01=>.002 QALYs, **$500 million/10**. Null if project gets built anyway, remains unfinanced or donor replaces developer membership money. Harm with central H,p,a but h=-.005=>-.1 QALY. Target at h=.05 requires >200 expected gift-attributable completed homes vs central 20. These inputs differ from SPUR because this model concerns a smaller, more concrete project-support pipeline; differences are judgments, not evidence-based organizational superiority.

Actual local program: HAC at 555 Montgomery Street SF provides project review, endorsement and hearing/approval support as well as statewide policy work: https://housingactioncoalition.org/our-work ; 2024 impact report https://housingactioncoalition.org/s/HAC-Impact-Report-022725.pdf describes prospective entitlement, permitting and zoning work. Project endorsements are intermediate outputs, not completed homes, and self-reported historical outputs do not establish donation additionality. Do not count Bay Area or statewide homes outside SF unless SF health spillover explicitly modeled.

Critical donation boundary: HAC has separate **501(c)(3), EIN 83-1881525**, and **501(c)(4), EIN 82-1817943**. Native donate route https://housingactioncoalition.org/donate links to https://secure.qgiv.com/for/fosfhac/ . Payment form says memberships default to c4 unless otherwise requested; c3 support is available. Homepage describes c3 as educational arm and c4 as advocacy arm: https://housingactioncoalition.org/ . Therefore a charitable card must identify c3 explicitly and NOT claim the c3 gift buys c4 advocacy or electoral operations. The modeled project-support mechanism needs correct-entity grant acceptance; it is hypothetical pending verification. Pure c3 education may have lower/more indirect additionality than the central project pipeline assumed here.

Same primary housing-supply study as above supports downstream affordability direction; no HAC-specific causal construction or QALY estimate exists. MTO voucher trials (https://www.nber.org/programs-projects/projects-and-centers/moving-opportunity) are not interchangeable with market housing construction and cannot supply h. Developer fees/memberships, city permitting, existing project capital and other coalition advocates create crowdout/attribution concerns. Need an actual prospective list of SF projects/reforms, whether delay is approval or financing, c3/c4 budget separation, staff capacity bought and independently tracked net completed homes. No outreach performed.
