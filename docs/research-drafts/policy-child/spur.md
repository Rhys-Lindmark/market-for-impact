# SPUR: completed donor research handoff

Researched 2026-09-07. Judgmental decision model, not an organization evaluation or measured cost-effectiveness study. Currency USD; outcome incremental discounted health QALYs accruing to San Francisco residents; no economic earnings or housing asset value counted as QALYs.

## Bottom-line estimate

Central best guess **$1,000,000 per 10 incremental QALYs** for a hypothetical **$100,000 additional SF housing-policy research/implementation grant**, producing 1 expected QALY. Optimistic $20,000/10; pessimistic $500 million/10; null infinite; adverse scenario -0.1 QALY per $100,000. These are explicit analyst priors, not published estimates or calibrated confidence bounds. Does not centrally meet <$100,000/10. General-fund donation performance may be lower/different because of regional allocation and other work.

Formula Q = H × p × a × h. H=1,000 additional completed SF homes conditional on a successfully implemented prospective reform; p=0.20 probability reform is enacted, implemented and homes financed/completed; a=0.10 share of that incremental implementation causally due to this *additional* grant rather than existing staff, other advocates and already planned work; h=0.05 discounted net health QALY per completed home, including residents' housing stress/environment changes and displacement/rent spillovers, not an empirically measured coefficient. 1,000×.20×.10×.05=1. Costs per 10 = 10×100,000/Q. H is conditional scale, a is conditional causal contribution, p is realization probability: do not multiply by an extra success probability or count already enacted laws.

Wide scenarios: optimistic H=5,000,p=.5,a=.2,h=.1 =>50 QALYs, $20,000/10. Pessimistic H=100,p=.1,a=.02,h=.01 =>.002 QALYs,$500m/10. Null if next gift substitutes existing funding or no additional construction. Harm example H=1,000,p=.2,a=.1,h=-.005 =>-.1 QALY if disruption, displacement or pollution costs exceed health gains. Break-even <$100k/10 for the $100k grant requires >10 QALYs, equivalently >200 attributable homes at h=.05, versus central 20.

## Mechanism and causal evidence

SPUR 2025–26 report describes housing, transit and climate research and coalition advocacy, including SF Family Zoning and state housing laws: https://www.spur.org/about/annual-reports/2026 (accessed 2026-09-07). It is self-reported contribution evidence, not counterfactual attribution. Completed historical changes earn no automatic credit for a new donation. Evaluate future implementation, financing bottlenecks and actually completed SF dwellings. Do not convert endorsements, electoral wins, allowable zoning capacity, state loan dollars or homes merely entitled into health gains.

Asquith/Mast/Reed's primary Philadelphia Fed working paper (February 2020) estimates nearby rents 5–7% lower relative to comparison locations following large market-rate apartment construction in studied low-income urban areas: https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/housing-and-neighborhoods/supply-shock-versus-demand-shock-the-local-effects-of-new-housing-in-low-income-areas . This supports the supply→rent mechanism, not donation→construction or rent→QALY. Nonrandom siting, context and rent-data population limit SF extrapolation; do not mechanically multiply the local rent percentage citywide.

Housing-mobility randomized evidence shows some long-term adult health benefits, but it is not an estimate of building market-rate homes. NBER primary MTO data/study documentation identifies low-poverty voucher experiment and 10–16-year follow-up: https://www.nber.org/research/data/moving-opportunity-mto-public-use-files-and-restricted-access-datasets ; original project: https://www.nber.org/programs-projects/projects-and-centers/moving-opportunity . Thus h above remains a broad judgment prior, not a borrowed trial effect.
