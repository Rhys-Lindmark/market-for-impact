## Verified source observations

All sources below were retrieved on 2026-10-18.

1. [2026 National Annual Report](https://www.salvationarmyusa.org/2026-annual-report/), financial summary and service highlights. It reports fiscal 2025 expenses of **$4,308,454,000** (the table is in thousands), **183,649,796 meals**, **10,265,292 shelter nights**, and **1,386,097 households receiving financial assistance**. These are organization-reported historical outputs. They are not mutually exclusive unique beneficiaries or incremental outcomes. The combined financial statement is labeled unaudited, although the narrative says its source is the corporations' audited statements. Do not describe the combined summary as an independently audited national impact evaluation.
2. [Southern Territory Pathway of Hope](https://www.salvationarmyusa.org/usa-southern-territory/pathway-of-hope/), progress section. Reports increased income for 33% of clients and a 39% average earned-income increase for a family. The page does not establish a controlled national effect, a compatible program budget, or sufficient denominator/follow-up definitions to use these percentages as national causal coefficients.
3. [Hong et al., “Hope is Greater than Barriers”](https://ecommons.luc.edu/socialwork_facpubs/187/), Journal of Poverty, published 2020. The abstract describes qualitative analysis of 11 focus groups with 30 service users and 32 service providers in 2017. This supports a description of the mechanism and participant experience; it does not estimate incremental QALYs or causal net household income.

## Initial modeling disposition

No numerical cost-effectiveness model is accepted. `p10`, `p50`, and `p90` prices are **unknown**, not zero. No existing open-BOTEC template covers the national mix of services. Do not attach this organization to the naloxone, diabetes-prevention or tai chi templates merely to generate a price. A new template requires a published cost-effectiveness model it can reproduce under the contribution protocol.

Do not divide total spending by meals, shelter nights, or assisted households: total spending covers multiple services, and any resulting quotient would misallocate costs. Do not sum those outputs into a beneficiary count. National resources or an appeal alone do not verify an unfunded, priced expansion plan.

Health and household resources both matter. Food, housing, treatment, case management and employment support can affect each; the current evidence screen cannot quantify their national net effects. A future resource model must deduct taxes, benefit withdrawals, childcare, commuting and participation costs, handle alternative providers, and prevent overlap with health or housing welfare. Unknown effects must remain unknown rather than being coded as zero.

## Evidence required for a priced contribution

1. Verify the legal recipient and how an unrestricted national gift is allocated among programs and territories, including restrictions and substitution of other funding.
2. Obtain program-specific full costs and a current, priced, unfunded expansion plan with its capacity ceiling.
3. Select a program with compatible native outcomes and a published causal or cost-effectiveness model. Pathway of Hope is a lead for further work, not an accepted national proxy.
4. Establish additional outcomes relative to available alternative services, persistence, harms, household costs and net resource changes; distinguish unique people from repeated contacts.
5. Build and reproduce a template, document input uncertainty and null/adverse cases, and obtain independent source/model review before acceptance.

No controlled national impact estimate or compatible national program cost model was located in this bounded search. This is a search limitation, not proof that none exists. No substituted numerical assumptions were used. Author affiliation/conflicts have not been affirmatively collected; resolve before producing a recipe.
