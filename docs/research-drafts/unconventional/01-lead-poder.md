# Unconventional 01 — PODER / Fix Lead SF enrollment support

Research date: 2026-09-07. Bottom line: plausible leverage on public remediation, but no verified PODER remediation or paid navigation tranche. Keep **provisional implementer**, not an established additional funded service. Childhood exposure prevention matters most before/under age five; the actual local rule is **child younger than six living or visiting frequently**, not everyone under thirteen.

## Effect evidence, including disconfirmation

- [Braun et al., JAMA Pediatrics 2018 HOME randomized trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2696978), DOI 10.1001/jamapediatrics.2018.2382: prenatal comprehensive residential intervention reduced dust lead, but overall child blood-lead change was −6% (95% CI −17% to +6%; P=.29). Broad neurobehavioral improvement was not demonstrated. Higher-exposure subgroup benefit cannot be exported as an unconditional race coefficient.
- [Muennig, 2009](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/382153) models approximately **0.2 discounted lifetime QALYs per child** from reducing the historical US birth-to-six exposure distribution to below 1 µg/dL. It is **not QALYs per µg/dL, per remediation, or a measured intervention effect**. The education-to-health bridge is observational/model-based. Earnings are separate and excluded here. This is a coarse upper-calibration; a local linear fraction is an analyst assumption, not a validated dose-response.

## Explicit exploratory model — donor facilitation cost, public repair costs excluded

Unit: one completed, safety-cleared navigation/remediation episode, with failed enrollment effort allocated into its cost. Do not additionally multiply by a completion probability unless converting to cost per referral. Formula:

`Q = children_under6 × 0.2 × realized_fraction_of_full_exposure_benefit × health_bridge_retention × funding_additionality`

`USD/10Q = 10 × donor_facilitation_cost / Q`

All inputs below except the external 0.2 calibration are **judgments**, not PODER quotes or local effects. Fraction-of-benefit includes baseline exposure severity, remaining exposure sources, timing and safe durable remediation; do not add another blood-lead multiplier to it.

| Scenario | Cost | Children | Realized fraction | Bridge retention | Additionality | Q | USD/10Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | $2,000 | 1 | .25 | .5 | .5 | .0125 | $1,600,000 |
| Favorable | $500 | 1.5 | .75 | .75 | .75 | .1265625 | $39,506.17 |
| Unfavorable | $5,000 | .5 | .05 | .25 | .25 | .0003125 | $160,000,000 |
| Null | any positive cost | — | 0 or additionality 0 | — | — | 0 | No finite positive cost-effectiveness |

Central cost is a planning allowance for labor-intensive consent, enrollment and coordination, including unsuccessful cases—not measured. Central exposure fraction is deliberately partial because the RCT's overall null, other lead sources, and non-total removal argue against full theoretical exposure elimination. This is a **conditional model sketch**, not an evidence-established best local estimate. Lifetime effects are already discounted in 0.2; no extra lifetime-years multiplier. Do not generalize the same calibration to ages 6–12: prevention can still matter, but neither eligibility nor the early-childhood coefficient establishes that value.

At central benefits, cost must be below **$125 per completed episode** to beat $100,000/10Q. At $2,000 cost, required Q is .2; the entire unattenuated 0.2 calibration for one child would only reach the threshold, before counterfactual dilution. A full-resource estimate must add induced inspection, abatement, relocation and maintenance costs; the donor-only result does not make those resources free.
