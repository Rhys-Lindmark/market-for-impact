# Unconventional 02 — Brightline Defense, targeted SRO filtration

Research date: 2026-09-07. **Verified SF implementer; exploratory central $1.8M per 10 QALYs, favorable $66,667, unfavorable $144M, credible null. No verified marginal tranche.** Evaluate adult SRO residents with respiratory illness and high indoor pollution; do not apply a pediatric-asthma effect indiscriminately to all SRO residents.

## Local evidence and financing

[Brightline's primary case study](https://www.brightlinedefense.org/news/em-magazine) reports 150 air purifiers received through an Air District mini-grant and distributed to Eastern SF residents, targeting self-reported respiratory problems. Before rules changed, staff spent over 100 hours helping applicants and fewer than half received a purifier. These historical barriers do not describe the current counterfactual. Over 90% improvement on post-distribution surveys is uncontrolled self-report, without a verified response denominator; it is not a 90% causal health effect.

[Current coalition page](https://www.brightlinedefense.org/community-coalitions/) says 600 people reached through a mixture of purifier distribution and workshops, **not 600 purifier recipients**. Partners are La Voz Latina and Central City SRO Collaborative. [Official Air District program](https://www.baaqmd.gov/en/About-Air-Quality/Wildfire-Air-Quality-Response-Program/Clean-Air-Filtration-Program?swcfpc=1) confirms public provision through health centers and community grantees. [Brightline donation page](https://www.brightlinedefense.org/donate) confirms nonprofit donations, not a priced restricted filter expansion. Filters, maintenance, replacement, electricity and failed outreach belong in full delivery cost even if paid by others.

## Causal evidence and non-equivalence

[CLEAN AIR randomized trial, Hansel et al., published online August 2021 / journal February 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34449285/), DOI 10.1164/rccm.202103-0604OC: 116 former smokers with moderate/severe COPD, active versus sham air cleaners, six months, 84.5% completion. Primary total SGRQ was not significantly different; some respiratory symptom and moderate-exacerbation outcomes improved. Per-protocol gains cannot replace randomized ITT. SGRQ is **not a preference utility** and no trial QALY is claimed here.

[Lanphear et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21149427/), DOI 10.1542/peds.2009-2312: 225 children age 6–12 exposed to household smoking, two active/sham cleaners, 95% retention; fewer unscheduled asthma visits but no significant parent-reported symptom difference. This supports a possible mechanism, not the adult SRO numerical effect. [2022 rural Latino-child trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34980119/) tests filtration added to education in only 75 poorly controlled children: neither its geography nor full education package should be attributed to a single SF filter.

## Transparent planning model (all numerical inputs below are judgments)

`Q = expected average preference-utility gain while effectively used × effective-use years × funding additionality`

`USD/10Q = 10 × full filter-and-delivery episode cost / Q`

Expected utility already includes clinical severity/response heterogeneity and modality transfer; effective-use years cover non-use and maintenance. No second response, PM reduction, asthma-visit or adherence multiplier. Model one resident per purifier, no uncounted household co-benefit, mortality or lifetime extrapolation.

| Scenario | Full cost | Average utility gain | Effective-use years | Additionality | Q | USD/10Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | $450 | .01 | .5 | .5 | .0025 | $1,800,000 |
| Favorable | $150 | .03 | 1 | .75 | .0225 | $66,666.67 |
| Unfavorable | $900 | .001 | .25 | .25 | .0000625 | $144,000,000 |
| Null | positive | 0, no use, or no additional provision | — | — | 0 | No finite positive ratio |

Central $450 planning allowance: $200 device + $100 consumables/electricity + $150 installation, outreach and follow-up allocation; these are **not vendor prices or a Brightline quote**. Six effective months avoids unsupported multiple-year persistence. A small .01 expected utility benefit reflects potentially modest symptom relief with mixed randomized primary results, but is not measured or mathematically inferred from SGRQ. This remains a low-confidence hypothesis, not a published health-economic result. Favorable costs require genuinely efficient procurement/delivery, not silently treating public devices as costless.

At central cost/use/additionality, utility would need to exceed **.18** to beat $100K/10Q; at central Q, cost must be under **$25**. A donor-only calculation may exclude an already secured in-kind device's purchase price only if new use is truly unlocked and the resource perspective is shown alongside it.

## Funding room and falsifiers

Seek a separately budgeted replacement-filter/installation tranche beyond current Air District grants, occupancy-linked eligibility, baseline PM and respiratory severity, device runtime, six-month preference utility, and unique recipients. Null if existing free provision would occur, replacement filters are available anyway, respiratory symptoms are not particle-responsive, runtime is low or room pollution remains uncontrolled. Noise, electricity burden and ozone-producing devices can harm; use appropriate non-ozone filtration. No claims that filters remove all gases, prevent all smoke harm, or substitute for respiratory treatment. Do not add the same resident's entire asthma-home-visit benefit to this estimate.
