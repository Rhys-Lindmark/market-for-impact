# Unconventional 03 — Rebuilding Together San Francisco, targeted fall-hazard repairs

Research date: 2026-09-07. Verified distinct SF nonprofit; keep separate from PODER outreach but do not double-count the same household's health gains. **Conditional central $5M/10 QALYs; favorable $66,667, unfavorable $1.5B; null/harm credible.** A targeted assessment plus completed repairs and follow-up is the intervention, not generic renovations or a leaflet.

## Local service and cost boundaries

[RTSF services](https://rebuildingtogethersf.org/what-we-do/) verify year-round home repairs/modifications, mobility/safety for older adults and people with disabilities, and MOHCD-supported Critical Repairs including fall-prevention installations. The services page says the Senior Home Repair Program is no longer accepting applications. However, [the current application page](https://rebuildingtogethersf.org/apply/) says rolling applications and **funding to serve 100 people by December 31, 2026**. Preserve that apparent program-specific discrepancy: neither a universal closure nor an unfunded waiting list is established. Landlord approval, income documentation and tenure documentation are requested.

[FY25–26 sponsorship page](https://rebuildingtogethersf.org/homeishealth/) offers $5,000/$7,500/$10,000 sponsorship levels with volunteer/recognition benefits. These are **not per-household repair prices**. No fully loaded marginal fall-repair cost or OT staffing arrangement verified. Include assessment, professional installation, equipment, administration and booster; volunteer labor is a resource even if not a donor cash outlay.

## Trial evidence

[Stark et al. 2021 HARP RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34463746/), DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.22044: 310 high-risk community-dwelling older St Louis adults, OT-delivered individualized assessment/removal over two weeks plus six-month booster versus usual services. Twelve-month retention approximately 81–82%. Secondary fall rates 1.5 versus 2.3/person-year (difference .8); primary hazard of first fall not improved. Reported quality-of-life difference was nonsignificant. This is not proof that .8 serious injuries were prevented; most falls are not serious injury. National model fidelity/training is described by [the originating laboratory](https://starklab.wustl.edu/interventions-resources/harp/); RTSF is not verified as delivering HARP.

[Cockayne et al. 2021 OTIS RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34254934/), DOI 10.3310/hta25460: 1,331 UK older adults; no demonstrated fall reduction (IRR1.17, CI.99–1.38). Economic base case reported **−.0042 QALY** and added payer cost. Thus direct utility evidence is not uniformly positive. Do not take a favorable trial's secondary falls result and present a measured positive QALY from these trials. No avoided payer costs are subtracted from donor costs here.

## Explicit analyst model

`Q = .8 fewer falls/person-year × local-effect retention × years × expected QALY loss per prevented fall × funding additionality`

`USD/10Q = 10 × full completed-course cost / Q`

Only .8 is directly calculated from a source trial. All other numbers below are judgments. Expected QALY/fall averages harmless falls and injuries; it is **not** a fracture decrement and includes the entire modeled consequence of a prevented fall. No additional fracture, fear, mortality or housing-retention bonus.

| Scenario | Cost | Effect retention | Years | Q/fall | Additionality | Q | USD/10Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | $1,000 | .5 | 1 | .01 | .5 | .002 | $5,000,000 |
| Favorable | $400 | 1 | 2 | .05 | .75 | .06 | $66,666.67 |
| Unfavorable | $3,000 | .1 | .5 | .002 | .25 | .00002 | $1,500,000,000 |

Central cost allowance: $300 assessment/follow-up + $400 installation labor + $200 materials + $100 coordination; not a RTSF quote. A .01 Q/fall loss equals 3.65 full-health-equivalent days averaged over **all falls**; chosen as a modest planning bridge, not validated local epidemiology. Transfer halves the secondary trial effect given fidelity mismatch and conflicting RCTs. One year avoids extrapolating an entire durable structure's lifespan into maintained health benefits. Favorable two-year scenario is extrapolation, and its .05 average Q/fall requires unusually harmful preventable falls; it is not the expected general older-adult case.

Null if no causal reduction/additionality; signed negative reduction permits harm and should display health loss, not a negative bargain ratio. At central cost/effect/time/additionality, average Q/fall must exceed **.5 QALY per prevented fall** to beat $100K/10Q; alternatively cost must be below **$20** at central Q. This strongly disfavors a general repairs-to-health-only threshold claim without much sharper risk targeting or substantial unmodeled nonhealth value.

## Fundability / decisive tests

Identify high-risk residents with documented modifiable hazards and no already-funded repair, a priced skilled-work/assessment tranche, installation completion, OT-equivalent fidelity, and a comparator tracking falls **and injuries/preferences**, not satisfaction. Existing funding for100 people means the next donation may replace public/sponsor commitments. Do not presume limited intake is solved by money: contractor capacity and permissions can bind. No verified open marginal funding gap. Mobility/autonomy, affordability preservation and dignity may matter independently of this narrow health-only estimate.
