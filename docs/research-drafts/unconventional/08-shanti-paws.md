# Unconventional 08 — Shanti PAWS, pet care enabling human treatment

Research date:2026-09-07. Verified SF service; evaluate a **short foster/in-home pet-care episode tied to a scheduled human treatment**, not the whole pet food/veterinary program. Conditional central **$1M/10humanQALYs**, favorable$26,667, unfavorable$200M/null. No causal treatment-completion effect or current marginal package is measured. Animal welfare is valuable but deliberately outside this human-QALY denominator.

## Local reality and bottleneck

[Current PAWS page](https://www.shanti.org/programs-services/pets-are-wonderful-support/) provides free pet support for older adults and adults with illness/disability, including fostering, dog walking, in-home cat care, veterinary help and food. **Client waitlist currently closed**, and City Disability and Aging Services partly funds the program. Closure is not proof an extra check can expand capacity.

[Volunteer page](https://www.shanti.org/volunteer/) verifies foster/in-home roles. [Historical program start-up kit](https://www.shanti.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/paws_start_up_kit.pdf) explicitly links foster care to hospital/rehab stays and housing emergencies; its old rates/eligibility/logistics are not current promises. [Winter2020 PAWS story](https://www.shanti.org/news-events/our-news/paws-prints-winter-2020/) requests emergency foster volunteers and illustrates overlapping Shanti cancer navigation, but is anecdotal—not an extra completed treatment caused by fostering. Fund legal entity **Shanti Project**, not unrelatedPAWS organizations nationwide.

## Health evidence and disconfirmation

[Singer,Hart,Zasloff1995 primary survey](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.1995.77.3.851) surveyed66homeless pet owners at a veterinary clinic; pet-incompatible housing was widely unacceptable. This is selected preference data, not evidence that foster care increases hospital treatment by93%.

[Pet ownership among homeless youth2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4194276/) concerns associations with service use/mental health and differs from PAWS's older/disabled population. [2026 UK OneHealth qualitative study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0354517) identifies plausible temporary-care coordination enabling inpatient treatment; interviews do not estimate a causal rate or QALYs. Pet ownership can provide support while creating access constraints; neither association proves PAWS reverses a specific health decrement. No randomized preference-utility or human treatment-completion effect found.

## Explicit hypothetical episode model

`Q = increase in probability of completing the scheduled human treatment because pet care is arranged × net Q gained by that treatment versus its actual delay/alternative × funding additionality`

`USD/10Q = 10 × pet-care episode cost / Q`

| Scenario | Cost | Incremental completion probability | Net Q/completion | Funding additionality | Q | USD/10Q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Central | $500 | .2 | .05 | .5 | .005 | $1,000,000 |
| Favorable | $250 | .5 | .25 | .75 | .09375 | $26,666.67 |
| Unfavorable | $2,500 | .05 | .01 | .25 | .000125 | $200,000,000 |

All numbers are **analyst judgments**, not local measurements. Central$500 allowance:7care-days×$30resource/day+$250coordination/supplies+$40administration. Foster volunteers can lower cash expenditure but not necessarily resource/opportunity cost. .2completion increase means most scheduled care occurs anyway or remains blocked for other reasons. .05Q corresponds to roughly a.2utility advantage for three months, not a lifetime saved; it is a placeholder for the **net treatment-versus-delay** effect. Actual condition/procedure must replace it before decision-ready use. Funding factor handles replacement of existing PAWS/public/family support, separate from the clinical completion counterfactual.

Centralcare/Q assumptions are deliberately modest but not empirically identifiable; this is a scenario sketch rather than a measured best guess. Favorable.25Q requires a much more consequential treatment delay and needs clinical corroboration. At centralcost/probability/funding, treatment would have to add **more than.5Q** versus delay to beat$100K/10Q; atcentralQ the pet-care episode must cost under$50. No animal lives, companionship Q, Shanti counseling Q or public hospital savings are added.

The [FY2022–23 impact report](https://www.shanti.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Shanti_Impact-Report_2023_Final.pdf), page8, reports a renewed four-year Animal Bonding contract serving more than200people/250pets. It does not isolate foster episodes or demonstrate an unfunded next client. This is useful evidence of funded commitments, not current spare capacity.

## Fundability and falsifiers

Prioritize clinician/social-worker-identified treatment blocked specifically by pet care, with a confirmed foster slot and reunification plan. Track planned/actual treatment completion, alternative care arrangements and how long delays would otherwise last. Null if friends/other charities provide care, treatment occurs regardless, clinical treatment adds no net health relative to brief delay, or foster recruitment not money is the bottleneck. Separation stress, unsafe foster care and failure to reunite can harm humans and animals; informed consent/animal welfare safeguards required. Induced clinical-treatment costs remain real: show a full-resource view alongside donor pet-care-only cost. No verified current expandable tranche or funding-room dollars.
