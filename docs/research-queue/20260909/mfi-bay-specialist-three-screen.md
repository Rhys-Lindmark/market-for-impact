## Primary clinical bridge and decisive limitations for BACC
[van den Berg et al2015 primary randomized trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2091663):155 people with chronic PTSD plus psychotic disorders; EMDR55/PE53/wait47; eight90-minute sessions. Loss of PTSD diagnosis60.0%EMDR versus27.7%wait; full remission16.4%versus6.4% was not significant. Benefits observed at six months; wait patients then offered active treatment. No observed QALYs or indefinite untreated control. Trial clinicians had protocol-specific training, supervised cases and audited fidelity. These facts support plausibility but not exact routine BACC effectiveness. Use only PTSD utility improvement; don't add psychosis/depression/suicide lives to the same patients.

[Power et al2002 primary trial](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cpp.341):105primary-care PTSD patients,≤10sessions; both EMDR and exposure/cognitive restructuring outperformed wait. Important dropout/completer analysis, and15-month follow-up was of active groups rather than an untreated counterfactual; subsequent treatment affected long-term estimates. Not a license to select low delivered session counts among completers as per-enrolled cost.

## Decision
The $1,360 course in the screening calculator is NOT a BACC quote: it assumes fifteen 50-minute treatment units plus one intake at $85, enough for the trial's720 treatment minutes. BACC's actual session length is unknown. The $850 comparator is a deliberately cheaper planning scenario, not proof of equal clinical dosage. The point is to test this uncertainty, not assert that duration matching necessarily costs $1,360.

Illustrative outputs: central total donor $1.568M, Bay $1.651M and SF $6.272M per10Q; favorable short-course Bay $75,525, versus $120,840 with the higher contact-hour planning cost. These are not recommended estimates. The raw calculator outputs include every gross and net resource ratio. Its 10 scenarios and 60 checks pass. The $400/course outside-resource allowance is an explicit composite prior for patient time, partner/clinical resources not covered by the donor cash; it is not $400 of insurance transfers counted as additional production. Whole-gift allocation40%, additionality60%, eligible unique fraction90%, clinical transfer60%, utility gap0.2 and later-care convergence hazard0.5/year centrally are all uncalibrated priors. Harms are gift-date PV and share the same nested residence fractions in this screening-only model. No lifetime mortality prevention is credited.

Preserve BACC as the best **bounded further-verification** candidate, not as a current high-probability threshold pass. Do not spend a full report slot yet. Companion mfi-bacc-feasibility.mjs makes the arithmetic transparent, with finite symptom-relief duration, later alternative-care convergence, relapse, mortality, full donation numerator, independent Bay/SF shares, public/private outside resources and retained alternative harms. All causal entries are explicitly illustrative priors, not posterior estimates. It shows why both unusually strong lasting benefit and high whole-gift allocation/additionality are needed; favorable assumptions should not become a recommendation.

Next narrow research that could change the decision: verified session duration and full course cost; number of additional subsidized trauma patients possible at10k versus100k; adultPTSD percentage of ordinary-gift spending; whether effective alternative care arrives within six months; and patient-residence shares. No outreach performed. Full publication packet deliberately not manufactured from these missing facts.
