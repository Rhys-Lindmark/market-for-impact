# Three Bay child/maternal clinical opportunities — bounded screen

Research date: 2026-09-09 UTC. Research only; no new completed report, recommendation, funding-room verification, or organization-count increment.

## Selection

**Sonrisas Dental Health is the strongest next research candidate of these three, but none presently looks likely to beat $100,000 per 10 QALYs on a whole unrestricted gift.** Mothers' Milk Bank California initially looked strongest because of randomized neonatal evidence; current delivery and trial-population checks materially weaken that hypothesis. Native American Health Center has a genuinely low-cost preventive intervention, but a broad portfolio and a public-care counterfactual dilute a gift's attributable benefit. Do not turn this relative ranking into a favorable recommendation.

## Comparable whole-gift screens

All rows use a hypothetical $100,000 ordinary unrestricted gift. Costs, allocation, additionality and utility bridges below are **screening judgments**, not observed organizational marginal yields. The numerator retains the entire gift; all unmodeled portfolio activity remains in cost and receives no quantified health credit. These are conditional clinical screens, not full audited portfolio estimates. No lifetime health is counted. Price is `10 × cost / positive net Q`; null/negative health has no favorable price.

| Candidate | Conditional US Q per gift | Donor USD / 10 Q | Partial associated-resource USD / 10 Q | Main missing bridge |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Sonrisas Dental Health | 0.375 | $2.67m | $2.99m | Gift-funded completed symptomatic episodes and earlier-care duration, not visits |
| Mothers' Milk Bank California | 0.015 | $66.7m | $83.3m | Incremental full-course very-preterm exposure, not short outpatient milk subsidy |
| Native American Health Center | 0.0125 | $80m | $82.5m | Whole-gift pediatric prevention allocation and additional preventive courses beyond covered care |

Resource columns add illustrative outside resource envelopes, without subtracting unverified avoided medical costs. They are partial associated resources, not measured net societal costs. The donation may replace other financing, which belongs in the clinical additionality factor, but does not justify dropping donor expenditure. Resource envelopes count outside resources only once, not again when already included in an organizational course price.

### 1. Sonrisas Dental Health — selected for next research, not publication-ready

Exact recipient: Sonrisas Dental Health, EIN 94-3390196. Current official pages describe clinical dentistry, school/community screening and navigation, community health workers, provider development, and a new disability-acclimation pilot. Clinics are in San Mateo County; the ordinary gift is not solely a children's screening or extraction gift. The patient page reports 45% of visits are pediatric and more than 75% serve people facing financial or other access barriers. These are visit shares, not gift allocation, symptomatic-case shares, causal completion, or unique people. [Programs](https://sonrisasdental.org/programs/), [patients](https://sonrisasdental.org/patients/).

The current homepage's Coastside Gives appeal reports $50,000 for 300 visits serving 100 neighbors. That is $166.67 per visit and $500 per three-visit patient episode as fundraising arithmetic, **not** a current complete marginal treatment quote. This updates the previous screen's $150 completion assumption: a visit must not be relabeled a completed episode. [Homepage](https://sonrisasdental.org/).

Conditional screen: 20% gift allocation to complete symptomatic pediatric episodes; $500 gift-funded episode allowance; 50% service additionality after other donors/public insurance; 75% successful, distinct clinical relief among funded episodes; 0.1 utility improvement for 0.25 years of earlier relief, giving 0.025 Q per relieved episode. `100000 × .20 / 500 × .50 × .75 × .025 = .375 Q`. The remaining 80% costs remain. Add a $300 outside-resource envelope per funded episode: $112,000 total associated resources. Clinical utility and timing are judgments, not inferred from testimonials or raw questionnaire scores. Bay share 1 and SF share 0 are screen location assumptions, not measured residence shares; a full model must distinguish service location and beneficiary residence.

Public baseline: clinics already serve Medicaid beneficiaries and provide a sliding-fee program; lack of insurance is not equivalent to no future treatment. Do not give all pediatric patients the symptomatic coefficient. Preventive and acute-care populations must be separated; do not stack caries prevention and later pain relief for the same lesion. Current annual-report link resolves to a flipbook with no readable content in the web extractor: [FY2024–25 report](https://sonrisasdental.org/impact-report-2025/). Current financial scope, reimbursements, direct event costs and treatment mix remain necessary before a publishable model.

Favorable but finite illustration: allocation .40, complete cost $300, additionality .80, success .90, and .05 Q per earlier episode produces 4.8 Q, or $208,333 donor/10Q. This is jointly favorable, not a probability interval. Clinical null, no additional care and treatment-harm cases must also be retained. At central allocation, cost, additionality and completion, hitting $100k/10Q would require .667 Q per relieved episode, versus the .025-Q quarter-year screen: an implausibly large generic acute dental bridge without a very different case mix.

### 2. Mothers' Milk Bank California — strongest RCT, failed cheap-gift translation

Primary organization describes nationwide hospital and family milk processing, donor recruitment, testing, storage, shipping, outreach and education. Hospitals/families pay processing fees and insurers may reimburse medically necessary milk. Donated raw milk is not zero-resource finished product; donor time and outside clinical resources matter. [FAQ](https://mothersmilk.org/about-us/faq/).

The new Healthy Start Fund is a $25,000 launch fund for California families. Typical approved assistance is up to 40 ounces/week for 1–2 weeks. It includes newborns, post-NICU recovery and short maternal-supply bridges, not exclusively very-preterm NICU infants. A financial gift to the whole organization cannot all be assigned to that fund, and the fund's short support cannot receive the benefit of prolonged high-risk trial feeding. [Healthy Start Fund](https://mothersmilk.org/request-milk/healthy-start-fund/).

Randomized synthesis: donor milk versus formula when maternal milk is insufficient reduces NEC in very-preterm/VLBW neonatal patients (RR .53; absolute reduction about .03), but pooled all-cause mortality RR is 1.00 (95% CI .76–1.31), and infection benefit is not demonstrated. Contemporary trials include up to 90–120 days of assigned feeding. These are not newborn-general-population effects. [Cochrane research](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39239939/), [full methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378496/). The large MILK trial found no cognitive advantage at 22–26 months and NEC 4.2% versus 9.0%; it does not license NEC cases × case fatality × lifetime health as an additional benefit while ignoring randomized mortality. [MILK trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38497706/).

Current public baseline is material: DHCS APL 26-005 requires managed-care plans to arrange medically necessary donor milk, including timely access policies. Coverage is not proof of perfect access, but must be included in the no-gift comparison. [DHCS, section D, page 32](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/th/formsandpubs/Documents/MMCDAPLsandPolicyLetters/APL%202026/APL26-005.pdf).

Generous conditional high-risk screen, not an estimated observed gift mix: 50% whole-gift allocation / $2,000 complete matched feeding-course allowance × 25% additionality × 80% eligibility/completion × .03 fewer NEC cases × .1 finite morbidity Q per avoided case = .015 Q. Mortality and neurodevelopment credit zero. The course price, eligible share, and morbidity-Q conversion are all unresolved priors; $2,000 is not an MMB quote. $1,000 outside resources per funded course adds $25,000 to the entire gift. Nationwide delivery means Bay/SF shares cannot be inferred from its San Jose headquarters.

The exact missing causal bridge is **ordinary dollars → otherwise unavailable complete high-risk donor-milk exposure → incremental NEC morbidity avoided under current hospital purchasing/public coverage**. Short-term outpatient access is real, but clinical NEC evidence does not establish its marginal health yield. Obtain eligible-course volume, regional recipients, displaced purchases, current course resources and neonatal morbidity utility before replacing this screen with a report. [Financials page](https://mothersmilk.org/financials/) links a FY2025 audit, but it has not been read in this bounded screen; no finance numbers are asserted. Do not adopt homepage cumulative 'babies fed' or 'lives saved' counters as unique clinical outcomes.

### 3. Native American Health Center — real prevention, heavily diluted ordinary gift

Bay recipient is Native American Health Center, not Sacramento Native American Health Center. Current official site lists medical, dental, behavioral health, WIC, school-based health and community wellness. Pediatric dental care emphasizes ages 0–5; school-based services explicitly include fluoride varnish, fillings and exams. Locations establish Oakland and SF operations. [Dental](https://www.nativehealth.org/services/dental/), [school services](https://www.nativehealth.org/services/school-based-health-centers/), [portfolio](https://www.nativehealth.org/services/).

Local randomized evidence: Weintraub et al. randomized 376 initially caries-free young children from low-income SF families to counseling with or without annual/semiannual varnish over two years. It supports caries prevention, not directly QALYs or mortality; odds ratios must not be treated as absolute risk reductions. Protocol deviation and incomplete follow-up matter. [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16434737/). In the MAYA trial, preventive scheduled varnish did not improve incidence over a control that received therapeutic varnish when early lesions appeared; this is particularly relevant to an active publicly funded comparator. [MAYA](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21999806/).

Conditional screen: 5% whole-gift allocation to two-year pediatric prevention / $160 course cash; .4 additionality beyond public care; .8 course completion; .10 absolute caries incidence reduction judgment; .25 symptomatic fraction; .1 utility difference for .5 years of symptoms avoided. Product = .0125 Q. $100 outside resources/course adds $3,125. The prevention effect is a two-year course effect, not per application, and the half-year symptom interval is already finite. Every input except the existence of varnish delivery is a modeling judgment. Remaining 95% gift has zero quantified benefit, not zero cost. Public Medi-Cal/sliding-scale and school no-out-of-pocket arrangements are explicit on current pages; no-insurance students already have school-care access. Bay/SF attribution requires residence data; screening use Bay 1/SF .15 only as labeled priors.

## Decision and bounded handoff

No candidate has a defensible low-cost whole-gift recommendation from present evidence. Sonrisas has the best next-research probability because its current pediatric delivery and relatively concentrated clinical portfolio are directly established; draft equation above is useful even if the ultimate result remains negative. A completed publication packet would require current complete finances and a justified distinct symptomatic-case funnel, not just the available visit counters. MMB should not leapfrog it using a survival claim contradicted by the relevant randomized synthesis. NAHC is a weaker whole-gift child-prevention hypothesis; its many other clinical benefits are unquantified, not demonstrated absent.
