# La Casa de las Madres: conditional post-shelter advocacy model

Research-only packet; checked 2026-09-08. No Site edits, offers, outreach or funding commitments.

## Bottom line

**Conditional maternal/adult mental-health estimate: $66.02 million per ten incremental QALYs**, favorable $1.38m, pessimistic $52.36bn, with explicit clinical/utility/funding nulls and harm. This is **not a measured La Casa impact estimate or a currently available grant**. Actual SF advocacy is verified, but neither a standardized ten-week Community Advocacy Project nor a nine-month Critical Time Intervention (CTI) course is verified at La Casa. The numerical model below uses a **conditional nine-month CTI-like follow-up episode**, because its primary paper provides an inspectable PTSD symptom contrast and dose. Do not relabel it as the effect of existing drop-in counseling, shelter or an available new cohort.

The main acceptance obstacle is local program match, not arithmetic. The QALY conversion is an explicit analyst judgment; the randomized primary quality-of-life endpoint was null. A zero-centered health estimate remains defensible. The finite central is a conditional small-positive scenario, not a posterior expected ICER or proof of cost-effectiveness.

## 1. Actual program and unit

[Current support overview](https://www.lacasa.org/support) describes services across nine community locations: safety planning, risk assessment, counseling, groups, housing assistance and referrals. [Drop-In Center description](https://www.lacasa.org/our-programs/2018/11/15/drop-in-counseling-center), dated November 16, 2018 and still linked by the current site, locates free confidential practical/emotional support at 1269 Howard Street, SF. [Shelter description](https://www.lacasa.org/our-programs/2018/11/15/emergency-shelter), November 15, 2018, describes up-to-eight-week stays and onsite advocacy. This establishes a plausible transition partner, not a proven manualized post-discharge intervention. Historical advertised bed capacity is not current vacant capacity.

[FY2025 report](https://www.lacasa.org/s/2025-Annual-Report.pdf) confirms ongoing SF delivery, including 231 women and children sheltered and 6,571 days/nights of service; 885 survivors used drop-in/appointment services. These overlapping all-age outputs are **not** counts of eligible adult post-shelter participants and cannot supply a cost-per-course denominator. Self-reported safety/milestone percentages have no randomized comparison. No unused post-shelter slot count, marginal price, restricted donation acceptance or current expansion request was found.

**Model unit:** one adult survivor allocated a nine-month CTI-like transition episode, having spent at least six weeks in shelter and moving to independent housing in the agency service area. These are trial-compatible analytic restrictions, not La Casa's general eligibility rules. The unit is assigned/enrolled, not a completer. No invented number of purchasable offers is supplied. An unrestricted gift supports La Casa overall and cannot be assumed to purchase this unit.

## 2. Randomized native outcomes: keep the interventions separate

### Community Advocacy Project: supportive but not the numerical anchor

[Sullivan and Bybee, 1999 randomized study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10028208/) studied individual ten-week post-shelter advocacy and reported reduced subsequent violence and improved quality of life/social support. [Primary investigator report archived by NIJ, 2000, NCJ185728](https://www.ojp.gov/library/publications/using-longitudinal-data-set-further-our-understanding-trajectory-intimate) describes 278 former shelter residents, interviews from exit through 36 months, and community-resource mobilization. All participants had already used shelter. The trial does not establish shelter versus no shelter, and the quality-of-life measure is not a preference-weighted QALY scale. Exact table-level effect and dose/cost extraction from the full 109-page report is not completed here; no invented risk difference is used. Do not combine its ten-week price with CTI's nine-month effect.

### CTI: inspectable active-comparator anchor and contrary evidence

[Lako et al., 2018 primary RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5938300/) randomized 136 women leaving nine Dutch shelters: CTI70, usual care66. Eligibility included adult age, at least six weeks in shelter and transition to housing without daily supervision. CTI comprised three three-month phases. Table 1 specifies 10 pre-discharge hours, 36 transition hours, 24 try-out hours and 6–12 transfer hours: **76–82 planned contact hours**, not ten weekly sessions.

Native adjusted follow-up difference: **−7.27 points on the 15-item Impact of Event Scale (IES)**, 95% CI −14.31 to −.22, P=.04. This is a secondary symptom outcome, not 7.27 QALYs, a PTSD diagnosis remission rate or a standardized effect. Primary quality of life, re-abuse and depression showed no significant differences. Usual shelter services had already adopted strengths-based care. Fidelity was only fair, follow-up ended at nine months, baseline imbalance and multiple secondary outcomes limit certainty. The trial reports an average follow-up contrast, not necessarily an instantaneous full effect at discharge.

The IES contrast is used because it is directly extractable and health-related, **not because the null primary outcome can be ignored**. Its narrow significance and selection among several outcomes require shrinkage and a genuine zero scenario. Netherlands-to-SF services, languages and housing availability are material transport issues. The two trials are not pooled or treated as replications of one identical program.

## 3. Explicit utility/time bridge

No validated IES15-to-preference-based utility mapping was verified. Central judgment `b=.001 utility units per IES point` makes the unattenuated 7.27-point contrast equivalent to .00727 average utility—not a published mapping. This slope only applies locally around the modeled contrast, not across the entire IES range or other PTSD scales. It should never be described as the study's QALY result.

Use **.5 effective years of benefit**, representing an area under the utility-difference curve inside the nine-month episode, allowing for ramp-up and fade. Do not multiply .5 by nine months again. The model does not claim benefit for six months after treatment ends. Favorable duration .75 year remains within observed follow-up; no lifetime PTSD, child development, mortality or intergenerational tail is added. Central local clinical transfer `t=.5` also shrinks secondary-outcome and delivery uncertainty; it is not a completion multiplier. Trial assignment effects already include imperfect participation.

A direct preference-based null or `b=0` is an important alternative: the study's quality-of-life endpoint did not improve. Do not sum a constructed PTSD QALY with broad quality of life, depression, “needs met,” housing or violence scores describing the same burden. Re-abuse prevention receives **zero extra QALYs** in this CTI-based model; its trial outcome was null. Broader benefits remain unquantified rather than proven absent.

## 4. Costs and funded baseline

**Central constructed donor cost C=$6,000 per assigned nine-month episode:** 82 hours × $60 fully loaded advocate labor = $4,920; plus $1,080 for training/supervision, secure documentation, coordination, transport/access and overhead. These are planning judgments, not SF quotes, actual trial costs or a marginal La Casa budget. If loaded labor already includes an overhead item, remove that overlap. Costs cover all nine months and pre-discharge work. The favorable $2,500 presumes a substantially cheaper skilled delivery model; the pessimistic $9,000 allows higher labor/support burdens. Do not assume volunteers cost nothing.

This illustration charges a donor the full incremental advocacy package; no simultaneous public reimbursement for these same hours is assumed. Existing shelters, usual support and ordinary clinical treatment remain the comparator. If a public contract already funds the package, funding additionality falls toward zero; the grant does not buy the same course again. Clinical/other resources induced by better navigation may add cost rather than save it. A diagnostic central allowance of $500 for genuinely additional outside services per additional episode, plus $300 participant opportunity cost per funded unit, yields `resource proxy = 6000 + 300 + .5×500 = $6,550`, and **$72.08m/10 QALYs**. The $500 and $300 are judgments, not verified utilization or wages; this is an expanded resource scenario, not a complete societal ICER. No payer savings, housing asset values or tax transfers are credited as donor cash savings.

The FY2025 report's financial page gives **contract revenue $2,880,380**, total revenue/support $5,329,808 and expenses $4,525,216 for year ending June 30, 2025. Contract revenue is not automatically all government without the underlying notes. In contrast, [FY2024 audited statements](https://www.lacasa.org/s/La-Casa-Final-24-FS-1.pdf), printed page 5, explicitly report **government contracts/grants $3,239,538**, total revenue/support $5,766,608 and expenses $4,761,718. These are different fiscal periods. They establish material funded baseline, not a present gap or program price. The 2025 report says full 2024–25 audit is available on request; no outreach was undertaken. Neither reserves nor revenue growth/decline demonstrates marginal capacity.

## 5. Inspectable deterministic model

`Q = a × (E × b × Y × t − h)`

`donor USD per 10 QALYs = 10 × C / Q` when Q>0.

E = IES-point improvement; b = judgment utility per point; Y = effective benefit-years; t = clinical transfer/shrinkage; a = genuinely additional funding/access fraction; h = other incremental QALY harm per genuinely additional assigned episode. All benefit and harm receive a. Funding replacement and clinical transport are separate; no extra adherence factor. C is incurred for the funded package even if another payer would otherwise have paid.

| Conditional central | $6,000 | 7.27 | .001 | .5 | .5 | .5 | 0 | .00090875 | $66,024,759 |

| Clinical / utility null | $6,000 | 0 or b=0 | — | — | — | .5 | 0 | 0 | No finite positive ratio |
| Funding null | $6,000 | 7.27 | .001 | .5 | .5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | No finite positive ratio |
| Harm stress | $6,000 | 7.27 | .001 | .5 | .5 | .5 | .003 | −.00059125 | Net harm, not a negative-price bargain |

Central arithmetic: `7.27×.001×.5×.5×.5=.00090875`; `60000/.00090875=$66,024,759.28`. Expanded-resource diagnostic: `65500/.00090875=$72,077,028.89`. Favorable and pessimistic E values use symptom-confidence endpoints, but the whole rows are **not confidence intervals**: their utility, costs, timing and additionality are combined judgments. h=.003 is an unmeasured sensitivity for adverse burden/displacement/privacy or safety consequences; no observed harm rate is claimed. Recurrent costs are already inside the nine-month package; continuing years would need new costs and evidence.

At C=$6,000 the $100,000/10-QALY target requires **.6 incremental QALY per assigned episode**, vastly above the central .00090875 and incompatible with simply rescaling a small symptom contrast over a few months. Even the favorable row does not reach the target. A lower ratio would need a materially different, evidenced health mechanism rather than adding children's lifetimes.

## 6. Acceptance / next steps

The appropriate classification is **conditional research model, local dose unverified**, not rejected organization and not current grant offer. The key next evidence is whether La Casa delivers an identifiable post-shelter follow-up protocol, its allocated hours and target population, its already-paid contract obligations and actual unmet demand. If its service instead matches the ten-week Community Advocacy Project, independently extract that trial and rebuild costs/outcomes; do not swap the label on this nine-month CTI model. If only broad drop-in contacts can be verified, retain this as a potential redesign scenario and do not publish a program-specific causal score.
