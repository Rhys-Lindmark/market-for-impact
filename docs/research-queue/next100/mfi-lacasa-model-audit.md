## Timing and utility: smallest model correction

No coefficient must automatically change, but the rationale for Y=.5 must change: it is an **assumed integrated trajectory from an endpoint-only symptom contrast**, not partial retention of a measured longitudinal average. Explicit example: a linear rise from zero to the endpoint level over six months, followed by that level for three months, yields .5 effective years within nine months. This example is an analyst trajectory, not trial observation. A linear rise across all nine months gives Y=.375 and central **$88,033,012.38/10Q**; present as an optional timing sensitivity, not a required empirical correction.

Favorable Y=.75 assumes immediate/full average benefit throughout nine months or some stronger interim benefit that offsets ramp-up. Call it an optimistic envelope. No extra post-treatment tail or second multiplication by .75 years belongs in the formula.

b=.001 utility/IES point is candidly judgmental; its central unattenuated utility difference .00727 is mathematically plausible but not validated. Clinical null, utility null and harm remain genuine alternatives. “A direct preference-based null” should be rewritten to “a zero preference-based benefit is plausible, although the study did not directly measure that outcome.” Avoid calling this a maternal-health estimate if eligibility does not require motherhood; use adult survivor mental-health symptoms.

Keep t for clinical transport/source reliability and a for funding-created access. Neither is supported by the share of revenue from contracts. No additional violence, depression, housing, child or mortality credit should be added to this symptom calculation without distinct causal endpoints and overlap checks.

Appropriate public title: “Potential structured post-shelter follow-up at La Casa: conditional CTI-like course scenario.” Do not title it as a measured existing program result, an available $6,000 package or the effectiveness of all drop-in counseling. It can be a transparent research report about a constructed course; it should not silently increase a count of verified existing protocol matches. Program cooperation, fidelity, safe transition criteria, available staff and restricted funding remain unresolved.

## Full-course cost and payer accounting

The full-course scale is appropriately priced, but **“fully loaded $60/hour” plus $1,080 supervision/overhead is internally ambiguous**. Smallest fix retaining $6,000: define $60 as salary/fringe or direct advocate labor cost, excluding every separately listed noncontact support category; make $1,080 the nonoverlapping remainder. Alternatively retain “fully loaded” and itemize only costs outside it. Do not assert double-counting is empirically proven—the cost is invented planning, not a ledger—but remove the ambiguity before implementation. If all $1,080 were removed, central would be $54,140,302.61; that is only a cost-boundary sensitivity, not the recommended new estimate.

Favorable $2,500 must still purchase the same planned course: across82 hours it is at most $30.49/hour before extra support. Call it a low-cost skilled-delivery assumption, not a shorter course. If volunteer time supplies the reduction, disclose the associated full-resource cost rather than treating it as free.

The comparator matters: the donor may fund a full alternative package while the clinical effect is incremental over usual support. That is permissible as a conservative gross cash scenario, but **not** an estimate of the incremental staffing cost of redesigning an already funded service. A later CTI adaptation offer could involve only training/redesign costs—or could merely replace already contracted work. Neither can be inferred here. Do not subtract assumed public reimbursement and also apply a replacement factor to the same already-incremental offer.

The $6,550 calculation is best labeled “expanded gross resource/expenditure diagnostic,” not a complete incremental societal ICER. The $300 participant-cost term needs to specify unpaid time and unreimbursed travel only, excluding transport already in donorC. Charging participant burden to all funded units while scaling outside services by a is a chosen conservative accounting convention, not verified counterfactual resource use. For fully identical care replacement, baseline patient time may cancel as well.

## Donor-useful bottom line

With this narrow, judgmentally mapped endpoint, the model does not approach $100,000/10Q. Threshold donor cost per episode is $9.0875 at central health assumptions or $181.11094 at favorable assumptions—far below the proposed full-course budgets. That does not establish low organizational value; it shows that an unverified CTI-like redesign plus an uncertain symptom-to-utility bridge cannot presently support a high-return QALY claim.

Recommended minimal acceptance: retain conditional central and all null/harm cases; correct endpoint timing and non-preference QoL wording; make cost components nonoverlapping; prominently label actual protocol/dose unverified. Do not convert source uncertainty into a false zero-effect certainty, or missing local match into a claim of existing delivery.
