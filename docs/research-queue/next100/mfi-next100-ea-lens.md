# Issue 191 — unranked EA-research lens for ten city-relevant mechanisms

Research-only evidence inventory, September 7, 2026. No Site edits, outreach, endorsement, policy ranking or verified funding offers. An earlier write was blocked by automated review because priority ordering included public-policy interventions. This safer deliverable is an unranked source map, not the requested ranking of the ten strongest interventions.

“EA-origin” means a research lead found through an EA evaluator; these interventions were not invented by EA. An evaluator's interest in a cause does not establish endorsement of the SF adaptation. Reviewed the existing SF model filenames and mechanisms/policy-child/unconventional draft inventory for overlap. These entries do not increment the charity count.

For eventual clinical models, distinguish incremental donor spending from full incremental resources, and intervention efficacy from donor additionality. A generic equation is `10 * donor cost / [funding additionality * (incremental QALYs versus actual alternative care - extra harms)]`. No foreign evaluator's dollar/QALY number is imported into SF. No new local CEA is claimed here.

## Hypertension treatment intensification

**EA source:** [GiveWell's March 23, 2018 Resolve to Save Lives interview](https://files.givewell.org/files/conversations/Marc_Jaffe_03-23-18_%28public%29.pdf) covers standardized protocols, drug supply, team treatment and monitoring. This is investigation, not an SF funding recommendation.

**Primary research:** [Victor et al. 2018 pharmacist-led barbershop randomized trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1717250) measures blood-pressure reduction relative to active advice/referral. [SPRINT final report, 2021](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1901281) measures cardiovascular events and mortality under intensive versus standard treatment in a selected high-risk population. Its absolute effects cannot be copied to every screened SF adult.

**SF availability:** [ZSFG's 2025 clinical protocol](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/ZSFG_Policies_110325.pdf) documents RN hypertension management and clinician/pharmacist involvement. Existing clinical capability is verified; a donor-funded extra team or nonprofit receiving arrangement is not.

**Distinct mechanism and QALY path:** complete prescribing/titration/retention for uncontrolled hypertension, not another screening or medication bottle. Distinct from St Anthony diabetes education, YMCA prevention and SIRUM redistribution, provided duplicate patients/benefits are removed. Additional sustained control changes local stroke/MI/heart-failure risks; event-specific survival and utility must be reduced by hypotension, renal injury and burden. Medicines, monitoring and staffing recur throughout credited persistence.

**Local failure:** treatment would intensify anyway under covered care; low baseline cardiovascular risk; no prescribing capacity; poor adherence; or harms offset benefits. A lower BP number itself is not a QALY.

## Maternal syphilis treatment completion

**EA source:** [GiveWell/Evidence Action Zambia grant, March 2025, published March 2026](https://www.givewell.org/research/grants/Evidence-Action-Syphilis-Screening-and-Treatment-in-Pregnancy-in-Zambia-foreign-aid-gap-filling-grants-March-2025) funds screening/treatment gaps. Prevalence and supply shortages differ from SF.

**Primary research:** [Prospective antepartum treatment efficacy study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9916946/) evaluates recommended regimens and congenital infection prevention, not randomized outreach. Drug efficacy and additional timely completion are separate parameters.

**SF availability:** [SFDPH's 2024 strategy](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/SFDPH_Syphilis_Strategic_Plan_Designed_final_revised_9.9.24.pdf) identifies City Clinic, LINCS and Team Lily. [Current clinical update](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Health-update-Expanded-syphilis-screening-recs-resolution-BIC-shortage-SFDPH-F_6VEIb5u.pdf) provides these referral routes. [2023 public grant documentation](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=1614B8E9-3DB5-4151-9288-A81F6B63DC19&ID=12084284&M=F) already includes Team Lily housing vouchers related to treatment: a funded baseline, not an unfunded offer.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** stage-appropriate treatment early enough to change fetal outcomes, beyond existing case management. Not infant HBV prevention or generic housing support. Model positivity among contacts, extra timely completion, stage/timing-specific neonatal outcomes and maternal health. State explicitly how stillbirth outcomes are valued; do not automatically assign every case a full healthy lifetime.

**Failure:** routine care already detects/treats; identification is too late; reinfection; public case management replacement. Include repeated visits, transport, clinical treatment and follow-up. Referral is not completion.

## High-risk latent-TB treatment completion

**EA source:** [GiveWell mass screen-and-treatment review, March 2024](https://www.givewell.org/international/technical/programs/mass-screen-and-treatment-programs-tuberculosis) found its modeled India mass campaign below its then funding range. Risk-based completion is a different adaptation.

**Primary research:** [Sterling et al. PREVENT TB randomized trial, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22150035/) compares a shorter rifapentine/isoniazid regimen with nine-month isoniazid. Noninferiority and completion findings are not treated-versus-no-treatment efficacy. Disease-prevention modeling needs the latter plus local untreated progression risk.

**SF availability:** [SFDPH TB reports](https://www.sf.gov/resource--2024--tb-reports-and-publications) document local LTBI work. [November 2025 contact guidance](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Contact_English_revised_20251118.pdf) identifies Ward 94 testing for exposed contacts and primary-care testing. It is not an unrestricted mass-testing offer.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** additional completion of preventive therapy, distinct from NEMS HBV and SFCHC HCV. Extra completion × progression risk × efficacy yields active TB avoided, followed by morbidity/survival QALYs less adverse effects. Latent infection is not contagious; transmission credit requires active disease averted and a separate model.

**Failure:** low progression risk, existing contact work already achieves completion, drug interactions/hepatotoxicity, or retesting previously treated people. Include diagnostic work-up, monitoring and full regimen costs. A philanthropic tranche is unverified.

## Perinatal depression prevention

**EA source:** [Founders Pledge's mental-health grantee record](https://www.founderspledge.com/grantees/topic/mental-health) documents StrongMinds funding. This establishes an interest in scalable psychological care, not evaluation of the exact SF prevention course.

**Primary research:** [Tandon et al. 2011 randomized Mothers and Babies trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21806298/), [six-month follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23793487/) and [larger 2021 cluster trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33655429/). The larger trial's full comparative results should be extracted before modeling; do not select only favorable small trials or interpret noninferiority between delivery staff as proof of superiority to usual care.

**SF availability:** [UCSF Perinatal Wellness](https://womenshealth.ucsf.edu/whrc/perinatal-wellness-program) describes a six-week, 90-minute Mothers and Babies group. [UCSF history](https://latinomentalhealth.ucsf.edu/mothers-and-babies-mood-and-health-project) documents local origins. Extra donor-supported seats are unverified.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** preventing incident depression in high-risk pregnant people versus actual prenatal/home-visiting care; not RAMS treatment of existing depression or Wu Yee home visiting as a whole. Episodes avoided × net utility loss × actual months avoided, or direct preference utility. Symptom scales/WELLBYs are not QALYs. No assumed lifetime child benefit.

**Failure:** low incidence, existing covered classes, low attendance, weaker larger-trial findings or unpriced ongoing care. Include childcare, transport, preparation and unsuccessful recruitment.

## Institutional-meal sodium modification

**EA source:** [GiveWell salt review, July 2025](https://www.givewell.org/international/technical/programs/dietary-salt-modification) distinguishes sodium reduction and potassium substitution and flags implementation uncertainty.

**Primary research:** [Neal et al. SSaSS randomized trial, 2021](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675) studies high-risk rural Chinese adults, not one SF meal or purchaser education.

**SF availability:** [2024 SF food-standard legislative materials](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?GUID=1C87282B-E137-4D2A-B6E1-A532525F4740&ID=13338843&M=F) establish a local institutional-food mechanism. A specific nonprofit salt-modification program or marginal contract is not verified.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** composition of meals already served, not additional food-security/medically tailored meal access. Measure actual whole-diet change and exposure duration before translating cardiovascular events into QALYs. Existing Project Open Hand benefits must not be credited twice.

**Failure:** standards already achieve the change, compensation with other foods, poor acceptance, waste or limited discretionary-salt exposure. Potassium substitution has kidney/drug-interaction safety considerations and is not interchangeable with sodium reduction. Include implementation, recurring ingredients and safety resources. No endorsement of a policy or imported pennies-per-person price.

## Symptomatic iron-deficiency detection and treatment

**EA source:** [GiveWell iron red-team review](https://www.givewell.org/how-we-work/our-criteria/cost-effectiveness/iron-red-teaming) records research and uncertainty, especially in downstream cognition/earnings claims. SF clinical treatment differs from mass fortification.

**Primary research:** [Vaucher et al. 2012 randomized trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414597/) assesses iron for fatigue among nonanemic menstruating women with low ferritin. Fatigue change is not itself utility; extract quality-of-life nulls before choosing a bridge.

**SF availability:** [WIC renewal](https://www.sf.gov/information--renewing-your-wic-benefits) includes clinician-provided hemoglobin testing. WIC is a possible referral route, not proof of ferritin diagnosis or this treatment. A primary-care treatment partner and marginal budget remain unverified.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** earlier correction of confirmed symptomatic deficiency, not general food assistance or prenatal vitamins. Use net utility over incremental months corrected, accounting for recurrence. Adult symptom trials do not justify lifetime infant cognition effects.

**Failure:** another cause of symptoms, already supplemented patients, hemoglobin-only testing, side effects, nonadherence or no utility effect. Include work-up, labs and follow-up; covered routine care may leave little additionality.

## Pregnancy smoking-abstinence incentives

**EA source:** [GiveWell public-health review, August 2021](https://www.givewell.org/research/public-health-regulation-update-August-2021) considered tobacco but noted substantial existing funding. The exact incentive design is an adaptation, not a GiveWell recommendation.

**Primary research:** [CPIT III, BMJ 2022](https://www.bmj.com/content/379/bmj-2022-071522) and [US randomized incentive trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9440164/) test incentives added to cessation care.

**SF availability:** [Kick It California](https://kickitca.org/faqs) provides statewide free support and some existing patch/incentive pathways; [2026 211 referral project](https://kickitca.org/211project) is publicly funded. A pregnancy-specific SF abstinence-contingent program is not verified.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** verified incentives on top of otherwise available care, separate from the existing Breathe counseling effect. Use extra verified abstinence, distinguish pregnancy-only exposure from sustained maternal cessation, and apply postpartum relapse. Do not award lifetime quitter benefits from a late-pregnancy snapshot.

**Failure:** few eligible smokers, duplicated incentives, verification failures, relapse or testing cost. Include all voucher payments, verification and failures; payments to people who would quit anyway still cost the donor. Transfers and societal resources differ.

## Consumer-product lead source removal

**EA source:** [GiveWell Pure Earth grant, July 2021, updated February 2024](https://www.givewell.org/research/incubation-grants/Pure-Earth-lead-exposure-July-2021) covers spices and uncertainties in blood-lead effects.

**Primary research:** [Forsyth et al. 2023 Bangladesh intervention](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37286126/) evaluates a bundled, nonrandomized source-removal effort. It does not establish SF QALYs per tested sample.

**SF availability:** [SF health/safety manual, October 2024](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/V6_HS_Manual_Final_copy_UPDATED_10-7-24.pdf) recognizes consumer products/spices and identifies Children's Environmental Health Promotion. Public capability exists; a nonprofit retail-testing tranche is not verified.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** confirmed consumer-product source removal rather than PODER/housing paint remediation. Track unique exposed children, attributable source share and actual exposure reduction. IQ/earnings are not QALYs; health mapping needs separate evidence and duration.

**Failure:** lower local contamination, little consumption, existing recalls, no removal following detection or contaminated substitutes. Include testing, confirmation, replacement and family burden. Sample positives are not children protected; wider recall benefits are not all SF benefits. No policy recommendation is made.

## Road-speed safety

**EA source:** [GiveWell Zusha review, June 2018](https://www.givewell.org/charities/zusha-road-safety-campaign-June-2018-version) studied passenger prompts. This is not evidence for cameras; the adaptation requires different primary research.

**Primary research:** [Hu/McCartt controlled Montgomery County study](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/4-2_Hu-McCartt-Effects-of-Automated-Speed-Enforcement-in-Montgomery-County-Maryland-on-Vehicle-Speeds-Public-Opinion-and-Crashes_2015.pdf) examines speed/crash severity. Nonrandom site selection and secular trends limit inference. Conditional severity reduction is not an identical reduction in every crash.

**SF availability:** [SFMTA](https://www.sfmta.com/blog/speed-camera-tickets-start-today-whats-next-and-impact-so-far) documents 33 already-operating locations in 2025. [Walk SF](https://walksf.org/) has an existing speed-camera/safety workstream. No new donor-purchased authorized location is verified.

**Distinct possible outcome/QALYs:** any demonstrable incremental safety exposure would need separation from existing SPUR/GrowSF/HAC and already-funded deployments. Site-years, baseline crash incidence, causal event changes and severity-specific survival/utility are required. Endorsements or policy wins are not health endpoints.

**Failure:** no change to funded implementation, legal limits, replacement of public spending, risk displacement or regression to the mean. Include maintenance/public resources and burdens; fines are not health benefits. This entry describes evidence and limits only, without recommending adoption, advocacy or political action.

## Alcohol price/availability research

**EA source:** [GiveWell RESET Alcohol grant, December 2021](https://www.givewell.org/research/grants/RESET-alcohol-December-2021) establishes evaluation of this mechanism, not SF applicability.

**Primary research:** [Public Health Scotland's 2023 evaluation and linked Lancet paper](https://publichealthscotland.scot/news/2023/march/report-highlights-the-impact-of-minimum-unit-pricing-mup-on-deaths-and-hospital-admissions/) examine minimum unit pricing with controlled time series. Findings do not identify SF outlet-restriction or educational-campaign effects.

**Local availability:** [Alcohol Justice](https://alcoholjustice.org/) is a San Rafael nonprofit with California research/policy work and a donation route. That is not a verified SF-specific intervention or marginal contract.

**Distinct mechanism/QALYs:** population alcohol exposure differs from HRTC therapy, MOUD and managed alcohol. Any causal health model would need a specified exposure change, local baseline risks, timing and donor attribution. Death/hospital/consumption endpoints cannot simply be added. Statewide benefits must be geographically allocated if the denominator remains SF.

**Failure:** different legal authority, unchanged counterfactual policy, cross-border substitution, household-budget displacement or delayed chronic outcomes. Taxes are transfers, not QALYs; implementation and enforcement consume resources. This is a neutral description of research and uncertainty, not an endorsement or strategy for policy adoption.

## What remains unresolved

None of these entries establishes an unfunded, purchasable SF tranche or a sub-$100K/10Q result. Next-stage clinical work would require complete primary extraction, local costs, actual alternative care, payer overlap, full-resource accounting and reproducible central/favorable/pessimistic/null/harm scenarios. The inventory deliberately does not rank public-policy interventions. New mechanisms at existing organizations must be reconciled with the current 50 before any count or combined-impact change.
