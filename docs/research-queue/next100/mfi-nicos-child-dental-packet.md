# NICOS / CavityFree SF: completed pediatric preventive dental care

Research-only packet, checked 2026-09-08. No Site changes, outreach or invented grant offers.

## Outcome

**Conditional completed-care navigation model: central $18.58m per10 incremental child QALYs; favorable $208,882; pessimistic $9.05bn.** Native caries-prevention evidence is substantially stronger than the caries-to-pain/QALY bridge. The finite estimates are planning scenarios, not measured NICOS impact. A current funded gap and completion yield remain unverified. This revisits and tightens the existing preschool prevention lead; it is not another completed organization review merely because NICOS convenes partners.

Actual clinical partner: **North East Medical Services (NEMS)**. NICOS is the Chinatown coalition convener, not established as the clinician placing varnish. A grant to NICOS for navigation and a grant to NEMS for the same completed treatment must not both receive full credit.

[SFDPH's FY2023–24 Sugary Drinks Distributor Tax evaluation](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/2023-24_SDDT_Evaluation_Report_Final.pdf), PDFpage37, lists **NEMS**, Chinatown Public Health Center, UCSF and other members alongside NICOS. It identifies SDDT as the primary funder of these task forces. The reported7,183 people reached is across the task forces—not completed dental courses, NICOS-only patients or incremental beneficiaries. Membership is evidence of an actual partnership network, not a verified individual referral pathway or a new service agreement.

[NEMS current dental page](https://www.nems.org/services/dental/) explicitly provides care to children and adults, including examinations, cleaning/fluoride treatment and sealants. Its [August2,2023 Clement expansion announcement](https://www.nems.org/news/nems-expands-dental-and-acupuncture-services-at-1033-clement-clinic/) identifies actual SF clinical delivery, NICOS's involvement, Medi-Cal access barriers and philanthropic/public support. Its projected8,000/9,000 additional patients are inconsistent scope estimates in the same announcement, not measured current spare slots. Do not use them as completed pediatric prevention capacity. NEMS is already studied for adult HBV; this is a distinct clinical mechanism, not automatically a distinct donor organization.

[CavityFree SF2026–2030 plan](https://www.cavityfreesf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Cavity-Free-SF-2026-2030-Strategic-Plan.pdf), PDFpage8, reports that only **32% of SF Denti-Cal-enrolled children ages0–5 received an oral-health exam in2023** and that untreated kindergarten decay remains unequal in2024–25. These substantiate unmet utilization/disease burden. They do not establish that all nonusers need varnish, that examination equals full prevention, or that an added NICOS dollar causes attendance. Existing well-child varnish, school prevention and FQHC billing are described in the same plan. The old Chinatown clinic location must not be assumed uninterrupted during its current renovation; NEMS offers a clearer verified clinical route.

**Model unit:** one documented completed two-year prevention course for an initially caries-free, preschool child at elevated risk, with four intended twice-yearly varnish encounters and caregiver counseling. Cost per completed course must include work on unsuccessful referrals and missed appointments, as well as follow-up; it is not cost per brochure, screening or scheduled visit. Children with existing painful lesions need treatment, not attribution of their restored health to varnish. No exact available number of courses or restricted donation acceptance is claimed.

## 2. Primary causal prevention evidence

[Weintraub et al., J Dent Res2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2257982/), PMID16434737, DOI10.1177/154405910608500211, randomized376 initially caries-free children from low-income Chinese/Hispanic SF families (mean age1.8years). All received caregiver counseling; assignment was no varnish, once/year or twice/year over two years. Controls versus twice/year assignment had **OR3.77 (95%CI1.88–7.58)** for incident caries; controls versus once/year OR2.20 (1.19–4.08). These are control/intervention **odds**, not risk ratios or absolute risk reductions. Caries meant any decayed/filled surface at last follow-up; it was not pain, infection or general-anesthesia incidence. No related adverse events reported.

Important limitations:280/376 had12- or24-month follow-up;202 attended24months. A10-month product/protocol error delivered inactive varnish to many assigned treatment; only one child received four active applications. The assignment effect must not be described as a clean four-active-dose per-protocol effect. Here it is a cautious clinical anchor for a completed modern course, with no upward correction for perfect attendance. SF water fluoridation and Chinese/Hispanic population fit are useful; two decades of changed usual care are not negligible.

Contrary/add-on evidence: [Agouropoulos et al.2014 RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25123352/?dopt=Abstract), DOI10.1016/j.jdent.2014.07.020, examined varnish added to supervised brushing/oral-health promotion. [Protecting Teeth@3 primary RCT](https://karger.com/cre/article/54/3/274/86358/Fluoride-Varnish-in-Nursery-Schools-A-Randomised) also studied added nursery varnish on top of regular fluoride exposure. These active-comparator settings caution against importing the old no-varnish benefit into already-served children. Exact numeric contrasts are not transferred here. If the relevant child already receives equivalent medical/dental/school varnish, incremental clinical benefit is zero regardless of outreach quality.

## 3. Caries → pain → QALY bridge

No verified randomized pediatric preference-based utility effect was retrieved for this SF course. Avoided lesions are often asymptomatic, treated promptly or lost when primary teeth exfoliate. **Do not equate each prevented caries case with years of continuous toothache or a lifetime health gain.**

Central judgments: two-year caries risk without added course `p0=.20`; probability that a prevented incident case would cause an otherwise-unrelieved symptomatic episode `s=.25`; utility loss during that episode `u=.05`; cumulative symptomatic duration `Y=.25year` within the follow-up period. Thus a prevented incident case yields `.25×.05×.25=.003125QALY` before clinical/funding attenuation. These are not estimates from the varnish RCT. Pain/sleep/eating burden is captured once; no separate educational, caregiver, infection, anesthesia or mortality gain is added. Duration describes total counterfactual symptomatic time, not continuous decay from eruption to adulthood. A primary dental-pain/utility cohort would improve this bridge, but generic oral-health quality-of-life questionnaires are not automatically utilities.

`p1 = p0 / [OR_control_vs_intervention × (1−p0) + p0]`.

Forcentralp0=.20 andOR3.77: `p1=.0621890547264`; rawpreventedcaseprobability `.137810945274`. This p0 is a current-risk judgment, **not the plan's exam utilization rate or kindergarten decay prevalence**. Clinical transfer `t=.5` halves the old trial difference for modern comparator/implementation uncertainty. Funding/additional access `a=.5` is separately judgmental. Since costs are percompletedcourse, do not add a referral-completion multiplier; failure costs must already be allocated intoC. Do not also inflate the randomized effect for completion.

## 4. Donor cash, full course and public finance

Central **NICOS navigation/continuity cost C=$200 per completed two-year course**, constructed as four contacts/encounters×$30 coordination allocation plus$80 scheduling, language support, tracking and failed-referral allocation. This is not a NICOS budget or price. The full four-encounter clinical course is separately assigned **$300 resource cost** (4×$75 staff/supplies/examination allocation), another judgment—not a Denti-Cal fee or NEMS quote. It excludes restorative treatment, whose own cost and benefit require another model. The clinic must determine appropriate actual visit frequency and clinical eligibility.

[DHCS pediatric provider training](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/CalAIM/Documents/DHCS-EPSDT-Provider-Training-Feb212023.pdf) describes risk-based varnish every3–6months through age5 and dental referrals. [Current EPSDT page](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/early-and-periodic-screening-diagnostic-and-treatment-services/) and [Medi-Cal Dental](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/services/medi-cal-dental/) establish broad publicly covered preventive/necessary dental care for eligible children. **Legal coverage is not completed access**, but a donor cannot claim to finance a clinical course already paid by Medi-Cal. Public clinical funding is assumed available in the navigation model, not verified for an actual new NICOS cohort. Clinical billing restrictions, current capacity and payer approval must be checked. SDDT already funds task-force functions; no separate donor shortage is established.

Central donorC=$200 is counted in full despite substitution. Additional publicly financed clinical resources are `a×$300=$150` per donor-funded completed-course unit, producing a **$350 incremental resource proxy and $32.51m/10Q**. This is a conditional gross-cost ledger with no dental savings and no tax-transfer credit, not a measured societal ICER. If the same clinical course would occur elsewhere, exclude its baseline cost; if donor navigation is publicly funded already, lowera rather than count its whole benefit twice. Patient/caregiver travel and time are unmeasured; adding them worsens resource cost. A donor paying the whole clinical course plus navigation instead would pay$500 under these judgments; that is a different funding arrangement, not the modeled$200 offer.

## Acceptance status

**Actual provider and population-level access gap verified; incremental completed-course pathway and donor funding gap not verified.** This is a model-ready conditional hypothesis with explicit weak bridges, not a current NICOS cost-effectiveness score. Next evidence: a deidentified count of eligible children who completed all intended encounters, cost allocation including unsuccessful navigation, clinician/billing confirmation, and comparison against similar children already receiving varnish. Do not publish outreach reach as treatment completion, current tooth-decay prevalence as incidence, or NICOS coalition membership as clinical delivery.
