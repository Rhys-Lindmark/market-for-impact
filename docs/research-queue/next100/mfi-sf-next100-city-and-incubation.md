# Toward the next 100: ten transferable interventions and ten SF venture concepts

Research-only queue, 2026-09-07. These are **20 intervention hypotheses, not 20 verified new charities, funding offers, or completed cost-effectiveness estimates**. The first ten have primary experimental evidence from other settings; that does not mean their SF delivery or QALYs are proven. The second ten are hypothetical Charity Entrepreneurship–style ventures, not programs endorsed by or affiliated with Charity Entrepreneurship. Existing SF services are labeled separately. No organizations were contacted.

## Shared decision rule

Use `USD/10Q = 10 × incremental donor budget / donor-attributable incremental QALYs`. The target $100,000/10Q is **$10,000/QALY**, not a conventional $100,000/QALY threshold. Price the complete clinical pathway, recruitment, noncompletion, supervision, maintenance and evaluation. Report donor cash separately from insurer/government/volunteer resources. Financing additionality concerns displaced funding; clinical effect concerns actual usual care—do not discount the same counterfactual twice.

For direct trial QALYs: `Q = additional offers × trial incremental Q/offer × clinical transfer × funding additionality − incremental harms`. For intermediate endpoints, require an explicit validated or judgmental health bridge and a bounded horizon. No automatic lifetime benefit from a test, refill, symptom score or diagnosis delayed. Null and net-harm cases remain substantive. One patient and one clinical episode cannot be credited fully to several partners.

## A. Ten approaches tested elsewhere, with possible SF replication

### A1. Pharmacist prescribing in trusted community venues — Los Angeles

**Primary anchor:** [Victor et al., NEJM 2018](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1717250), 319 Black male patrons at 52 barbershops; pharmacist-led prescribing produced an adjusted **21.6 mmHg greater systolic reduction at six months** than an active barber education/referral comparator.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** contracted pharmacists embedded in willing trusted venues, targeting clinically confirmed uncontrolled hypertension. No SF barbershop program or participating venue verified here; a flyer or BP screening alone is not this intervention.

**Q pathway/cost:** additional treated person-years → sustained BP difference → locally calibrated absolute cardiovascular event differences → discounted event-specific QALY loss avoided, net adverse treatment effects. Pay pharmacists, labs, medicines and referral oversight; never credit full treatment effects to a donated cuff. **Disconfirmation/unknowns:** trial was a specific population and intensive prescribing model, not race as biological response. SF usual care, pharmacist authority, adherence and durability may erase the incremental effect. Need priced marginal staffing and actual eligible caseload.

### A2. Vacant-lot remediation for neighbors' mental health — Philadelphia

**Primary anchor:** [South et al., 2018 cluster RCT](https://www.fs.usda.gov/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2018/nrs_2018_south_001.pdf), 442 sampled adults around110 lot clusters. Greening reduced reported depression/worthlessness; the overall poor-mental-health endpoint was nonsignificant.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** permissioned neglected-parcel maintenance in genuinely comparable residential microareas, not general beautification or park advocacy. No SF landowner agreement identified.

**Q pathway/cost:** unique exposed residents × incremental preference-utility difference × measured duration, less relocation/displacement harms. A depression-item percentage is not depression diagnoses or QALYs. Price land access, soil safety, installation and recurring maintenance. **Falsifiers:** scarce comparable vacant lots, overlapping resident buffers, null generic utility, maintenance failure or benefits captured by people who move in rather than intended residents. Distinct from broad citywide environmental policy, but do not duplicate any existing greening review.

### A3. Earlier clinically indicated first-eye cataract surgery — Nottingham

**Primary anchor:** [Sach et al., 2007 randomized economic evaluation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17585002/), DOI10.1136/bjo.2007.118687, evaluates first-eye surgery in older women against deferred/no surgery over the trial horizon.

**Existing SF service:** [UCSF cataract care](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/care/conditions/cataracts) is real; a new charity-funded acceleration pathway is hypothetical. UCSF is an existing university health system, not a newly discovered nonprofit candidate.

**Q pathway/cost:** earlier surgery versus actual waiting time → direct trial utility integrated only until comparator catch-up. Retrieve exact incremental QALY table before numerical modeling. Pay navigation/transport plus any genuinely unfunded surgical resources. **Falsifiers:** public insurance already buys timely treatment; benefits from bilateral surgery or refractive glasses wrongly assigned to first-eye surgery; postoperative harms. This is not another PHC glasses model, and cannot inherit its utility coefficient.

### A4. Verified smoking-cessation incentives in pregnancy — UK maternity services

**Primary anchor:** [CPIT III, BMJ2022](https://www.bmj.com/content/379/bmj-2022-071522), biochemically verified nonsmoking **26.8% versus12.3%** with incentives added to existing stop-smoking services.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** voluntary, nonpunitive incentives alongside existing prenatal care and cessation support; no currently available SF donor-funded incentive course verified.

**Q pathway/cost:** incremental late-pregnancy quits → measured postpartum sustained quits → relapse-adjusted maternal health yield. Add infant outcomes only through separately supported attributable event differences; do not award a whole infant lifetime per maternal quitter. Price vouchers, verification, counseling and medication. **Falsifiers:** postpartum relapse, limited eligible local caseload, existing paid incentive programs, burdens or stigma. Overlaps Breathe cessation research: count as a distinct pregnancy/incentive mechanism, not a second general-cessation organization.

### A5. Integrated hepatitis C treatment in opioid/community clinics — Norway

**Primary anchor:** [INTRO-HCV, Fadnes et al., PLOS Medicine2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34061883/), DOI10.1371/journal.pmed.1003653: sustained virologic response among all assigned participants **83% integrated versus64% standard**, while rates among treatment initiators were much closer.

**Existing SF ecosystem:** [End Hep C SF](https://endhepcsf.org/) and [SFAF HCV services](https://www.sfaf.org/health-services/hepatitis-c-testing-treatment/) already exist. A marginal completion/navigation tranche is hypothetical, not a new elimination organization. City plans describe treatment as widely available with persistent access disparities.

**Q pathway/cost:** extra cures per offer × stage-specific cure-versus-untreated QALYs, accounting for reinfection, later usual-care cure and competing mortality. Price antivirals, labs and clinical staff even if a donor only funds peer coordination. **Falsifiers:** referral already succeeds, untreated people are inaccessible to this modality, or effective short-course care replaces existing care. No extra naloxone/MOUD survival credit for the same course.

### A6. Structured peer telephone support for high-risk postpartum depression — Ontario

**Primary anchor:** [Dennis et al., BMJ2009](https://www.bmj.com/content/338/bmj.a3064.abstract), elevated EPDS score at12weeks in **14% versus25%**; more than85% followed up.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** supervised, language-matched trained peers for consenting high-risk new parents, with clinical escalation. No named local additional funded program verified.

**Q pathway/cost:** prevented/worsened depression episodes × preference-based decrement × actual episode duration. EPDS threshold is a screening endpoint, not a diagnosis or utility. Price recruitment, training, supervision and safeguarding. **Falsifiers:** existing postpartum services provide the same support; benefit dissipates by later follow-up; severe illness requires clinical care. Count maternal health first; infant/family spillovers need separate evidence. Distinguish preventive peer support from existing clinical counseling reviews.

### A7. Digital CBT for insomnia with access support — Norway

**Primary anchor:** [2026 two-year randomized follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13186012/), DOI10.1016/j.lanepe.2026.101691, reports **.025 incremental QALYs (95%CI .010–.041)** versus online patient education. This is a direct health-utility candidate, unlike most symptom-only leads; detailed missing-data and licensing review remains necessary.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** clinically appropriate software access with limited digital/language support, not generic sleep-hygiene advice. No SF license price or operator identified.

**Q pathway/cost:** direct trial Q per offered course × transfer/additionality; do not multiply by another two years. Illustrative threshold only: .025×.5 transfer×.5 additionality=.00625Q permits at most **$62.50 donor cost** for $100K/10Q. This is not an estimated local cost or ranking. **Falsifiers:** licensing/support exceeds threshold, control already has equivalent digital CBT-I, poor adherence, inappropriate treatment for unrecognized sleep disorders. Do not treat societal productivity savings as donor revenue.

### A8. Minimal-resource home pulmonary rehabilitation — Melbourne

**Primary anchor:** [Holland et al., Thorax2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27672116/), DOI10.1136/thoraxjnl-2016-208514, randomized home versus center rehabilitation showed comparable short-term outcomes.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** a clinician-supervised home option for COPD patients who otherwise cannot attend—not a pamphlet or general exercise class.

**Q pathway/cost:** access-induced rehabilitation completion × QALYs versus no accessible rehabilitation. **Important:** equivalence versus center care does not establish a positive health increment when merely substituting home for center. In that case the central health difference may be zero and the value resource savings. Price assessment, follow-up, exercise safety and maintenance. **Falsifiers:** no marginal access improvement, loss of benefits after treatment, publicly reimbursed alternatives, medical contraindications. Distinct from Breathe's asthma home visits and Brightline filters.

### A9. Person-directed occupational therapy, nursing and home modification — Baltimore CAPABLE

**Primary anchor:** [Szanton et al., JAMA Internal Medicine2019](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2720136), randomized multicomponent intervention reduced disability scores at five months versus attention control.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** a complete functional-goal course for low-income older adults, with clinical assessment and home work. Do not assert RTSF or Openhouse currently delivers CAPABLE.

**Q pathway/cost:** direct preference utility if obtainable; otherwise validated ADL-state utility difference integrated over demonstrated duration. Price occupational therapist, nurse and modification contractor together. **Falsifiers:** ADL gain not generic utility, no durability, housing permission and existing home-care overlap. **Duplicate flag:** related to RTSF falls/repair research; only count a new program review after explicitly separating whole-course goals and patient cohorts—not as a new organization.

### A10. Supervised lay-delivered Problem Management Plus — Peshawar

**Primary anchor:** [Rahman et al., JAMA2016](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2583388), 346 adults with distress in a conflict-affected setting, brief problem solving/behavioral activation/social-support/stress-management package versus enhanced usual care; symptoms improved at three months.

**Hypothetical SF replication:** supervised multilingual lay support for a specifically eligible underserved group, with escalation for severe mental illness. Not a claim that migrant background alone predicts treatment response.

**Q pathway/cost:** mapped or measured preference utility over short follow-up, with cultural/modality transfer; price supervision, screening and training. **Falsifiers:** different clinical context, ineffective translated delivery, specialist needs, US staff costs. **Duplicate flag:** overlaps generic therapy mechanisms already reviewed; would require a genuinely distinct task-sharing delivery question, not cloning RAMS/HRTC QALYs.

## B. Ten hypothetical SF Charity Entrepreneurship–style concepts

Each name below is a working concept, **not an existing organization**. Incubation funding would first purchase validation and a pilot, not established delivery QALYs. Budget setup and failed pilots explicitly rather than omitting them from eventual cost-effectiveness.

### B1. FIT-to-finish: close the entire colorectal-screening loop

**Anchor:** [STOP CRC2018 cluster RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30083752/) of EHR-triggered mailed FIT, reminders and implementation support in26 Oregon/California clinics. **Concept:** consent-based registry outreach plus navigation through diagnostic colonoscopy after a positive result. **Q pathway:** extra completed screening sequences × modeled stage-specific benefit minus false-positive/procedure harms; no lifetime benefit per mailed kit. **Disconfirmation/unknowns:** mailing without colonoscopy capacity may add anxiety, not health; [a randomized incentive study](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2728631) found no additional FIT return from incentives. Existing SF clinic screening, payer coverage, diagnostic slots and marginal gap unverified. Cost the whole loop, not postage alone.

### B2. Cervical-screening access and abnormal-result resolution

**Anchor:** [STEP randomized mailed HPV self-sampling trial, JAMA2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2812323), direct mailing increased screening by more than14 percentage points compared with education. **Concept:** culturally appropriate outreach for genuinely overdue eligible patients, authorized test access and tracked follow-up. **Q pathway:** extra completed screening-to-treatment pathways → cervical-disease model net harms. **Falsifiers:** most recipients already screened, low kit returns, no colposcopy or treatment completion; opt-in is not the same intervention as direct mail. Verify current US test authorizations, setting requirements and clinical guidelines before design; no recommendation for unsupervised unapproved testing. Operator, licensing and funding gap unknown.

### B3. Short-course latent-TB treatment completion service

**Anchor:** [PREVENT TB, NEJM2011](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1104875), randomized short-course versus nine-month regimen, completion **82.1% versus69.0%**. **Existing SF service:** [SFDPH TB prevention and clinical services](https://www.sf.gov/tuberculosis-resources-for-health-professionals); government service, not a nonprofit candidate. **Concept:** missed-visit recovery/transport/language support for clinically selected patients beyond current funded support. **Q pathway:** incremental completed appropriate courses × individual untreated progression risk × efficacy × disease QALY loss avoided, net toxicity. **Falsifiers:** short-course therapy is already standard; regimen trial difference is not the outreach effect. Exclude low-risk indiscriminate testing; reconcile drug interactions, public contracts and unique patients.

### B4. PrEP continuity rather than another awareness campaign

**Anchor:** [iPrEx, NEJM2010](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1011205) established prevention efficacy; historical assigned-arm reduction44% includes adherence. **Existing SF service:** [SFAF Magnet PrEP/PEP](https://resources.sfaf.org/services/prep-pep/) already provides navigation and options. **Concept:** close specific insurance, lab or follow-up interruptions for patients choosing PrEP—not create a duplicate clinic. **Q pathway:** extra appropriately protected person-years × current counterfactual incidence × net effectiveness × contemporary HIV QALY burden. **Falsifiers:** same care obtained anyway, changed exposure risk, outdated historical incidence, double adherence discount. Costs include labs, clinician and medicine regardless of who pays. This is a new mechanism review at an existing organization, not another nonprofit toward100.

### B5. Congenital-syphilis treatment-course completion

**Anchor:** [CDC primary2022 surveillance analysis, MMWR2023](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7246e1.htm):88% of congenital-syphilis cases involved missed timely testing or adequate treatment. This is descriptive preventability analysis, **not88% efficacy for a navigator**. **Concept:** clinician-connected rapid outreach, transport and course completion for identified pregnant patients. **Q pathway:** additional timely adequate maternal treatments × attributable prevented fetal/infant disease outcomes, modeled with explicit pregnancy and health-state conventions. **Falsifiers:** small reachable caseload, already active public outreach, inadequate treatment timing or reinfection. Clinical partner, medication availability and exact SF gaps need verification; no automatic full lifetime per treated pregnancy.

### B6. Anticoagulation continuity for eligible atrial-fibrillation patients

**Anchor:** [IMPACT-AF2017 cluster RCT](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28859942/) tested a multifaceted intervention to improve anticoagulant use. **Concept:** clinician-led reconciliation and refill/access support for patients with an existing indication, not community agents prescribing anticoagulation. **Q pathway:** additional appropriately anticoagulated person-years × patient-specific absolute stroke reduction minus bleeding harm. **Falsifiers:** prescribing already high, nonuse clinically justified, little continuous exposure despite a prescription, costly medication subsidy. Current SF clinical partner and unfinanced gap unknown. Do not count all detected AF as preventable stroke or assign an uncalibrated lifetime event risk.

### B7. Post-fracture osteoporosis-treatment completion

**Anchor:** [HORIZON recurrent-fracture RCT, NEJM2007](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa074941), new clinical fractures8.6% versus13.9% with therapy versus placebo. **Concept:** a fracture-liaison completion service that gets eligible recent-fracture patients through assessment and chosen preventive treatment. **Q pathway:** extra completed courses × time-matched subsequent fractures avoided × fracture-specific QALY loss, net treatment harms. **Falsifiers:** intervention compared medicines, not navigation; current care already treats; contraindications or cost barriers persist. No separate survival credit initially even though the source also examined mortality. Overlap with falls programs requires separating fall prevention from secondary bone-health treatment and avoiding double credit for the same fracture.

### B8. CPAP restoration and sustained-use support

**Anchor:** [SAVE, NEJM2016](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1606599) improved sleepiness/quality-of-life measures but **did not reduce recurrent serious cardiovascular events**. **Concept:** repair, replacement consumables and safe setup for diagnosed patients whose prescribed equipment is unusable, not generalized sleep-apnea screening. **Q pathway:** additional effective-use months × preference-utility improvement, net burden; no inferred cardiovascular mortality. **Falsifiers:** device replacement already insurer-funded, poor acceptability, wrong diagnosis or no symptomatic gain. A repair service's effect must be measured, not copied from prescribing CPAP. Budget sanitation, clinical oversight, mask fitting, electricity and follow-up; no SF operator or marginal offer verified.

### B9. Postpartum hypertension follow-up with clinician titration

**Anchor:** [POP-HT, JAMA2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2811976), physician-optimized self-management lowered BP through nine months postpartum versus UK usual care. **Concept:** monitoring access plus rapid clinician response for eligible patients after discharge, not cuff distribution alone. **Q pathway:** measured short-run utility/events or cautiously modeled sustained BP-related risk; do not grant permanent cardiovascular prevention from nine-month change. **Falsifiers:** current SF obstetric teams already offer it, inadequate monitoring adherence, no clinician capacity, overtreatment. Distinct from A1 in population and care transition; merge if the eventual same program/cohort is used. All payer costs and clinical staffing required.

### B10. Sight-threatening diabetic-retinopathy treatment completion

**Anchor:** [NEI's primary-program evidence summary](https://www.nei.nih.gov/sites/default/files/nehep-pdfs/NEHEP_NEI_DRCR_Webinar_Advances_in_the_treatment_of_diabetic_retinopathy_FINAL_508.pdf) reports the Diabetic Retinopathy Study's reduction of severe vision loss with indicated laser. [NEI's randomized modern treatment comparison](https://www.nei.nih.gov/research-and-training/research-news/lucentis-effective-proliferative-diabetic-retinopathy) also shows different burdens/tradeoffs for injection versus laser. **Existing SF care:** [UCSF ophthalmology](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/care/services/ophthalmology-and-optometry) treats diabetic eye disease. **Concept:** resolve loss to follow-up after a sight-threatening finding, including transport and repeated treatment attendance. **Q pathway:** additional completed treatment × time-matched vision-loss prevention × vision-state utility, net procedure burden. **Falsifiers:** screening without treatment access; credit for both eyes counted as two independent lives; ordinary refraction confused with retinal disease. Treatment choice belongs to clinicians/patients. Verify current pathways, clinical eligibility and additionality before pricing.

## Research order and counting discipline

Start with **A7 digital insomnia** for a direct QALY endpoint and inspectable licensing cost; **A3 cataract acceleration** for a direct economic trial and measurable actual wait avoided; **A5 HCV integration** for a randomized access-to-cure effect and an established SF ecosystem. In the incubation set, first investigate whether actual unresolved SF care gaps exist for **B1/B2 screening completion, B3 LTBI, B5 congenital syphilis and B7 post-fracture treatment**. This is an evidence-development order, not a giving ranking.

For each lead, the next deliverable should specify: legal operator and residence scope; a confirmed additional service budget; actual no-gift care; primary endpoint and denominator; a central/favorable/pessimistic/null model; shared health/cost overlaps; and a stop condition. Reject or merge ideas when they merely relabel a first50 model. The research set can expand toward100 **program evaluations**, but it cannot honestly be described as100 distinct nonprofits without verified additional entities. None of the hypothetical venture names should appear as an existing charity or donation button.
