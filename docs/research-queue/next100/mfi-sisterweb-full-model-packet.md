# SisterWeb: conditional incremental community-doula cohort model

Research packet, 2026-09-08. No Site changes or outreach. **Central donor estimate: $361.94 million per 10 incremental maternal QALYs**, favorable $1.61m, pessimistic $48bn; null and harm plausible. This is a transparent conditional model of a narrow maternal recovery endpoint, not an estimate of every benefit of doula care and not a confirmed funding opportunity. The high ratio is a consequence of modest randomized average effects, short recovery duration and substantial course resources—not organizational size.

## 1. Precisely what the donation would buy

Constructed tranche: **20 additional offers of full pregnancy/birth/postpartum doula care for eligible, publicly insured, nulliparous, lower-risk SF residents**, enrolled by 27 weeks and planning SF delivery. Donor funding supports enrollment/coordination/mentorship and unreimbursed support needed to make these offers feasible; covered care is paid separately. Twenty is an analyst planning unit, not a public SisterWeb request or verified spare capacity. Use offers, not completers, because the clinical anchor is intention-to-treat. Restrict the modeled subgroup to the trial's clinical eligibility; do not apply it to all obstetric risk profiles.

Central donor budget: $30,000, or $1,500 per offered course. Half of those offers are assumed genuinely additional relative to existing community-doula access: ten additional offers in expectation. The other half represents replacement of care/funding that would have occurred anyway. Clinical transport is separately reduced by half; this is not a second attendance correction.

## 2. Actual SF implementation and donor identity

[Kindred Birth Companions (KBC)](https://www.sisterweb.org/kindred-birth-companions) is a current SisterWeb program serving Black families in SF. Its posted eligibility includes living and planning birth in SF, pregnancy no later than 27 weeks, and opting into prenatal, birth and postpartum support, with a nondiscrimination footnote. The page names current doulas but does **not** establish FTE, unfilled slots or cohort capacity. The modeled publicly insured/nulliparous/lower-risk subset is an analytical restriction, not a claim that KBC excludes other clients.

[SF Doula Hub](https://www.sisterweb.org/san-francisco-doula-hub) now has a primary program page, stronger than the earlier social-media-only documentation. It offers Medi-Cal/PAVE enrollment, credentialing, mentorship, client connections and business support for SF-serving community doulas. Hub administration is not itself a clinical doula course. This model requires documented linkage from such enablement to additional KBC-compatible offers; it does not assume every enrolled doula generates extra births.

[SisterWeb's home/donation page](https://www.sisterweb.org/) directs gifts to SisterWeb and describes no-cost SF doula care. It advertises FY2023–24 satisfaction and breastfeeding outcomes, not randomized comparisons. A program-specific restricted gift, current legal receipt/EIN and exact public/private funding allocation still require verification before a donation CTA or grant contract. The program exists; the modeled tranche is not yet secured.

## 3. Randomized anchor and exact arithmetic

[Mottl-Santiago et al., Health Equity 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10507922/), DOI 10.1089/heq.2022.0200, is the best retrieved pragmatic safety-net match. Publicly insured, nulliparous, lower-risk participants received usual maternity care with or without enhanced community-doula support. Usual care included substantial clinical, social-work, lactation and language services. Primary analysis: 367 participants.

Table 2: cesarean births **53/187 intervention versus 54/180 control**, OR .92 (95% CI .59–1.45). Exact unadjusted risk difference favoring intervention is `54/180 − 53/187 = .01657754010695184`; not a significant average effect. Postpartum depression was 30/161 versus 29/152, with adjusted OR .93 (.50–1.72). Preterm birth was 17/187 versus 13/180; NICU admissions 24/187 versus 22/180. No neonatal benefit is credited. Table 3's Black subgroup cesarean difference was `26/63 − 19/67 = .12911632314617388`, exploratory and statistically nonsignificant; favorable scenario only.

These numbers are trial findings, not SF outcomes. Primary incidence contrasts include trial nonattendance; do not multiply by a completion percentage. Avoid observational claims of large reductions in prematurity or cesarean risk as the central causal anchor. A small positive point estimate is not proof of benefit, and confidence intervals admit harm. The subgroup's relevance to KBC does not remove its imprecision or exploratory status.

The central model deliberately uses the overall randomized difference, shrunk by a .5 judgment transport factor. An alternative central centered exactly on zero is defensible; this packet supplies a finite best-guess **conditional on a small positive recovery effect**, with a conspicuous zero alternative. This is a deterministic scenario, not a posterior expected ICER; ratios become unstable near zero.

## 4. Maternal utility and duration: explicitly judgmental

No preference-based incremental QALY effect was measured in the retrieved doula RCT. Avoiding a medically unnecessary cesarean can plausibly reduce recovery burden, but a lower cesarean rate is not automatically a health improvement and clinically indicated surgery must not be discouraged.

Central bridge **qC=.01 maternal QALY per net cesarean avoided**, represented as average .10 utility improvement over .10 year (about 5.2 weeks). This is a judgment capturing short-term net recovery only, **not an empirically estimated cesarean utility effect**. It excludes long-term pelvic-floor differences, future pregnancies, infant health and mortality. Favorable .03 assumes a larger/longer burden; pessimistic .005 assumes smaller recovery differences. qC=0 is a required utility-null scenario.

Strong contrary utility evidence: [2024 Australian prospective postpartum EQ-5D-5L cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11457989/) followed 134 participants weekly for six weeks. Utility improved from about .70 at week one to .87 at week six, but mode of birth was not a significant predictor in its final analyses. Those overall improvements are spontaneous postpartum recovery, **not doula or avoided-cesarean gains**. The cohort excluded ICU admissions and had selection/geographic limitations; it neither establishes no causal effect nor validates .01. This is why the bridge remains judgment, bounded to weeks with an explicit null.

Do not import the dated utilities in [the 2010 external-cephalic-version economic model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2826287/) as new randomized doula QALYs. No separate depression benefit is added: the trial contrast is near zero and any physical/mental recovery valuation risks overlap. Birth satisfaction, confidence, breastfeeding initiation and Apgar scores are not standalone QALYs.

## 5. Dose, costs, payer overlap and capacity

[SisterWeb time-use study, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9989372/) describes the local model: intake, three prenatal visits, labor support, four postpartum visits and substantial extra contact. From February 2021 logs it extrapolated roughly **32 direct-support hours/client**, including average 12.25-hour labor support. The one-month pandemic sample involved eight doulas; travel, broader supervision and development were not included in that per-client estimate. It is not a current full-course time-and-motion audit. A cohort of doulas/mentors shares care; do not multiply all hours by every team member unless separately recorded. Historical staffing is not current capacity.

**Constructed cost assumption:** $1,500 donor enablement/unreimbursed support per offer (rough planning allocation: $600 coordination/recruitment/billing support, $600 mentorship/unreimbursed contact and contingency, $300 travel/access/general overhead). All are judgments, not SisterWeb prices. Separately allow **$2,000 per genuinely additional clinical offer** as a rough payer-financed clinical-resource proxy. A $3,500 combined completed-support envelope corresponds loosely to substantial direct labor plus support infrastructure; it is not a measured resource cost. Actual labor, cancellation, shared staff, cash timing and induced services need reconciliation. Do not treat a reimbursement tariff as exact economic cost or subtract it twice.

[DHCS current FFS billing chart](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/medi-cal-ffs-doula-billing-codes-chart/) lists TRI rates effective for eligible services from January 1, 2024: initial 90-minute visit $197.98; ordinary prenatal/postpartum visit $162.11; vaginal-delivery support $685.07; cesarean support $795.73; extended postpartum visit $486.36. These are doula-support payments, not obstetric-surgery fees. Intake plus seven ordinary visits plus vaginal support totals **$2,017.82** if all meet billing conditions. The $3,152.65 vaginal-delivery maximum requires all initial-recommendation services, including extended visits; do not automatically claim it for KBC's seven-visit historical pattern. Second-recommendation visits require additional eligibility. Managed-care contracting/claim collection and SisterWeb reimbursement are not established by a FFS table.

Central **incremental ledger for 20 offers**: $30,000 donor spending plus `.5 × 20 × $2,000 = $20,000` induced covered clinical resources, total $50,000 proxy incremental resources. The other ten courses would have been funded/delivered anyway and their baseline payer spending is excluded. Donor support is conservatively charged in full even when access is replaced. This ledger requires the $1,500 and $2,000 to be nonoverlapping cost components; if administrative payments are already included in clinical reimbursement, remove overlap. A gross all-20-course financed budget of $70,000 is **not** the same counterfactual incremental resource denominator. Patient time and additional obstetric downstream costs are unmeasured, not zero by definition; no claimed cesarean savings offsets the donor bill.

Current payer rules and [SF implementation research](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10995-022-03373-x) showing historical SFDPH support make funding substitution material. No verified present shortfall, rejected-claim volume, unfilled slot count or ability to add 20 offers was found. If staff/hospital access rather than money binds, the funding-additionality parameter may be zero. Never claim that the twenty-person planning unit is an available investment.

## 6. Formula and scenarios

`Q per funded offer = a × (ARR × t × qC − h)`

`Q cohort = N × Q per offer`; `donor $/10Q = 10 × N × C / Q cohort` for positive Q.

N=20; C=donor cost per offer; a=probability/fraction truly additional access; t=clinical transport/shrinkage conditional on extra access; qC=net recovery QALY per cesarean avoided; h=other attributable QALY harm per truly additional offer. The qC term already includes time: do **not** multiply it by years again. Both benefit and harm receive a.

The central 20-offer cohort yields **.0008288770053 QALY** on the selected endpoint. Its $50,000 incremental resource proxy implies **$603,225,806/10 QALYs**, before unmeasured patient/downstream resources. Neither $361.94m nor $603.23m is an organization-wide valuation. The harm scenario is a sensitivity assumption, not measured intervention injury; a signed negative ARR is another permissible harm scenario. Do not floor net Q at zero before reporting it.

The favorable row deliberately combines several optimistic assumptions and is not a confidence limit or the preferred KBC-specific estimate. Even it misses $100,000/10 QALYs substantially. More favorable results would require verified additional health endpoints, substantially cheaper enablement, or a far stronger clinical effect—not unbounded infant lifetime gains.

## 7. Model-ready handoff and remaining acceptance checks

Arithmetic and source/judgment distinctions are ready for independent audit. Clinical effect and utility can both be null. Local cohort, exact current grant budget, payer receipt, capacity, and donor restriction are **not accepted facts**. The meaningful donor takeaway is: promising service access and strong user-valued support do not yet translate into a compelling demonstrated donor-QALY claim. This narrow model favors no positive neonatal benefit; it does not assert that infant or experiential benefits are impossible.

Before implementation, confirm the cohort's eligibility and next-available slots, allocation of paid/unpaid labor, covered claim conditions and who funds each cost, and whether the funding changes care versus other available doulas. Do not report program-wide ratings using this conditional tranche. Preserve the null and the alternative zero-centered clinical judgment beside any finite central estimate.
