## Required small corrections

1. **Define the constructed tranche consistently with the trial or acknowledge timing transfer.** For strict matching, restrict to adults enrolled at 16–24 weeks, not merely at ≤27 weeks. Local ≤27-week eligibility is real but is broader. Name the hospital-embedded intervention's medical-legal support enhancement as an external-package mismatch; SisterWeb clinical benefit cannot be attributed specifically to Hub administration. The .5 transport judgment can cover this package/timing difference, but it is not an empirical correction.

2. **Replace “incremental resources” with “incremental donor-plus-payer expenditure proxy.”** The $2,000 component is calibrated to a payment tariff, not measured opportunity cost. The packet already admits this; the $603.23M headline should use the same precise label. A full-resource CEA would need actual staff, volunteer, travel and infrastructure inputs and counterfactual resource changes. Charging all donor dollars despite replacement is a conservative donor-spend convention, not proof that every dollar adds resource consumption.

3. **Make every qC scenario's time integral explicit.** Retain .01 central=.10 utility difference×.10 year. An inspectable favorable .03 example is .20×.15 year (7.8 weeks); pessimistic .005=.05×.10 year. These decompositions are judgments, not evidence. If the report caps every scenario at six weeks, .03 instead requires an average utility difference .26 over six weeks; label that demanding assumption. Positive ranges are sensitivity choices, not empirical utility bounds. Retain utility-null and signed-negative clinical scenarios.

4. **Correct or specify the postpartum utility endpoint.** The cohort abstract reports modeled utility .70 at week one and .85 at week six; do not state .87 without identifying a distinct unadjusted table estimate. No coefficient changes follow because this cohort is only contrary/context evidence. “No significant mode-of-birth association” is not evidence that a causal difference is exactly zero; adjustment for downstream birth-related variables can also obscure total effects.

## Primary checks and causal boundaries

[Mottl-Santiago et al., 2023, publisher full text](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/heq.2022.0200), published September 7, 2023: Table 2 confirms 53/187 versus 54/180 cesareans, and Table 3 confirms the exploratory Black subgroup's 19/67 versus 26/63. The packet's ARR arithmetic is correct. Trial recruitment was 16–24 weeks, age ≥18; intervention included medical-legal partnership support alongside doulas. The paper identifies the subgroup analysis as a priori exploratory, so do not call it post hoc. Overall and subgroup estimates remain nonsignificant. The overall point estimate is a permissible explicitly conditional calibration, not established benefit. Preserve the offer-based estimand: no second completion haircut. Funding additionality must refer to the donor causing an offer beyond the local counterfactual, not to another adjustment for uptake already embedded in ITT. Trial-specific usual care and local alternatives need comparison.

[Australian postpartum utility study, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11457989/): primary indexed abstract/full-text passages verified 134 participants, weekly EQ-5D-5L over six weeks, modeled .70→.85 recovery and nonsignificant mode-of-birth association. Overall recovery is not avoided-cesarean utility. This does not validate .01 or .03; no maternal mortality, neonatal lifetime, future-pregnancy or extra depression credit is supported here. Avoid describing every net cesarean reduction as removal of an unnecessary operation. Define qC as net health consequence under safe clinical decision-making, including recovery tradeoffs, and keep harm possible.

Direct PMC page access produced a browser challenge in this audit; trial verification used the primary publisher, and utility verification used indexed primary-page passages. No challenge was bypassed.

## Payer and local delivery audit

[DHCS current FFS chart](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/medi-cal-ffs-doula-billing-codes-chart/) independently confirms the listed rates. $197.98 + 7×$162.11 + $685.07 = **$2,017.82**. $3,152.65 is the maximum initial-recommendation vaginal-birth package, not automatically the historical KBC course. These are doula service payments, not surgery charges; the packet correctly avoids claiming cesarean hospital savings for donors. Current FFS rates do not establish managed-care collection, SisterWeb receipt, marginal eligibility, or a present shortfall.

[KBC current page](https://www.sisterweb.org/kindred-birth-companions) verifies SF residence/birth, ≤27-week eligibility, full-support opt-in and the nondiscrimination footnote. It does not verify the proposed 20-offer tranche. The Hub URL returned an internal error during this audit; the provider's [LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/sisterweb) corroborates enrollment, credentialing and mentorship support. Preserve the original author's successful primary-page read as its own provenance; do not claim independent re-verification here.

[SisterWeb time-use study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9989372/) supports approximately 32 hours of client care per standard course and substantial work outside direct visits. It does not establish a current $1,500 marginal enablement quote. The proposed $1,500 support and $2,000 payment envelopes must cover nonoverlapping work. Billing support and overhead may already be financed inside clinical receipts; an overlap is possible but not established quantitatively. Do not automatically subtract the entire reimbursement from $1,500, since that amount was already defined net of separately covered clinical work.

The simple `N*C + N*a*P` ledger is coherent only as its declared hypothetical counterfactual: donor outlays cover all offers, and payer outlays increase only for genuinely additional access. If a gift only substitutes funding or redirects clients between providers, map those payer/resource changes separately. No published data identifies a=.5 or a=.8. Neither public funding presence nor open intake proves those values or a positive funding gap.

## Donor-useful bottom line

Accept as a transparent conditional research scenario after these wording/scope fixes. There is no supported path to the $100,000/10-QALY threshold using this narrow recovery endpoint and the stated costs: central assumptions allow only **$0.41444 donor cost per offered course** at that threshold; even the favorable joint assumptions allow **$30.98792**, versus the packet's $500. These are threshold tests, not feasible provider prices.

The useful conclusion is not “SisterWeb is worth little.” It is that avoided-cesarean recovery alone, with currently retrieved causal evidence, does not substantiate an inexpensive incremental-QALY giving claim. A donor considering birth equity, respectful care or access may value other outcomes; those should remain separately described until a valid incremental health bridge exists. Do not use unsupported infant lifetime gains to rescue this ratio. Current capacity, a restricted additional offer, and a reconciled payer/donor budget remain unverified.
