# Funding audit: SF central-ranking picks 17–20

Reviewed2026-09-08. Research only, no outreach or Site changes. Read current model files in `work/market-for-impact-large-bay-impact/data/san-francisco`; prior four briefs cover ranks1–16. Selected17 HYA,18 Five Keys,19 Openhouse,20 HRTC. Central prices identify exploratory models, not verified offers. **Verified marginal funding room remains unknown (`null`) for all four.** Existing service, general fundraising and funded expansion do not establish what an additional restricted gift buys.

## 17. Homeless Youth Alliance — supported buprenorphine initiation (~$4.687M/10 QALYs)

**Exact modeled unit:** a supported initiation at the collaborative on-site clinic, with central0.25 medication-covered years—not one referral, all services at the drop-in, or a year of prescriber care. Central$2,793.20 assumes ten service hours at a shared$279.32 budget benchmark, with unsuccessful engagement allocated over initiations. Public prescriber/pharmacy costs are excluded; additionality0.5 jointly allows alternative treatment access and finance replacement.

[HYA November27,2024 announcement](https://www.homelessyouthalliance.org/hya-is-taking-over-the-haight-ashbury-free-clinic/) verifies full-time occupation of the former Haight Ashbury Free Clinic, consolidating existing Monday/Wednesday/Friday all-age services and Tuesday/Thursday youth-only drop-in. It describes operational efficiency from less transport and broader access, but no remaining2026 expansion budget or additional medication starts. Up to280 people during a two-hour drop-in is neither a buprenorphine cohort nor capacity for280 new starts. [Current donation form](https://homelessyouthalliance.networkforgood.com/) is organizational giving; the inspected form does not identify a medication restriction, unit price or remaining gap.

**Most consequential overlap, shared with HRTC:** [May4,2026 SF Health Commission proposal](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/SFAF_-_1000037481_-_HC_Memo_May_2026.cleaned.pdf), CID1000037481, names HYA and HRTC as SFAF subcontractors. It proposes$5,111,593 annually,18,300 service hours, July1,2026–December31,2027, with a total$8,587,476 including contingency. Publicly financed services already include treatment-readiness counseling, immediate navigation, clinical counseling and medication storage/adherence. These are broad shared commitments, not an HYA-specific rate or available donor tranche. Budget-finalization language means execution/subaward allocation remains unverified. The$279.32/hour benchmark cannot establish that private donations add services rather than finance already contracted work.

**Evidence that could overturn0.5:** an executed subcontract already covering the proposed support hours; unused funded outreach capacity; no additional prescriber/pharmacy slots; or patients already accessing another low-threshold service. Conversely, a documented uncovered retention function with prescriber capacity could support positive room. Require HYA-specific staff/time allocation, actual fills/coverage days, incremental versus existing clients, payer responsibilities, and provider-accepted dated restricted budget. Do not sum full naloxone and medication mortality benefits for overlapping recipients.

## 18. Five Keys Independence High School — credential-producing education (~$4.902M/10 QALYs)

**Exact modeled unit:** an additional credential caused by one ADA-equivalent year, across the school—not a unique learner, SF-only seat or existing graduate. Historical FY2024 gross expense$37,213,618/2,232.74 annual ADA=$16,667.24; model assumes0.10 additional credentials per ADA-year and0.34 QALY per credential. The bridge contains **no separate numerical funding-additionality factor**: public displacement appears only in its null warning. Thus it is especially important not to describe the gross-cost estimate as a verified marginal donor price. The additional credential effect is itself judgmental.

[Current school FAQ](https://www.fivekeyscharter.org/school-faqs) advertises free, year-round open enrollment and in-person/online options. This is evidence of an available public education pathway, not of unfunded places or a tuition scholarship opportunity.

**Newer plan materially strengthens the overlap concern:** [FKIH2025–26 LCAP](https://www.fivekeyscharter.org/s/2526_INDH_LCAP.pdf), physicalpp1,4–6, reports mean enrollment durations30days in custody and60days in community, and P2 ADA2,484.28. These are not the historical model's annual ADA denominator. It describes already implemented weekly goal-setting/credit-progress meetings and CSI-funded extra teacher pay, team stipends, training and coaching for graduation improvement. This is a plausible narrower mechanism, but already publicly financed; it is not a new philanthropic expansion proposal. It reports improvement without a causal comparison. No dollar budget for an uncovered additional cohort was verified in this document.

[Official LCAP index](https://www.fivekeyscharter.org/local-control-and-accountability-plans) now links2025–26 plans, so2024–25 should not be called the latest plan. The official Donate link failed retrieval in this audit; no restricted FKIH/SF credential offer was verified. Other Five Keys housing, reentry or event fundraising cannot be assigned to this school model.

**Decisive next evidence:** SF-site-specific teaching/navigation constraint and incremental cost; charter LCFF/ADA revenue generated by the additional instruction; CSI and other grants already paying for graduation support; whether enrollment could expand without a gift; and a net-of-public-reimbursement private budget. Need continuation across custody release, actual dose and a credential counterfactual. Do not silently multiply the model by0.5 or change its historical denominator; a funding-adjusted donor model requires explicit new inputs. Fully replacement funding makes the donor effect null.

## 19. Openhouse — additional six-month Friendly Visitor match (~$5.333M/10 QALYs)

**Exact modeled unit:** one proposed new six-month companionship match. Central$1,000 assumes ten staff hours at$60 plus$400 screening, training, transport and administration; volunteer time remains an additional in-kind resource. Engagement0.75 and funding additionality0.5 are distinct judgments. Housing and therapy are excluded.

[Current volunteer page](https://www.openhousesf.org/volunteer) verifies active Friendly Visitor recruitment and ongoing social/emotional support. No numerical volunteer queue, trained reserve or number of unmatched elders is published. Money alone cannot guarantee an appropriate sustained pairing.

[Current giving page](https://give.openhousesf.org/campaign/722261/donate) associates a$100 gift with matching someone to a trained Friendly Visitor. This is more program-specific than a generic appeal, but gives no duration, full versus partial cost, marginal capacity, quantity available or enforceable restriction. **Do not replace the model's$1,000 six-month assumption with$100 as if the same service is quoted.** Ask whether this is a subsidized contribution toward already budgeted matching or full incremental cost.

[DAS July19,2023 grant memorandum and appendices](https://www.sfhsa.org/sites/default/files/media/document/2023-07/f_multiple_grantees_intergenerational_program.pdf), physicalpp2,45–54, already budgets Openhouse intergenerational work through June30,2027 at$270,381 annually, entirely General Fund in the program budget. FY2026–27 targets195unduplicated consumers and1,200programming hours; these are broader program targets, not new six-month matches. The budget includes$35,302 annually for Little Brothers Friends of the Elderly, plus staffing and administration. It is not a Friendly Visitor-only cost center, so exact match coverage requires reconciliation. Current amendments and payments were not verified.

**Evidence that could overturn0.5:** the next pairing already falls within funded targets/staff hours, volunteer availability is the binding constraint, or the same support is already provided by another partner. Request a separate match budget, current unpaired elders/qualified volunteers, staff capacity, covered grant obligations and gift restriction. Additionality should apply to genuinely added useful contact—not ordinary churn or existing pairs relabeled as new.

## 20. Harm Reduction Therapy Center — adult alcohol-focused therapy (~$5.548M/10 QALYs)

**Exact modeled unit:** a proposed analytic five-session alcohol-focused course within Adult Mobile Behavioral Health, not confirmation of local HaRT-A protocol delivery. Central$1,500 equals five one-hour contacts plus2.5hours engagement/missed appointments/coordination at about$200/hour. It includes failures and shared overhead; no second blanket overhead should be added. Additionality0.5 includes alternative treatment and finance replacement.

[November5,2024 Health Commission report](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/HC_Monthly_November_Report_FINAL_0.pdf), printedpp4–5, describes continuing adult mobile services and proposes a July2024–June2029 agreement: annual$899,823,4,530staff hours and500planned clients. Its$198.64/hour is an existing resource-budget benchmark, not a marginal therapy-course offer. It says services remain the same, with certification then pending external review.

[April17,2026 DPH planning inventory](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/DPH_Contracted_Programs_4-17-26.pdf), physicalp3, rows168–171, separately lists TAY treatment$210,180; treatment connections$150,000; STI work$43,242; and mobile behavioral health$899,823. The mobile line proposes a$20,000 July1 reduction under harm-reduction supply policy. That does not establish a$20,000 alcohol-therapy gap, nor final executed funding. The shared May2026 SFAF proposal above adds subcontract overlap that a prime-only ledger misses.

[August27,2026 newsletter](https://harmreductiontherapy.org/news/august-2026-newsletter-harm-reduction-therapy-isnt-what-you-think-it-is/) verifies current organizational activity and a general support appeal, not a dated incremental course plan. Its linked [Donorbox campaign](https://donorbox.org/harm-reduction-therapy-as-an-act-of-love) could not be retrieved; no restricted offer verified.

**Evidence that could overturn0.5:** mobile/SFAF budgets already covering the same therapist time, no spare clinician capacity or a modality mismatch with the external trial. Require executed budget and subaward crosswalk, staff hours by site/recipient, protocol/clinical indication, actual new course offers, and restricted private cost net of public coverage. Do not count clinical contacts or an organizational funding cut as added health. No priced next-gift tranche was found.

## Integration recommendation

Keep room amounts null, distinguish general giving from verified restricted offers, and label all four funding additionality claims unverified. Highest-priority corrections are Five Keys' already-funded graduation support and absent numerical donor adjustment; Openhouse's$100 partial/unspecified giving anchor; and joint HYA/HRTC public-subcontract coverage. A current service plan can show meaningful work while simultaneously weakening the assumption that the next donor dollar adds it.
