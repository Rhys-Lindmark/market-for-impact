## 1. San Francisco AIDS Foundation: additional targeted outreach

### Existing assumption and evidence

Model uses $20 per additional dose, explicitly a judgment about twice a historical proposed $250,000/26,016-target-dose bundle. Its 50% marginal-yield retention already jointly discounts displacement, harder-to-reach expansion and reporting mismatch. Do not add another arbitrary funding haircut without separating these components.

[Current SFAF service page](https://www.sfaf.org/health-services/overdose-prevention-response/) confirms distribution at 6th Street, mobile sites and Strut. Its 2025 figures are organization-reported outputs, not funded incremental capacity. [General donation form](https://donate.sfaf.org/campaign/773029/donate) was readable but supplies no targeted naloxone tranche or itemized expansion in retrieved content. Recipient is SFAF, EIN 94-2927405.

[May 4, 2026 Health Commission request, contract 1000037481](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/SFAF_-_1000037481_-_HC_Memo_May_2026.cleaned.pdf), pp1–3: proposed July 2026–December 2027 agreement $8,587,476 including contingency; base $7,667,389, annual base $5,111,593, 100% General Fund. Broad cost-reimbursement scope includes naloxone, clinical care and other services, with HYA, GLIDE and HRTC among subcontractors. Approval request explicitly remains subject to budget finalization. It is neither executed expenditure nor a naloxone-only price. No supersession crosswalk to the historical $250K anchor was verified.

### Counterweights and funding unknowns

[April 20, 2026 DPH budget proposal](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/042026_DPH_Budget_Update_Final.pdf), physical p18: proposes transition of outdoor supply distribution at SFAF Hemlock/Duboce and HRTC Victoria Manalo Draves Park, reallocation toward indoor clinical care, and maintenance of core overdose prevention. The associated $80K annual proposal is not a published private replacement offer. The separate $414K syringe-cleanup reduction is not a naloxone gap. Implementation/final disposition needs confirmation.

[SFAF CEO letter, August 27, 2026](https://www.sfaf.org/news/ceo-letter-updates-on-national-hiv-healthcare-changes/) reports successful local/state budget advocacy alongside federal funding threats; the aggregate HIV amounts are not this program's shortfall. [August 31 overdose-awareness statement](https://www.sfaf.org/news/on-international-overdose-awareness-day-san-francisco-advocates-say-awareness-is-not-enough/) requests broader service investment but provides no priced additional outreach plan.

Decisive next-gift evidence: dated additional shifts/sites and target population; executed contract/subcontract allocations; remaining restricted cash; staff availability; public naloxone supply commitment for additional volume; overlap with DOPE/other distributors; unique added reach and rescue counterfactual. Budget needs outreach/logistics/supervision plus any nonpublic procurement—not buying already publicly supplied stock. No quantified current unmet allocation verified.

## 2. Project Homeless Connect: prescription glasses

[Donation page](https://www.projecthomelessconnect.org/donate/) currently says PHC programs/events are privately funded and illustrates $100 providing two prescription pairs. Gifts route through fiscal sponsor Community Initiatives, EIN 94-3255070; checks designate PHC. This is a fundraising benchmark, not an incremental cost quotation, ring-fenced glasses offer or remaining program balance. The linked donation processor did not render for this audit; restriction options therefore remain unverified rather than asserted absent.

[Core Senses](https://www.projecthomelessconnect.org/programs/coresenses/) verifies monthly Optical Day and one prescription pair annually, with valid prescription required. It does not publish a glasses waiting-list count. Its stated dental waiting list and denture lottery must not be transferred to vision as evidence of unmet glasses demand.

Model central $100/completed dispense doubles the $50 benchmark, allocates unsuccessful referral effort across completions, and excludes other financed resources. Additionality 50% jointly represents alternative correction and replacement of funding. Do not multiply referral completion again if unit is already completed dispense.

[DHCS current vision benefits](https://www.dhcs.ca.gov/providers-partners/pharmacy-benefits-division-and-vision-care-program/medi-cal-vision-benefits/) covers routine exams/glasses every 24 months for full-scope members, with qualifying earlier replacement. Privately funded PHC operations do not imply recipients lack public alternatives; neither does coverage prove successful access.

Decisive next-gift evidence: available prescriptions and unfilled orders, actual laboratory invoices, optician/coordination costs and donated inputs, insurance payer reconciliation, funded versus unfunded appointments, written acceptance of a vision restriction and date-bounded additional dispense ceiling. Do not treat sponsor-wide accounts as PHC cost or cash room. Current model's unverified-room warning is appropriate.

## 3. GLIDE: rental assistance, not generic housing provision

[FY2024–25 report](https://www.glide.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/GLIDE-Impact-Report-2024-2025.pdf), physical PDF p7 / printed pp10–11, inspected by direct download and text extraction after browser size failure: identifies a major 2022 Wells Fargo investment, then a $100K/39-household example, including case management and housing-stability workshops. FY25 headline separately reports 317 people and $965,254 distributed for security/first-month rent. It describes both corporate money distributed by GLIDE and connections to Catholic Charities/Season of Sharing. These are not one matched fiscal/outcome cohort or current uncommitted funds.

**Concrete model correction:** historical $100K already supported case management/workshops. The 20% addition that produces $3,077 per case must be labeled an uncertain contingency/marginal-cost uplift or additional unaccounted-resource allowance, not automatically the cost of services absent from the source. It could double-count delivery costs; actual ledger needed. Also the 2022 launch date does not precisely date every dollar/outcome in the reported $100K cohort.

[Welcome Center](https://www.glide.org/programs/welcome-center/) confirms emergency rent/back-rent/deposit help and workshop requirement; case management availability is qualified. No current eligible-unfunded applicant count or dollar ceiling is provided. [Giving page](https://www.glide.org/give/) distinguishes Foundation social-service giving from Church giving; Foundation option is broad, not a rental-assistance restriction. Use the Foundation route for this model, not the separate Church option.

Decisive next-gift evidence: source-by-source case ledger, sponsor/public/partner commitments and balances, eligible denied cases, staff/landlord capacity, assistance type and household denominator, restricted gift acceptance and incremental awards over specified dates. A gift replacing Season of Sharing or another committed payer is not additional housing benefit. The old $100K cohort is not verified next-gift room.

## 4. Breathe California: proposed additional SF cessation course

[Ash Kickers](https://lungsrus.org/ash-kickers-smoking-cessation-program/) establishes six sessions and trained facilitators. Its end-of-course success claim is not six-month incremental quitting and supplies neither a course budget nor present SF enrollment slots. [SF directory](https://lungsrus.org/san-francisco-county/) lists Breathe California Golden Gate/Ash Kickers, but is not a dated cohort announcement. Concrete staleness signal: it labels Margo Sidener CEO while the [current staff page](https://lungsrus.org/our-staff/) lists Tanya Payyappilly as CEO and Margo as deputy. Staff page mentions a CTCP grant; scope/amount and whether it pays for cessation rather than tobacco-control work are unknown.

[Donation page](https://lungsrus.org/donate/) solicits broad lung-health support and gives the San Jose mailing address; [provider donation portal](https://apps.lungsrus.org/) identifies Breathe California of the Bay Area. This is not proof that a general gift pays for an SF Golden Gate cessation cohort. Resolve receiving legal entity, operating relationship, restricted accounting and delivery geography before publishing an actionable tranche.

Model's $200/enrollee is constructed: $72 facilitator preparation/delivery, $68 coordination, $60 space/materials/follow-up. The ten-person $2K course is not a quote or capacity reservation. Count empty places and failed enrollment effort; medication resources are excluded, not free. Funding additionality .5 is a judgment, separate from long-term health-yield transfer.

[Kick It California FAQ](https://kickitca.org/faqs/) confirms publicly funded free statewide coaching, multiple languages and modalities, including uninsured residents. Some adults qualify for a two-week patch kit; this is not universal full-course medication coverage. Additional Breathe quits must be measured against feasible access to this and other usual care, not no treatment.

Decisive next-gift evidence: named SF host/cohort and dates, staffing and recruitment commitments, signed all-in course budget, medication payer, existing grants/restrictions, otherwise-unserved demand and six-month abstinence follow-up. No public priced, restricted, unfunded SF course found. Readiness is lower than PHC: actual local marginal delivery itself remains unresolved.

## Implementation guidance

Keep donation links separate from “fund this modeled program.” Preserve null room and hypothetical quantity labels. Public budget proposals, old sponsored cohorts and giving-level examples belong in different evidence fields. A restricted gift can still replace planned private/public spending; restriction alone does not establish additionality. Conversely, absence of an online offer is not evidence of zero room. These four require clarification from providers before any funding-ready tranche label; this audit made no contact.
