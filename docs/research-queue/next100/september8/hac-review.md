# Housing Action Coalition: can developer assistance create enough additional housing health benefit?

Reviewed September 8, 2026. Conditional research model, version 1. Companion: `/private/tmp/mfi-hac-model.json`. Underlying brief independently accepted in `/private/tmp/mfi-hac-audit.md`; the scenarios are illustrative judgments, not measured organizational effectiveness or a confidence interval.

## Bottom line

The Developer Pathway Program is real. An additional SF project-year funded by a new donor is hypothetical. Our illustrative central donor estimate is **$15 million per 10 incremental QALYs**. Favorable and unfavorable stresses are **$80,000** and **$15 billion**, with explicit null and harm cases. The favorable result is not evidence that HAC currently offers a sub-$100,000 opportunity: it assumes a 100-home SF project, material completion leverage and a health effect not demonstrated for this cohort.

This is a narrower replacement for the previous broad HAC project-advocacy scenario, not another benefit stream to add to it. It does not establish that HAC's civic or housing work is ineffective. It establishes how much additional housing and health would need to be caused by this particular hypothetical gift.

## What is actually delivered?

HAC's [Developer Pathway Program](https://housingactioncoalition.org/dpp) offers a year of project-readiness and financial/entitlement technical support, with Onyx Equity Partners and Sobrato Family Foundation support. Its geography includes nine Bay Area counties and Sacramento. Applications are closed; the stated pilot selection cap is 15. Approximately five monthly engagement hours describe participant time, not provider labor. Standard participation costs $500, or $250 for eligible alumni; the advertised $12,000 service value is not an audited cost or donor quote. We model technical assistance only, not electoral activity or separate broad policy effects.

The [2026 collective](https://housingactioncoalition.org/2026dppcollective) includes an SF-based developer focused on low-density infill, alongside regional participants. A business location is not proof of an SF project. No inspected source establishes a specific SF address, net unit count, unfunded slot or completed project caused by DPP. Career production in biographies is not program output.

HAC has separate c3 (EIN 83-1881525) and c4 (EIN 82-1817943) recipients. Its [giving form](https://secure.qgiv.com/for/fosfhac/) says memberships default to c4 unless requested otherwise; its [homepage](https://housingactioncoalition.org/) distinguishes educational and advocacy arms. General giving exists, but neither a restricted SF DPP offer nor the accepting legal entity for this modeled scope is verified. Do not imply a c3 gift purchases all c4 work.

## What must the next dollar change?

The intervention is a complete **additional advisory-project-year**, including failed efforts. It is not merely paying a membership fee for someone already supported. The comparison includes existing Sobrato underwriting, Onyx advice, developer consultants, city implementation and other advocates. Replacing an existing participant requires crediting the net outcome difference, not the incoming participant's full project.

The model separates two questions: `p`, the difference in completion probability caused by extra support when delivered; and `a`, whether the gift expands that support instead of replacing funding. Completion probability already includes financing and competing assistance. Do not apply another realization probability or another generic causal-share multiplier. `H` counts net SF homes after replacement, demolition and offsetting construction, not applications, endorsements or entitlements.

## From homes to health

[Asquith, Mast and Reed's February 2020 working-paper summary](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/community-development/housing-and-neighborhoods/supply-shock-versus-demand-shock-the-local-effects-of-new-housing-in-low-income-areas) reports lower nearby rents following large new apartment buildings. That supports an intermediate affordability mechanism—not a DPP completion effect, a small-infill effect or an established health-utility conversion. We found no program-matched causal QALY estimate in the inspected sources. Supportive-housing or voucher effects cannot automatically be applied to new market housing.

The health bridge is explicit judgment: a small number of people per net home experience less unhealthy crowding or housing insecurity, with a finite net utility improvement. It does not count every new occupant as escaping homelessness, monetize rent savings as health, or award a lifetime of health to a home.

`Q = H × p × a × affected people per home × net annual utility change × discounted incremental health years − donor-specific health harm`.

Net homes account for lost housing; net utility accounts for shared displacement/construction and other health harms. These are different quantities, not two subtractions of the same health harm. Do not subtract shared harms a second time outside this bridge. A separate outside term is only for donor-specific health harm that can exist even when services are fully replaced; financial opportunity cost is not such a health term.

## Results and assumptions

Every input in this table is an analyst judgment, not a HAC outcome or price.

| Input or result | Central illustration | Favorable stress | Unfavorable stress |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Donor delivery budget | $15,000 | $7,500 | $30,000 |
| Conditional net SF homes H | 20 | 100 | 5 |
| Incremental completion probability p | .10 | .25 | .02 |
| Funding additionality a | .50 | .75 | .10 |
| Health-affected people per net home | .10 | .50 | .10 |
| Net annual utility change | .02 | .02 | .01 |
| Discounted incremental health years | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Gift-attributable net homes | 1 | 18.75 | .01 |
| Net QALYs per home | .01 | .05 | .002 |
| Incremental QALYs | .01 | .9375 | .00002 |
| Donor cost per 10 QALYs | **$15 million** | **$80,000** | **$15 billion** |

The central advisory allowance is 100 technical hours at $100 fully loaded, 40 coordination hours at $75, and $2,000 other operating cost. These are planning judgments, not provider staff measurements. Charge all attempted delivery, including failed projects. Favorable/unfavorable budgets halve/double that allowance. No participant fee is deducted. Recruitment or additional delivery expenses beyond the allowance must be added rather than treated as free.

The health horizon is finite and already discounted to the gift date. Five effective health years is neither a year of advisory delivery nor five calendar years immediately after donation. As an illustrative calendar translation only, a 3% annual discount rate and benefits beginning three years after the gift require about 5.96 subsequent years to produce five discounted years; benefits end about 8.96 years after the gift. Two discounted years correspond to about 2.26 subsequent years, ending at year 5.26. These durations are implied by the chosen exposure assumptions, not independently observed. Actual later completion or earlier counterfactual catch-up must shorten/re-discount exposure. Assistance that only accelerates construction receives benefits only before the other project would catch up.

At the central cost and .01 QALY per net home, the target requires **more than 150** gift-attributable net homes, versus the illustrative one. Favorable assumptions require more than 15 and posit 18.75. Reducing its project size from 100 to 20 yields $400,000/10; reducing funding additionality from .75 to .25 yields $240,000/10. The favorable 100-home scale is not established for the named SF-based participant.

## Null, harm and resources

Net benefit is zero if the gift substitutes existing funding, support does not change completion, no SF project results, or housing has no incremental health effect. Keep the donor cost and report null—not a negative cost-effectiveness bargain. A signed shared-health stress with central inputs and net utility -.004 gives -.002 QALYs. An implementation regression with zero funding additionality but .001 donor-specific QALY harm gives -.001 QALYs; this is a hypothetical stress, not an allegation of observed harm.

The numerator prices advisory delivery only. A **full-resource result is unpriced**, not zero: participant labor, net development/land opportunity costs, public resources, moving and operating costs remain unknown without a project. Distinguish financing and transfers from real resources and compare incremental costs against the same counterfactual. Private building capital is not automatically a donor expense, but cannot be described as a free social resource. No full-resource numerical comparison is claimed.

Count HAC/Onyx assistance and other advocates in one project ledger. Do not stack a general HAC advocacy model, credit all endorsed units, or add unmeasured electoral, climate, income or injury benefits.

## What would materially improve this estimate?

A dated SF project with net units, its binding bottleneck and no-new-support completion timeline; an actual additional advisory budget and correct recipient; evidence existing underwriting cannot provide that support; and a credible resident exposure/utility bridge. Outcomes must include failed projects and construction that happens anyway. The absence of a verified offer does not prohibit conditional research, but neither completion leverage nor health per home is empirically established here. Current evidence therefore supports a transparent low-confidence model, not a demonstrated high-yield funding recommendation.
