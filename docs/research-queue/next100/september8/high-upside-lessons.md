# Where an SF sub-$100K opportunity could survive scrutiny

8 September 2026. Synthesis of this block's existing research packets; no new broad search or new organization report. The threshold is **$10,000 per incremental QALY**, considerably stricter than common health-system cost-effectiveness thresholds. A model can give a conditional best estimate without a published marginal offer; uncertainty about an offer is not itself a reason to refuse modeling.

## Where cheap *additional* health could come from

The most credible structure is **a small, binding last-mile cost unlocking a complete effective intervention for a known patient whose usual care leaves meaningful health loss**. Examples worth testing are a cataract surgery subsidy backed by genuinely available clinical capacity; clearing an abnormal-FIT diagnostic/treatment bottleneck; or a specific administrative barrier to curing known untreated HCV. These are not claims that the needed low-cost tranche exists.

Two other structures may work but require evidence: preserving a proven large benefit cheaply when supplies or follow-up are the true reason treatment would otherwise lapse, and correcting a reversible functional loss that would genuinely persist for years without help. Neither screening alone nor the purchase of a device establishes those effects.

What most often destroys an apparent winner is the conjunction of **the wrong comparator and an incomplete cost boundary**: a whole-treatment effect is assigned to a small navigation gift, a temporary delay is treated as lifetime denial, or a donor price omits the clinical service that produces the benefit. Repeated multipliers do not repair a mismatched intervention. A second common failure is treating a surrogate—BP, particles, referrals, prescription fills, falls—as already integrated generic QALYs. A third is adding the same outcome under multiple partner names.

## Three falsifiable next-search rules

### 1. Search for the binding bottleneck, not another charity delivering the same broad service

Before a deep dive, identify a **named high-need untreated/undertreated cohort**, its actual next step, and the obstacle that a small extra resource changes. Obtain at least one program-specific basis for the all-offered cost or allocation and identify who finances the clinical inputs. Use `required net Q per offer = donor cost / 10,000` as the quick diagnostic.

**Pass:** a $200 complete additional-access course could plausibly produce >.02 net QALY with a program-matched mechanism and realistic delivery. **Falsifier:** ordinary care already contains that same navigation, the proposed $200 buys only a small unproven layer, or the complete course actually costs thousands. MNHC shows why an existing integrated baseline can erase the large imported access contrast. Missing a public offer alone is not the falsifier; lack of a plausible low-cost causal margin is.

**Next-search consequence:** prioritize surgery-slot underwriting and specific failure-resolution tasks over generic more counseling/navigation in an already integrated clinic. Stop a bounded check when realistic cost and effect scales cannot approach threshold; do not expand it into a full weak-org report to fill the count.

### 2. Ask when the counterfactual catches up before extending health gains

For each candidate, distinguish treatment **never obtained**, treatment **obtained later**, and treatment **already arranged**. Require an explicit finite time model: utility-level differences need integration; integrated QALYs must not be multiplied by the horizon again. Measure both-arm care timing and continued effective use where possible.

**Pass:** reliable evidence supports enough *incremental* untreated time to exceed the calculated break-even duration. **Falsifier:** the under-$100K result disappears when benefit ends at realistic usual-care catch-up, or depends on multiplying an endpoint by years of device life. Hearing's roughly 1.48 effective-year favorable threshold and cataract's one-year-versus-lifetime contrast are concrete tests, not targets to choose assumptions around. Preserve a longer-horizon stress when plausible, but do not rank it as the central case solely because it crosses.

### 3. Demand a complete causal package and one outcome ledger before celebrating low donor cash

Translate the program's actual outcome using matched causal evidence or a clearly labeled judgmental bridge. Show donor cash **and** a resource sensitivity, including clinical/lab/treatment inputs actually induced. Carry nulls and harms; treat financing replacement separately from clinical failure without applying the same adjustment twice. Assign each patient/course once across partners, and verify the receiving entity separately from clinical efficacy.

**Pass:** the cheap case survives reasonable treatment-resource, usual-care and health-bridge alternatives—or is transparently a donor-only opportunity supported by genuinely additional contributed resources. **Falsifier:** it requires treating waived charges as economic savings, screening as pharmacotherapy, a net payer ICER as the donation budget, or the same cataract/hearing benefit as independent outcomes at two nonprofits. OA's favorable donor/resource divergence and the PVF/Lions overlap show why both checks matter. Recipient problems should change routing/counting recommendations, not be misreported as proof the clinical mechanism fails.

1. A specific low-cash cataract support margin with actual denied/delayed-care duration and a single PVF/Lions payer split.
2. OA positive-FIT case mix, realistic alternative delay and downstream treatment, to test whether .20 net QALY/completion is credible rather than merely possible.
