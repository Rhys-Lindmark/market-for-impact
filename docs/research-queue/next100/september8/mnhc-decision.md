# Mission Neighborhood Health Center: HCV treatment gap check

2026-09-08. **Current treatment is more plausibly established than the original screen indicated, but no current RNA-positive untreated backlog or unusually cheap additional access mechanism was found. Deprioritize a full new report for now; this is not a finding that HCV treatment is ineffective.** Missing funding offer alone is not the stop reason. The reasons are weak evidence of an incremental clinical gap, an already-integrated baseline, and an unfavorable program-matched conditional screen.

## Identity and current service

Primary organizational disclosure identifies **Mission Area Health Associates, DBA Mission Neighborhood Health Center, EIN 94-2284365**. This is not Mission Neighborhood Centers (the early-childhood charity), San Francisco Community Health Center, or NEMS. https://www.mnhc.org/en/about-us/annual-statement . Current primary Clínica Esperanza page confirms HIV/HCV testing, medical care, case management, treatment-adherence support, integrated behavioral health and Spanish-language care at 240 Shotwell Street, SF; it accepts Medi-Cal/most insurance and assists enrollment. It does not explicitly call the treatment-adherence service HCV-specific. https://www.mnhc.org/en/services/hiv-services .

**Important positive correction:** the current CDC National Prevention Information Network Shotwell listing explicitly includes *Hepatitis C Treatment*, not only testing. https://npin.cdc.gov/organization/mission-neighborhood-health-center-2 . It is a currently accessible government directory, but a recent crawl is not a provider-verified service-update date. Its posted hours differ from the current provider page; use the latter for hours. Resource Center and Excelsior directory entries confirm testing but do not separately establish onsite HCV prescribing there. Thus the candidate is known-positive treatment access through the Shotwell pathway, not an assumed new HCV clinic at every MNHC site.

## Local outcome and backlog evidence: historical, not current

MNHC's primary 2015 conference abstract reports a multidisciplinary pilot begun April 2014. It described at least 91 active HCV-monoinfected patients and 35 with HCV/HIV (the source's second phrase contains an apparent mono-infection typo). By January 2015, 24 people enrolled, 22 had initial medical evaluation, 17 completed initial labs/ultrasound, 8 received treatment, 3 awaited authorization and 5 completed treatment with an end-of-treatment response. SVR was still pending. The authors said cost-effectiveness and sustainability remained undetermined. **Do not call the five cures, 126 a current untreated RNA-positive backlog, or 8/24 a causal incremental treatment rate.** Source: abstract 07, printed pages 6–7, March 3, 2015: https://www.forumresearch.org/storage/documents/annals-pdf/2015v16n1_fchr.pdf .

A 2016 provider presentation confirms the intended active-drug-use/marginal-housing population and integrated clinical approach. Its upload date is not its study date: https://www.sf.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/item_5d._packet_lbr-2015-16-011_mnhc.pdf . Current public sources searched did not provide HCV RNA-positive untreated counts, fibrosis distribution, current starts/SVR, waits, navigator caseload or a current HCV-specific marginal budget. This absence is not proof there is no unmet need.

## Counterfactual and funding

MNHC already has an integrated medical/case-management baseline. The large Eckhardt accessible-care versus facilitated-referral contrast (55/82 versus 19/83 cures within a year) is inappropriate for simply adding another navigator inside that baseline. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35285851/ . The organization is already publicly supported; HHS identifies Mission Area Health Associates DBA MNHC as an HRSA grantee. Its behavioral-health expansion award is **not an HCV grant** and cannot be treated as an HCV funding gap: https://taggs.hhs.gov/Detail/AwardDetail?arg_AwardNum=H8N53910&arg_ProgOfficeCode=153 .

The provider's insurance-enrollment support means usual payer access must remain in the comparator. A gift could pay otherwise-unreimbursed outreach, but could also replace existing clinic/contract funding. Medications, prescriber/lab visits and downstream care are not costless because an insurer or manufacturer pays. No current public source established how a gift changes a binding MNHC HCV spending or capacity limit. General MNHC giving is not a restricted HCV offer. Patients linked from SFAF/IFR/SFCHC must be deduplicated; do not credit the same cure to each referral organization. NEMS's HBV model is a different infection, but shared clinical staff/payer budgets still should not be independently claimed as free capacity.

## Program-matched conditional screen

If investigating further, define one **already RNA-positive, untreated HIV/HCV-coinfected person offered up to six months of additional care facilitation**. This is a hypothesized augmentation, not a verified new MNHC protocol. Restricting the denominator avoids counting antibody-positive cured people or negative screens. For HCV-monoinfection, transfer from the following trial is an additional uncertainty.

CTN-0064 randomized care facilitation versus usual care and improved care-continuum steps. The cure secondary outcome was only 6/41 versus 5/53 among RNA-positive people, difference **.05200184**; only 11 cures and uncertain follow-up make this a noisy anchor, not proven positive efficacy. No extra treatment-completion multiplier should be added to this already cure-based endpoint. https://academic.oup.com/ofid/article-abstract/8/8/ofab334/6310155 .

The trial's microcosting reports roughly $450 per participant-week observed and $110 at a hypothetical caseload of ten; the latter yields approximately $2,600 for six months. This is an efficient modeled staffing scenario, not observed MNHC cost. It includes outreach/supervision/overhead, and found no short-term healthcare cost offsets. Cost year is 2017 USD. Primary cost abstract and indexed author manuscript: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35042101/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9238179/ . Direct PMC page retrieval challenged; targeted primary indexed passages were inspected. Do not confuse this paper with the separate $6,035 integrated-clinic microcosting study.

Health reference: MONARCH's 40-year-old lifetime model yields .23–.64 discounted QALY per ten-point SVR improvement, algebraically 2.3–6.4 per additional cure for F0–F4. These are UK modeled cure-versus-no-cure benefits, not SF observed outcomes and not cure-now versus cure-next-year. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0117334 .

For a transparent screening calculation, use an **effective incremental QALY per additional one-year cure** of .5 central, 2 favorable and .05 pessimistic. These are explicit judgments after later usual-care cure, reinfection, competing mortality, fibrosis/age transfer and duration; they are not extracted source values. A full report would replace this lumped assumption with the existing HCV timing model or a clinical state model. Do not label it a measured utility.

Formula: `Q per offered person = (6/41 - 5/53) × clinical-effect transfer × funding additionality × effective incremental Q per cure`. `Donor $/10Q = 10 × all-offered facilitation cost / Q`.

| Favorable | $2,600 | 1 | .75 | 2 | .07800276 | $333K |
| Pessimistic positive | $6,000 | .25 | .25 | .05 | .000162506 | $369M |

Central cost is the external efficient $2,600 × analyst current/local multiplier 1.35; favorable retains old-dollar cost (optimistic, not inflation-adjusted); pessimistic is constructed. These are not confidence intervals, recommendations or MNHC prices. Costs apply to all offered people, including failures. Null: no incremental cure effect, immediate counterfactual cure or zero funding additionality. Negative clinical effect/independent implementation harms can produce net health loss. No transmission benefit or cost offset is credited.

This is a **facilitation donor-cost screen**, conditional on existing clinical/drug financing, not full-resource CEA. A complete resource numerator must add incremental assessment/prescribing/lab/DAA costs for additional starts (including failures), remove overlapping costs, and retain ordinary-care comparisons. Starts, not cures, drive drug spending. Missing payer price and incremental-start data prevent a defensible numeric full-resource total here. Adding positive clinical resource costs would worsen this gross screen; savings are unverified.

At favorable effect/additionality, a $2,600 course requires **>6.666 discounted incremental QALYs per induced cure** to cross $100K/10Q. That exceeds even the 6.4 reference for age-40 F4 before later-cure/reinfection adjustments. A radically cheaper targeted mechanism or a much larger incremental effect could change this, but neither was found. This is why a full new MNHC report is currently lower priority—not simply because no priced offer is public.

## Decision and reopening gate

Retain MNHC as a real HCV-treatment lead with current-directory corroboration. **Stop the full deep dive in this phase.** Reopen if primary evidence identifies a current known-RNA-positive untreated cohort and a specific low-cost barrier that differs from already-provided integrated support (e.g. a demonstrated administrative bottleneck, not generic more navigation). Then determine baseline eventual treatment, disease severity, cure rather than referral outcomes, all-offered cost and payer overlap. Do not fabricate an untreated backlog or transplant SFCHC's much larger integrated-care effect to make another row cross the target.
