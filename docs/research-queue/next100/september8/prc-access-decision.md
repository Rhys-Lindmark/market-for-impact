# PRC medical-expense EFA: bounded access decision

2026-09-08. **Stop a full ART-interruption cost-effectiveness report in this phase.** PRC genuinely funds medical needs for SF residents living with HIV, but the additional evidence supports a predominantly housing-funded emergency-assistance service, not an identified cohort facing prolonged, clinically consequential ART interruption. The stop decision is based on clinical/counterfactual mismatch, not merely lack of an advertised marginal offer. This is not a conclusion that EFA is ineffective or that every public benefit is accessible in practice.

## Actual service and eligibility: current versus obsolete

[Current EFA page](https://prcsf.org/emergency-financial-assistance/) verifies one-time support for low-income SF residents living with HIV/AIDS, including medical, dental, vision, hearing, housing and threatened utility disconnections. It currently specifies appointments only. The same page's **up-to-$1,000 / once-per-three-years rule belongs to Eviction Prevention**, not a medical grant cap. A medical payment maximum is not stated.

[Current eligibility page](https://prcsf.org/eligibility-access/) requires SF residence, HIV-positive documentation, income/residency evidence dated within six months, and the bill or relevant housing documentation. It says income limits depend on circumstances, not a single published medical-program threshold. Its walk-in application hours conflict with the dedicated EFA page's appointment-only notice; do not offer a guaranteed same-day or walk-in rescue pathway. PRC operates at170 9th Street and is EIN94-3078431. Former AIDS Emergency Fund is this program, not another charity.

The [brochure uploaded in2022](https://prcsf.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Emergency-Financial-Assistance-Brochure-ENG-2022.pdf) is internally dated **January2020**. It describes up to$500 for the first year, a $1,011 monthly individual income threshold, insurance premiums/uncovered medical bills, and vendor payments rather than unrestricted cash. These are historical rules; the upload year is not a 2022 policy date and the current pages do not verify those dollar limits for2026. Vendor payment is a documented mechanism, but current processing time and medical caps remain unknown.

## What is actually disbursed: do not substitute housing totals for ART cases

- [2024/25 impact report, printed p6](https://prcsf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/PRC_ImpactReport_2024-2025_11.19.25_website-final.pdf#page=6): $1.26M grants to1,297 clients;64% of funds supported permanent housing. The broad average is approximately$971/client, calculated from a rounded dollar numerator. It is neither cost per medical grant nor cost per interrupted treatment averted. Remaining36% is **not all medical**. The report's$500 example is illustrative, not an all-in medical price.
- [2022/23 report, printed p4](https://prcsf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PRC_ImpactReport_2022-2023_final_online.pdf#page=4):1,294 clients and$1,139,836 assistance; category chart indexed as65% temporary housing,10% permanent housing,13% telephone/utilities,6% medical and6% other. Thus approximately$68,390 medical spending in that historical year if the rounded6% applies to that grant total. It is not a current medical budget, ART-only spending, unique medical-client count or unspent grant opportunity. Do not apply6% to the2024/25 total.
- Current provider stories describe dental access and cancer-related financial relief. Neither supplies a documented ART prescription gap, delay duration, subsequent disease event or controlled health comparison. [Provider healthcare stories](https://prcsf.org/tag/healthcare/). A paid medical bill can concern care already received, so “medical expense” does not automatically mean additional treatment.

## Existing funding and care-access baseline

The SFDPH FY2024–25 objectives document, created July1,2025, explicitly lists **PRC-EFA, Ryan White Part A, CID09024**, and separately PRC-EAHP CID10913. This establishes a public EFA baseline rather than generic organizational public funding. [Official document PDF p43, printed sheet5](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/24-25_HIVHlthSvcsObjectives.pdf#page=43). It also identifies an existing NAHC HIV dental pathway requiring DPH Dental referral; do not presume all PRC dental needs lack a public alternative.

The [audited FY endedJune30,2025 federal-award schedule, printed p20/PDF p24](https://prcsf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/PRC_Consolidated_-Audited_-Financial_Statements_FY2024-25-_-Final.pdf#page=24), audited March31,2026, records four1000009024 lines: $449,994+$611,418+$192,029+$54,996 = **$1,308,437**. The matching CID supports an EFA-associated funding crosswalk, not a claim every dollar is a medical grant. Lines span award periods; their sum is the year's reported expenditure, not four newly available offers. It cannot be subtracted from the rounded$1.26M disbursement figure to infer a deficit or surplus. The audit's generic “2,000” annual EFA reach differs from the impact report's1,297; use neither as a medical-case denominator. Both relevant funding tables were visually inspected after browser screenshot retrieval failed.

PRC already has [Equal Access to Healthcare advocacy](https://prcsf.org/eligibility-access/) for Medi-Cal, Medicare, Covered California/private coverage and other programs. Its published healthcare-advocacy eligibility is not EFA's income rule. A hypothetical grant-assisted insurance correction must count the existing advocacy pathway as usual care, not assume no one helps without the new gift.

### ADAP is a particularly material substitute

[Current CDPH eligibility](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/pages/OA_adap_eligibility.aspx), checked2026-09-08, covers California residents diagnosed with HIV/AIDS, age18+, income up to600%FPL, not fully covered by Medi-Cal/another payer. Benefits include covered medications and insurance/Medicare premium assistance. Enrollment and renewal requirements can create real access friction; formal coverage does not prove timely receipt. But no PRC-specific residual failure rate was found.

[CDPH memorandum2026-01, January13,2026](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/ADAP-MM-2026-01.aspx) announces movement to an open formulary covering FDA-approved medicines except selected safety/cost exclusions, intended to reduce interruptions during transitions between ADAP and Medi-Cal. Therefore a claim that ADAP only covers HIV drugs, leaving all chronic-condition medicines for EFA, is outdated. The HTML has stale officials in its template; use the dated memorandum's operative content, corroborated by the [PBM-hosted original memo](https://cdph.primetherapeutics.com/cms/cdph/static-assets/documents/provider/news/ADAP%20MM%202026-01%20ADAP%20Open%20Formulary.pdf).

An [older June22,2017 emergency-access memo](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/CDPH%20Document%20Library/ADAP%20MM%202017-04%20Emergency%20Access%20Process%20ADA-ADA.pdf) documents expedited handling but excludes some long-expired/ineligible/TAP-expired clients. It is historical, not a verified2026 promise of one-hour access. The [current communications index](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/OA_adap_communications.aspx) lists later temporary-access and enrollment updates. Do not substitute the current **PrEP-AP** self-enrollment/temporary portal for HIV-positive ART access: that is a different prevention program and population.

## Clinical bridge and threshold: what survives

Plausible residual cases include a verified uncovered medicine, an unresolved insurance premium barrier or a delay in enrollment that actually prevents effective treatment. Actual EFA medical/dental services make such cases possible. The public material inspected does **not** quantify which cases are ART, whether dispensing would stop without payment, interruption duration, immune status, earlier/later alternative treatment, or treatment restored by EFA versus another source.

[SMART2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17135583/) compared planned episodic versus continuous ART and found3.3 versus1.3 opportunistic-disease-or-death events per100 person-years. This is not an effect of a brief payment delay, not mortality alone and not an annual benefit guaranteed by a one-time grant. No SMART-based QALY score is constructed here. The parallel clinical auditor is independently checking short-interruption evidence; no local window has been supplied to that auditor because none was found.

Keep the prior break-even test only: `Q = funding additionality a × additional treatment-gap prevention p × net QALYs q per prevented gap`; passing$100K/10Q requires `a*p*q > C/10,000`. At hypothetical all-offered C=$600,a=.5,p=.5, q must exceed **.24**. At C=$200 it must exceed **.08**. These are judgments/thresholds, not PRC outcomes or costs. A brief or clinically inconsequential gap can fall far below them; no current evidence justifies choosing a severe long gap to manufacture an attractive central case.

For any reopened model, allocate staff/assessment/failures to all offers, distinguish the paid bill from the medical resources it purchases, and add net induced clinical/medicine costs without double counting. Grant face value is a donor cost even when part is a transfer; a social-resource perspective is different. Payment substituting for an existing payer or paying for already-delivered care can have zero treatment increment; donor substitution and clinical counterfactual are separate discounts. Harms/displaced effective services should remain signed if supported. No infection-transmission, infant, housing or lifetime HIV benefits are added.

## Final disposition

**Do not commission a broad PRC full report or ART-access model now.** Preserve the real medical-payment service as a low-priority reserve. Reopen only if existing primary evidence identifies a clinically consequential treatment-specific cohort, its actual alternative timing and a reasonably priced barrier-removal unit. A conditional model does not require an advertised restricted offer, but it does require more than an urgent-bill label plus long-interruption efficacy. Dental/vision/hearing examples should use their own symptom/severity and timing evidence and must not duplicate the already-reviewed clinical-access providers.
