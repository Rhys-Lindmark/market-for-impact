# PRC HIV medication access: public-payer counterfactual audit

2026-09-08. Primary-source research only; not individualized eligibility advice. **No verified PRC untreated-ART duration, uncovered prescription count or marginal share was found.** A small-dollar emergency bridge remains possible, but published assistance categories do not demonstrate that ordinary grants buy otherwise-unavailable ART.

## Decisive current counterevidence

1. **Coverage is broader than older references suggest.** [CDPH memo2026-01, January13,2026](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/ADAP-MM-2026-01.aspx), primary indexed text, announces an open formulary: FDA-approved medications except selected safety/cost exclusions. It specifically includes treatment for common chronic conditions and aims to reduce interruptions at ADAP/Medi-Cal transitions. Do not assume hypertension, psychiatric or other non-HIV medication is outside ADAP merely because an older list omitted it. Verify the actual prescribed drug, client coverage and exclusions. The [primary communications index](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/OA_adap_communications.aspx) links later2026dental/OTC additions as well. Web templates show stale officeholder names; rely on dated memo text rather than page-template chronology.

2. **Current income references use600%FPL, not500%.** [CDPH memo2025-10](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/ADAP-MM-2025-10-Income-Policy-Exemption.aspx), effectiveAugust6,2025, discusses600%FPL and outdated prior-year income records. [California Senate February26,2026 budget hearing document](https://sbud.senate.ca.gov/system/files/2026-02/02.26.2026-calhhs-scdd-emsa-dcsd-cdph-dor-dcss-agenda.pdf) independently states the600%criterion. Some older/currently indexed pages still say500%; do not copy them uncritically. Core program is for California residents with HIV, adults18+, with applicable income and other-payer conditions; individual determinations belong to certified enrollment workers. A high prior-year tax return alone is not proof of current ineligibility.

3. **Missing documents need not interrupt dispensing.** [Official CDPH8728 TAP form, revisedJuly2024](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/CDPH%20Document%20Library/ControlledForms/cdph8728.pdf) grants approved new/re-enrolling applicants30days temporary ADAP eligibility while obtaining identification, residence, income and/or Medi-Cal application/determination documents. HIV evidence is required, with a positive rapid test and confirmatory test pending exception. This is conditional coverage, not guaranteed30days of free medication for every applicant. It directly falsifies the shortcut “30-day administrative delay means30days withoutART.”

4. **Emergency restoration exists, but do not promise an old service standard.** [CDPH2017-04 emergency-access memo](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/CDPH%20Document%20Library/ADAP%20MM%202017-04%20Emergency%20Access%20Process%20ADA-ADA.pdf), indexed primary page2, says resolution usually within one hour/complex requests one business day; excludes expired eligibility>30days, substantive ineligibility and expired TAP from that particular emergency process. It is historical, **not verified current SLA or proof nobody falls through**. Re-enrollment/exception processes must be checked before pricing private replacement. Do not confuse PrEP/PEP immediate-access portals with HIV-positive ART coverage.

5. **Other insurance assistance also substitutes for private cash.** [Covered California's official HIV/AIDS page](https://www.coveredca.com/support/before-you-buy/people-with-hiv-or-aids/) describes ADAP/OA-HIPP support and premium coordination. [Current CDPH branch page](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/Pages/OAadap.aspx) identifies OA-HIPP, employer-based and Medicare premium-payment programs. Being insured with copay/premium trouble is not automatically an uncovered patient. No unrestricted donor dollar should be credited with the entire publicly financed medication course.

## SF emergency financial assistance boundaries

[SF EMA2024 EFA standards document](https://sfhivplanningcouncil.org/FullCouncil/Presentations/2024/6.%20June/Emergency-Financial-Assistance-Standards-of-Care.pdf) was downloaded and **fully text-extracted**. It labels itself a **draft**; do not call it final binding current PRC policy. It describes one-time/short-term support including medications not financed by ADAP, requires evidence of urgent need and absent other funding, prohibits ongoing/annual payments and payments covered by ADAP/another payer, and defines a unit as a transaction—not a health outcome. These are RyanWhite-funded restrictions; do not automatically impose them on every private PRC fund, but shared baseline payer-last-resort logic remains relevant.

[PRC's actual EFA page](https://prcsf.org/emergency-financial-assistance/) and [2022–23 impact report](https://prcsf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/PRC_ImpactReport_2022-2023_final_online.pdf) include financial barriers/medications among several uses. They do not isolate antiretroviral purchases, viral suppression caused by grants or days without drug otherwise. Harvey owns provider/financial interpretation; this memo does not infer all EFA is publicly reimbursable or all “medical expense” isART.

## Potential residual mechanisms—hypotheses, not observed gaps

- Truly urgent pharmacy supply after existing medication is exhausted and a certified worker cannot restore public/manufacturer/clinic supply before a clinically meaningful gap. Must record actual denial and dispensing timeline, not just enrollment processing time.
- Enabling a specific funded prescription to reach the patient (transport/delivery, lost-device communication) when no existing support can do so. May cost less than buying drugs; a funded prescription does not establish adherence.
- An actually excluded necessary non-ART drug or care expense. Model that drug/condition separately, not with HIV survival gains. The2026 open formulary makes old exclusion assumptions especially unreliable.

Do not default to long-term untreated HIV for a temporary barrier. Private medication purchases may replace publicly paid fills; if bridge drugs are subsequently reimbursed, net donor cost must reflect refunds and admin rather than charge plus public cost twice. Existing PRC navigation/clinic assistance also belongs in the baseline.

## Model-ready denominators and stop rule

Track eligible aid offers → actual otherwise-unfilled prescribedART → incremental timely medication-days (subtract stock, pharmacy/clinic emergency supply and public coverage restoration) → actual ingestion/retention → incremental health over the residual untreated interval. Count costs of failed offers and administrative work. Do not add a generic referral-success factor if the health effect is already per delivered incremental course.

Administrative records needed: prescription/regimen; supply-on-hand and last fill; payment rejection reason/date; eligibility and temporary-access attempts; expected/actual alternative fill date; PRC payment/dispensing date; subsequent reimbursement; clinical status and observed adherence. No primary source here identifies the fraction of PRC applicants whose alternatives fail, so that fraction and duration must remain explicit uncertain assumptions with zero-gap/null scenarios.

A useful evidence of a **different** possible access bottleneck: [CDPH2026–27 May Revision](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CID/DOA/CDPH%20Document%20Library/2026-27-ADAP-May-Revision.pdf), indexedpage18, says SF jailADAP pilot had an approved enrollment site but lacked designated trained staff. This is not evidence jailpatients receive noART, not PRC's program, and not an authorized donor tranche. Do not transfer it into PRC's denominator.

**Conclusion:** retain conditional research only if provider evidence identifies a real residual medication-time gap. Current public evidence primarily strengthens substitution and short-gap counterfactuals. No defensible source-derived positive central additionality or30-day ART gap can be asserted from the inspected payer material alone.
