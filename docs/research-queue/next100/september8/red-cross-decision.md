# Red Cross targeted SF alarms: deeper decision gate

2026-09-08. **Do not advance to a full SF CEA or new organization count now.** Current actual SF delivery is better verified than in the initial screen, but neither exceptional absolute risk nor additional unfunded installations nor current full unit cost is established. The sub-$100k stress case does not survive as a credible central estimate. This is not evidence that smoke alarms are ineffective or the free program is saturated.

[Official2026 event page](https://www.redcross.org/local/california/northern-california-coastal/about-us/news-and-events/events/sound-the-alarm-northern-california-coastal.html), fully inspected: April3,2026,9am–3pm SF event at **Bayview Hill Gardens,1075LeConteAvenue,94124**. Volunteers canvass or attend scheduled appointments, with installer/educator/documenter roles and provided supplies. Sponsors are listed, including PG&E, Chevron and Arista. Event location is a staging/visit location, not proof every installed alarm was inside that property. The page does not report SF installed counts, missing-alarm prevalence, local queue or unmet budget. Do not assign a numerical risk premium to Bayview solely from its name or an “at-risk” description.

[Current regional program](https://www.redcross.org/local/california/northern-california-coastal/about-us/our-work/home-fire-campaign.html) reports1,128alarms/459households/1,502residents for2026 across the region. It tests existing alarms and offers up to three free installations when needed, subject to resources, prioritizing signature events. This verifies a funded baseline, but lacks an SF denominator, duplicate visits, eligible-unserved households or wait-time distribution. Neither “no room” nor “ample additional room” follows from these counts. The broad home-fire donation option includes preparation, response and recovery—not a verified SF-installation earmark.

The [property operator's primary page](https://www.mercyhousing.org/california/bayview-hill-gardens/) identifies Bayview Hill Gardens as Mercy Housing. [SFFD's current owner notice](https://sf-fire.org/annual-smoke-alarm-information-notice-building-owners) states landlords must promptly repair/replace nonworking alarms on request. Existing owner maintenance is therefore a material competing payer/counterfactual, not proof any particular owner failed or that every event installation substituted maintenance. Additionality requires whether the extra visit creates earlier functioning protection than landlord action, existing charity scheduling or other provision. The funding recipient remains American National Red Cross, not a separately incorporated SF alarm nonprofit.

## Empirical evidence, precisely bounded

[Istre et al.2014 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23873498/) examined Dallas36high-risk census tracts:8,134program houses versus24,346nonprogram houses. Teams installed alarms when residents were home and consented. Over mean5.2years, combined fatal/nonfatal injury rates were3.1versus9.6/100,000, unadjustedRR.32(95%CI.10–.84); demographic adjustment gave63%lower rates. Differences declined after year6. **Nonrandom acceptance/access and residual confounding remain.** This is not an SF trial or a pure hardware effect separated from education. Ten-year battery labeling does not establish ten-year effective incremental protection.

[Yellman et al. primary economic analysis](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/47479/cdc_47479_DS1.pdf), Tables1–2: fatal rates1.6versus6.1/100,000person-years, difference4.5; nonfatal1.6versus3.5. Mean2.47alarms/house and$59.18/installed alarm in2013USD, based on program personnel (including volunteer valuation), supplies and other costs. That is approximately$146/house historically, not a2026SF quote. The cost observation window and injury observation window differ; do not divide mismatched annual amounts. Claimed societal savings include productivity; these are not donor cash offsets or extra QALYs. Use no nonfatal benefit in a mortality-only screen rather than add inadequately mapped injuries.

[Liu et al.2012 primary decision model](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/36304/cdc_36304_DS1.pdf) supplies a strong counterweight: installation approximately$45,630/QALY, i.e.$456,300/10Q in2011USD, under its high-risk-community assumptions. This is not a local causal estimate, but makes an easy <$100k claim less credible. Alarm function, preexisting working alarms and fire mortality baseline are decisive.

## Cost/health threshold and decision

Retain only the earlier **screen**, not a publishable point estimate: Q per newly served home =4.5e−5 external annual death reduction × residents × discounted additional protection-years × discountedQ/death × SF/causal transfer × funding additionality. The external effect already includes the installed program's observed operation; do not multiply a duplicate installation-effect factor. Shorten effective years for later equivalent care and attrition rather than crediting device life. Funding additionality captures replacement of budgets/services, not the same catch-up delay twice.

At constructed$200all-in/home,3residents,4effective years,20Q/death,.5transfer,.5funding, Q=.0027 and cost/10Q=$740,741. All parameters except external absolute effect are explicit judgments. At$150,3.5residents,5effective years,30Q/death,.75funding **with no SF/causal haircut**, cost/10Q=$84,656; applying a.5haircut makes$169,312, before inflation/cost overruns or fewer additional years. Thus the apparent upside rests on nearly full transport of unusually favorable observational risk and durable counterfactual denial, not on verified SF evidence.

At the central exposure/health assumptions, the qualifying full cash cost ceiling is$27/home; no inspected local source demonstrates that complete marginal cost. A small device-only donor payment could be below it if installation capacity is already genuinely idle, but resource costs still exist and existing sponsors may finance exactly those devices. Free volunteer labor cannot be assumed infinitely expandable.

**Restart only on new decisive evidence:** (1) SF-specific queue for homes without functioning alarms and a documented inability of current funded teams/owners to serve promptly; (2) actual complete incremental cash and resource budget including failed access, recruitment, training and follow-up; (3) defensible absolute risk in that queue, not merely a high-risk demographic label; (4) alarm function and alternative-care timing; (5) permitted SF installation restriction. Without these, prioritize stronger mechanisms. No nationwide attributed “lives saved” anecdotes, invented SF risk, outreach, Site change or new reviewed-organization count used.
