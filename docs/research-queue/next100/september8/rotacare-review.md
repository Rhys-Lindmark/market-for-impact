That is an important warning against treating referral/support as incremental completed treatment. Neither candidate had a newly verified cheap clinical-access gap; hold rather than create another optimistic cure attribution.

A separate severe-untreated-hypertension stress gives **$90.3K Bay / $85.8K total**, but requires a sustained 30-mmHg incremental systolic difference, inexpensive complete care and high additionality. One hypothetical **$100,000 unrestricted gift** allocates 40% to effective blood-pressure care offers and 60% to the remaining primary, acute, other chronic, diagnostic and shared portfolio. All cash is counted once; other health benefits are unquantified, not asserted absent. This **marginal allocation is judgmental**, not a reported functional budget. Necessary BP-stream administration is already in its price; no second overhead penalty is applied.

Each offer is a fully budgeted five-year care pathway, not one screening/visit. Constructed annual cash is $350 central ($60 medicines + $60 tests + $230 delivery/coordination/overhead); $200 favorable ($40+$40+$120); $700 pessimistic ($120+$120+$460). These are **analyst budgets, not quotes**. The favorable price is only a contextual optimistic allowance, not validation of the report’s $200 claim. Future five-year cash is prepaid/reserved from this gift at time zero; no income from the reserve or inflation is modeled. This finances 22.9 central, 40 favorable or 11.4 pessimistic offered courses, including failures. The gift is material relative to annual operations; neither staffing availability nor five-year follow-up capacity is established.

Gross unpaid-resource allowances add $450/$300/$650 per offer-year for donated clinical labor, facilities and other inputs not already in cash. Central total is $151,429; favorable $160,000; pessimistic $137,143. These are undiscounted forward spending envelopes, not a complete present-value social CEA or net incremental public opportunity cost. At zero additionality the nominal funded package could replace existing activity, so its gross resources are not net new resource use. Do not add the value of a donated lab or medication if already in the cash allowance.

## Primary causal evidence and counterweights

[Ettehad et al., Lancet 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26724178/) reports major cardiovascular-event RR **.80 per 10-mmHg systolic reduction** in a meta-analysis of randomized trials. Separate stroke, heart-failure and mortality estimates must not be added to that composite. We transfer this association to the incremental sustained achieved difference against ordinary accessible care, not the clinic’s raw pre/post BP change.

[Blood Pressure Lowering Treatment Trialists’ Collaboration, 2014, author-institution abstract](https://www.ndph.ox.ac.uk/publications/481129) estimates 14, 20, 24 and 38 events prevented per 1,000 treated over five years across increasing risk groups. This supports risk targeting but does not demonstrate a comparable RotaCare cohort. [HOPE-3](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1600175) found no overall benefit in its intermediate-risk cohort, with greater apparent benefit in the upper BP subgroup. A [2024 randomized coaching/practice-facilitation trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2816426) found no superior BP control over enhanced usual care. Thus screening, coaching or attendance is not enough; sustained incremental treatment matters.

Central/favorable/pessimistic **five-year no-new-gift usual-care first-event risks are .10/.25/.03**, and average achieved systolic differences **10/20/3 mmHg**. The comparator may already receive treatment; it is not universally untreated. These are unmeasured local judgments, with completion and persistence already incorporated in the achieved difference. Do not multiply another adherence rate. Funding additionality .5/.8/.2 separately captures whether the gift adds activity versus replacing grants, other donations or public care. Clinical usual-care substitution belongs in the achieved difference, not a duplicate extra discount. The severe-untreated stress changes favorable achieved difference to 30 mmHg; it is not a claim that 30 mmHg is achieved or harmless for the ordinary population.

## Finite, nonduplicated health bridge

Define `RR = .8^(achieved difference/10)`. For a five-year baseline risk R, treated risk is modeled as R×RR. Convert each cumulative risk to a constant annual first-event probability `p = 1−(1−R)^(1/5)`. At each year y=1..5, expected first-event difference is `(1−p0)^(y−1)p0 − (1−p1)^(y−1)p1`. This is a transparent risk-shape approximation, not estimated RotaCare survival curves. Discount each event-year difference by 1.03^y. There is no BP benefit after year 5; later consequences arise only from first events changed during those five years.

Partition those avoided first events into mutually exclusive fatal/stroke/MI/HF shares: central .20/.30/.30/.20, favorable .25/.35/.20/.20, pessimistic .10/.20/.40/.30. These **case-mix judgments** are not extracted from the pooled trial. A fatal stroke is in the fatal cell, never also the nonfatal stroke cell. No separate all-cause mortality effect, secondary event or duplicate diabetes benefit is added.

The [Oxford Vascular Study, 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3806919/) provides longitudinal EQ-5D context: stroke utility around .70 after six months versus roughly .85 in controls, with important severity heterogeneity. This is observational survivor-state evidence, not an intervention effect. It informs scale only; primary indexed text was inspected, not a successful direct full PMC fetch.

All health-trajectory inputs are explicit judgments. Fatal events use utility .75/.80/.65, annual competing mortality .05/.03/.08 and 20/30/10 post-event years. Nonfatal stroke losses .15/.20/.10 last 5/7/3 years; MI .04/.05/.02 lasts 2/3/1; HF .10/.12/.05 lasts 5/7/3. The same competing-survival factor is applied to each annual health loss. Health is summed at 3% discount from the event; its event year was separately discounted, not discounted twice over the same interval. The model omits extra mortality from nonfatal events and later benefit after the finite horizons. It does not call all first-event types equivalent to a stroke or a saved lifetime.

Subtract a small shared present-valued adverse-health allowance per offer of .001/.0005/.0002 QALY before funding additionality. Those are **judgments**, not measured adverse-event rates; medication complications and burdens could be greater, motivating the harm case. Gift-specific independent health harms are separate, never monetary opportunity cost.

### Durable consequences versus later catch-up

The core health tail assumes that first events changed during care create durable event-specific consequences. It does **not** explicitly model whether those events reappear after year 5, or whether a postponed event reduces rather than eliminates the health loss. Saying that blood-pressure benefit ends in year 5 does not resolve that uncertainty. Competing mortality alone is not a full catch-up model. Treating all avoided first events as permanently avoided could overstate the core result.

Two additional sensitivities clip every event's health flow at the end of calendar care year 5: an event at year 1 can contribute four later annual flows, and an event at year 5 contributes none under conservative end-of-year timing. Complete five-year costs and shared harms remain. Central Bay health then falls to .06281 QALY, **$15.92M/10 Bay QALYs** ($24.11M gross-resource). The severe-untreated stress falls to 2.03380 Bay QALYs, **$491,691/10** ($786,706 gross-resource). Neither meets the target. The model's weighted_qaly_per_first_event remains the unclipped reference; these two cases use the explicit event-specific clipped_qaly_per_first_event values instead.

## Decisive next evidence and publication boundary

Needed: actual uninsured/uncontrolled BP and cardiovascular-risk distribution; where SF/Bay recipients live; achieved BP versus alternative care; treatment persistence; a reconciled marginal five-year cash/resource budget; public payer substitutes; and a funded operational capacity plan. General free services or an intake limit do not prove donor-remediable room. Existing grants, reserves, donated clinical inputs and other free/public care remain baseline. The model does not price clinical advice for any particular patient.

This packet is useful chiefly because it rejects a misleadingly cheap $200 complete-cost interpretation while retaining a transparent conditional high-risk mechanism. The central result is not below target, and the severe-untreated scenario is not a demonstrated opportunity.
