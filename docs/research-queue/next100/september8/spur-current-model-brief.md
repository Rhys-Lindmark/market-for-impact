## Causal chain and native health anchor

Additional work → genuine earlier gas-to-electric conversions versus funded/required conversion → reduced outdoor primary and secondaryPM2.5 → modeled mortality reduction → explicit remaining-health-years bridge. **Higher permit compliance alone is not more electrification**: existing unpermitted installations might simply become documented. Reduced fees are not QALYs. Shorter permit processing might alter an emergency replacement choice, but this behavioral effect is not measured here.

The Air District's December2022 [primary atmospheric/health assessment, AppendixE TableE.2, pp6–8](https://www.baaqmd.gov/~/media/dotgov/files/rules/reg-9-rule-6-nitrogen-oxides-emissions-from-natural-gasfired-water-heaters/2021-amendment/documents/20221220_sr_appe_rg09040906-pdf.pdf?rev=f05e1e6f12874600a0382b178b04ab0d#page=7) models **37–85 fewer premature deaths per year** with elimination of applicable regional commercial/residential space/water-heating emissions and resulting totalPM2.5 reductions. Its23–52 secondaryPM-only estimate is an alternative, not additional benefit. The range reflects epidemiological-study choice, not SPUR uncertainty. CMAQ atmospheric modeling and BenMAP health-risk functions are not randomized policy evidence. Monetary valuations are largely willingness-to-pay, not recoverable health-system savings. Table visually inspected.

No asthma, cardiovascular morbidity, indoor air, climate, electricity-system or cooling benefits are added, avoiding unvalidated overlap. Benefits are not assigned separately to both replaced appliances and each household occupant. Regional modeled health is not automatically SF health. The total-PM anchor applies to actual gas-to-electric conversion removing combustion emissions; a hypothetical zero-NOx gas appliance might retain primary-PM emissions and cannot inherit the full37–85 anchor.

## Inspectable model

`Q = F × P × A × T × S × D × L − H`; `donor $/10Q = 10*C/Q` only ifQ>0.

- **F:** conditional package's equivalent fraction of full regional appliance exposure reduction brought forward, net of what happens anyway. This is an outcome-scale judgment, **not a demonstrated fraction of installations or a linear emissions coefficient**.
- **P:** probability the prospective implementation package changes actual conversion timing/uptake; it is not a bill-passage probability.
- **A:** share causally attributable to this marginal grant, allowing replacement of already-budgeted SPUR work and contributions by public agencies, contractors and other organizations.
- **T:** equivalent discounted steady-state mortality-impact years. Includes adoption ramp, actual comparator catch-up, mortality lag and calendar discounting. It is not heat-pump lifetime or automatically years since permit issuance. A future full model needs explicit annual exposure/lag trajectories.
- **S:** fraction of affected health accruing to SF residents. Geography judgment, not merely SPUR's address or SF population share.
- **D:** full-region annual modeled mortality37–85; midpoint61 is analyst choice, not a source central estimate.
- **L:** remaining QALYs per mortality case, discounted from case time. The3/8/12 values are judgments, not measured life expectancy or full healthy lifetimes. Calendar timing is handled inT, not discounted twice.
- **H:** independent net SF health harm attributable to the grant, already adjusted for its own implementation and additionality pathway. Raw harms per unsafe installation cannot be inserted here without that adjustment. Central0 means unquantified, not established absence. Identical replacement funding leaves shared installation harm unchanged and therefore zero increment; distinct donor-specific harms can persist.

## Existing baseline, implementation costs and what could falsify this

The [Air District's actual appliance program](https://www.baaqmd.gov/rules-and-compliance/rule-development/building-appliances) and [current explanatory site](https://knowthefacts.baaqmd.gov/) document existing zero-NOx standards and evolving implementation. Do not model all future conversions as optional without this baseline. This brief does not assert a current exact exemption/deadline schedule from olderSPUR text. SF itself is already addressing permitting: [February25,2026 official state-legislation packet](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/February_25_2026_State_Legislation_Committee_Packet.pdf) documents SF Environment's involvement in heat-pump access. SPUR is one contributor among agencies, the Building Decarbonization Coalition, implementers, homeowners and incentive programs.

Full-resource costs are **not zero or measured here**. A completed model needs net extra city/contractor implementation, equipment/electrical work, operation/maintenance, disposal and financing burdens versus the actual replacement schedule. Do not add the whole price of a replacement if the baseline buys one anyway; do not treat permit-fee reductions or rebates as pure resource savings. The cash budget omits these net induced resources. No energy or healthcare cost offsets are claimed.

At the stress result21.42Q, the $100K/10Q threshold allows total net resources of$214,200, only **$114,200 beyond the$100K grant**. Another$1M of net resources would make that same health coordinate approximately$513,539/10Q. Thus its donor-only upside is fragile, especially if large numbers of installations need incremental public/private spending. Central and favorable already fail before adding resource costs.

Most decisive future checks: attributable earlier conversions rather than permits; age/type of replaced gas equipment; actual alternative replacement dates under existing rules; SF exposure share from geographic health modeling; whether the technical work is truly additional; and net implementation/equipment costs. No published dose-response links an extra$100K of SPUR work toF/P/A. Do not invent one, infer proportional effect from the whole organizational budget, or multiply results from other housing/transit programs into this model.
