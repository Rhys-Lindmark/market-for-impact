# SPUR housing and transportation: whole-organization model inputs

September 8, 2026. Independent primary-source research asset for the manager's whole-organization estimate. **Clean heat alone is not representative of SPUR's full portfolio.** Housing implementation and transportation coordination/fiscal analysis are real additional channels. However, no primary source establishes the causal success probability or marginal health yield of an unrestricted gift. The contribution parameters below must remain explicitly judgmental; this packet does not manufacture them.

## Actual housing activity and counterfactual

**Implementation knowledge:** [California Housing Laws, June 30, 2026](https://www.spur.org/publications/research/2026-06-30/california-housing-laws) is an actual guide/database explaining roughly 100 post-2016 laws across approvals, affordability, density and design. Updating guidance, diagnosing misapplication and technical engagement are plausible uses of incremental staff capacity. The guide already exists; its entire production and every subsequent unit built under a described law are not new-gift effects. State agencies, planners, developers and legal counsel supply substantial alternative expertise. Downloads or events are intermediate outputs, not additional homes.

**Permitting/governance:** [Permitting Progress, May 27, 2026](https://www.spur.org/publications/spur-report/2026-05-27/permitting-progress) diagnoses inflexible departmental structures, prescriptive charter rules and appeals. The actual recommendations importantly require charter/ballot change in several areas: do not describe them as an already delivered non-electoral administrative pilot. Technical research and implementation advice are legitimate components; this packet offers no election strategy or probability of passage. A whole-organization estimate must disclose whether electoral advocacy effects are excluded rather than silently assigning them zero factual value.

**Economic feasibility:** [Realigning Housing Policy With Real-World Economics, June 25, 2026](https://www.spur.org/news/2026-06-25/realigning-housing-policy-real-world-economics-san-francisco) supports recalibrating inclusionary requirements/impact fees and directly links the city controller's feasibility work. This is an actual policy position, not a source-measured production gain. Construction cost, interest rates, financing, demand and baseline projects remain constraints. Reduced requirements could enable projects but also reduce affordable units/revenue on projects that would have proceeded anyway. Net effects must compare those two margins. The June article describes proposals; their subsequent legal status was not independently resolved in this bounded pass, so label them dated advocacy rather than currently unpassed or adopted.

**Historical wins:** SPUR's [2025–26 annual account](https://www.spur.org/about/annual-reports/2026) reports elected-official briefings/workshops around Family Zoning, co-sponsored transit-oriented housing legislation, and office-conversion financing influence. These document involvement, not sole or pivotal causation. [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan) independently dates Family Zoning signing December 12, 2025 and effectiveness January 12, 2026, after a multiyear city process. Today's donation cannot retroactively cause adoption. Zoning capacity is not construction, occupied homes, formerly homeless families or QALYs. Public rezoning obligations and coalition partners weaken a no-SPUR/no-policy counterfactual without proving zero contribution.

## Actual transportation activity and counterfactual

**Fiscal/operational research:** [Taking Muni's Vitals, March 26, 2026](https://www.spur.org/publications/research/2026-03-26/taking-munis-vitals) is an actual operator benchmarking report. SPUR's annual account claims recommendations reflected in SFMTA's 2026–27 budget. That is evidence of congruence/self-reported influence, not independent causal attribution. Additional research could support better service per dollar, fare simplicity or implementation monitoring; an unrestricted gift does not automatically purchase service-hours.

**Bridge funding:** [SPUR's February 13, 2026 support letter](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/Transit%20Loan%20Letter%20of%20Support%20%281%29.pdf) documents participation. [Governor's February 19 enactment announcement](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/02/19/governor-newsom-signs-legislation-authorizing-590-million-emergency-loan-to-bay-area-transit/) verifies that **$590 million is a public loan**, not a SPUR grant or permanent revenue gain. [MTC June 24, 2026 report](https://mtc.ca.gov/news/report-commission-june-24-2026) describes implementation for the July 2026 fiscal year. Avoid attributing all retained transit service to a September gift, or treating already authorized dollars as fresh marginal policy impact. Financing has repayment/opportunity costs. Future transit funding remains relevant to SPUR's portfolio, but no election-outcome probability is derived here.

**Regional fare/network coordination:** [MTC's fare-coordination program](https://mtc.ca.gov/operations/transit-regional-network-management/transit-fare-coordination-integration) establishes existing agency-led integration, BayPass and interagency transfers. [MTC's Phase 1 evaluation](https://mtc.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025-06/Report_BayPass_Phase_1_Evaluation.pdf?cb=aa2be76b) contains randomized student-pass research. The [evaluating team's primary summary](https://thebehaviouralist.com/portfolio-item/understanding-the-impact-of-free-travel-passes/) reports approximately 30%/32% greater trips in years one/two. These estimate access to a pass in selected student settings, **not SPUR advocacy, mortality or healthy travel**. Don't use the older preliminary 35% headline as an invariant regional causal coefficient. Extra transit trips may replace walking, cycling, driving or no trip; health sign depends on substitution. Current public rollout and other advocates are the baseline.

## Model-ready architecture without invented probabilities

Use one unrestricted gift G and a joint portfolio response, not G independently spent in full on every policy. Two coherent options:

1. Allocate G across modeled incremental staff/resource uses with explicit judgmental shares summing to at most one, then estimate each channel's response.
2. Model SPUR as a jointly produced organizational capability: total incremental outcomes from G across channels, with shared staff/overhead and correlated success represented once. Do not additionally apply arbitrary allocation shares if outcomes are already net grant-attributable.

Historical organization expenses in the existing accepted SPUR packet are context, not marginal policy prices. A transparent budget illustration might be **$100,000 for 0.5 loaded staff-year at $160,000/year plus $20,000 shared data/convening/admin**. Both quantities are analyst allowances, not a SPUR quote, actual hiring plan or source-supported allocation. The same staff-year cannot be assigned simultaneously to housing, transit and heat. Unrestricted gifts may instead preserve current staffing, replenish reserves or substitute other gifts; preservation can be beneficial but requires an actual loss counterfactual.

Housing bridge: additional or accelerated *occupied home-years* caused by the organization's incremental activity → changes in crowding, housing insecurity, costs or displacement for affected people → finite incremental health. Separate permanently additional housing from completion brought forward; stop acceleration credit when baseline catches up. Market-wide affordability spillovers and direct occupant effects overlap. Housing welfare is broader than health, and should be reported separately rather than converted wholesale into QALYs.

Transit bridge: additional reliable service/rider-access exposure attributable to new activity → travel-mode, physical activity, pollution, collisions and missed-care changes → net finite health. Model access and injuries separately, avoiding double credit when the same avoided car trip affects both. Regional gains require an SF-resident share if the website denominator is SF-only. Regional unrestricted activity does not become locally restricted merely because SPUR has an SF office.

The common break-even for a $100,000 gift is **10 net QALYs**. If housing were credited with .01 net QALY per additional occupied home-year (illustrative judgment, not empirical mapping), it would require 1,000 grant-attributable home-years. At .1, 100 home-years. These thresholds expose what an estimate claims, not plausible success probabilities or recommended inputs. For an unknown transit yield q per attributable rider-year, the requirement is 10/q rider-years; no unsupported positive q is supplied.

## Harms, nulls and whole-organization interpretation

Retain nulls for research not changing decisions, policies changing no feasible construction/service, funding replacement, and outcomes benefiting non-SF residents outside the chosen boundary. Potential harms include construction disruption/displacement, reduced affordable-unit funding on otherwise viable projects, unequal fare burdens, exclusionary fare enforcement, lost alternative public investment and public debt. Do not equate fee reductions or fare increases with health gains without net distributional accounting. Donor-specific harm may persist under financing replacement; shared implementation harms cancel only under genuinely identical replacement.

Housing near transit, fare integration and clean heat interact: the same households, reduced vehicle travel and pollution cannot receive full independent credit in every channel. Whole-organization QALYs are the sum of nonoverlapping marginal effects, not the sum of separate policy headline benefits. Cost per QALY is computed only after aggregation, not averaged across program ratios. The clean-heat analysis should remain one disclosed component or sensitivity, not be relabeled a complete SPUR estimate. If the manager assigns whole-portfolio central judgments, show their implied occupied home-years/service outcomes and broad null/harm scenarios alongside the score. Evidence here supports actual mechanisms and baseline constraints, **not a calibrated numeric success rate**.
