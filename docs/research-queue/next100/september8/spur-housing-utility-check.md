# SPUR housing-health utility check

September 8, 2026. Bounded primary-source check of the .01 direct and .005 spillover utility priors. No new portfolio model or Site changes.

## Decision

**No directly transferable causal or longitudinal preference-based utility estimate was identified for modest rent savings, reduced rent burden or crowding improvement. Neither .01 nor .005 is source-calibrated.** The best matched evidence supports a plausible, heterogeneous mental-health pathway, not a universal rent-to-QALY coefficient. The .005 spillover prior is more vulnerable than the .01 conditional direct prior because it is currently awarded to every counted spillover adult-year without an explicit probability of health-relevant response.

Three decisive sources below are outside a simple homelessness-to-housing contrast. Do not use SF-36 mental-health points, K10 scores or depressive-symptom prevalence as if they were preference utilities. No validated mapping is established here.

## 1. Bentley et al.: actual affordability transitions, small nonutility change

[American Journal of Epidemiology, 2011, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21821543/), “Association between housing affordability and mental health: a longitudinal analysis of a nationally representative household survey in Australia.” Entering unaffordable housing among low-to-moderate-income households was associated with a **−1.19 mental-health-score change (95% CI −1.97 to −.41)**, controlling for income changes and moving. The within-person design improves on a cross-sectional income/health gradient but is not randomized and does not establish symmetry when burden falls.

Relevance: harmful affordability transitions can matter even without homelessness. Limitation: this is a mental-health scale, not −.0119 utility, SF-6D or QALYs. Nor is it an effect of a 1% rent reduction across all renters. The bounded check verified primary indexed abstract passages; direct PubMed retrieval encountered a browser challenge. No full-text/table read is claimed.

## 2. Denary et al.: stronger transition comparison is null-compatible

[Primary PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34144434/), [author-institution record](https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/nhperl_articles/24/), [primary manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299474/). Social Science & Medicine 282, August 2021, DOI10.1016/j.socscimed.2021.114100. Cohort:400 low-income New Haven adults. Rental-assistance recipients had significantly lower distress than waitlisted participants in the cross-sectional comparison. **Within-person transitions into assistance showed a statistically nonsignificant distress decrease.** The transition analysis involved84 participants, comparing K10 immediately before/after assistance; not a utility instrument. Sensitivity analyses addressed homelessness and supportive-housing observations, rather than assuming all benefit was homelessness relief.

This is an important contrary result: even actual receipt of substantial assistance did not establish a precise immediate longitudinal mental-health benefit. It does not prove no effect; sample size, moving stress and timing matter. It weakens confidence in automatic spillover utility gains or immediate full benefit from housing changes. Primary indexed methods/abstract passages inspected; PMC/publisher direct full-text retrieval failed. No unverified coefficient is supplied.

## 3. Reeves et al.: benefit cuts cause small absolute symptom changes, not measured QALYs

[Primary institutional full manuscript](https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/67723/1/Reeves_Reductions%20in%20UK%20benefit_2016.pdf), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27613659/). American Journal of Epidemiology, September15,2016,184(6):421–429, DOI10.1093/aje/kww055. Quasi-natural experiment following April2011 UK private-renter Housing Benefit cuts, mean approximately**£1,220 per year**. Relative to private renters not receiving that benefit, depressive-symptom prevalence increased **1.8 percentage points (95% CI1.0–2.7)** during April2011–March2013 after controlling for prior trends.

This anchors absolute mental-health response to a material financial shock. It is not a randomized rent-reduction study, diagnosed major-depression incidence, utility decrement or proof that reversing cuts produces symmetric benefits. Its historic UK currency amount must not be treated as a current SF dose or linearly rescaled. Primary PDF text and abstract inspected; no generic preference-utility endpoint identified.

## What this says about the current priors

The direct .01 is conditional on a genuinely improved harmful housing state, and central counts only15% of direct residents. Thus average modeled direct improvement is **.0015 per resident-year**, not .01 for every occupant. That distinction must remain visible. A physically meaningful reduction in crowding, hazards or severe stress could support a conditional .01 judgment, but the model currently does not identify the state or dose. Do not claim that the present papers validate it. A separate clinical hazard or crowding pathway would need condition-specific evidence and cannot simply add to this net utility.

The .005 spillover is an **average** per counted nonoccupant adult-year in the existing formula. It cannot be called a conditional responder utility without another response probability or redefining the counted adults as demonstrated responders. If, purely diagnostically, a symptomatic health episode carries .10 utility loss, .005 average improvement implies5 percentage points fewer episodes sustained over a year; .001 implies1 point. The .10 value is an illustrative unit check, **not a sourced depression disutility or new model coefficient**. Reeves'1.8-point symptom effect after a sizable cut indicates that5-point annual-average gains from unspecified modest rent relief are not an obviously conservative assumption. This comparison does not establish an upper bound because populations, outcomes and treatment direction differ.

**Suggested judgment update for coordinator consideration:** retain .01 direct only as a conditional harmful-state planning value, but reduce provisional central spillover average from .005 to **.001**, with zero as a prominent null and old .005 preserved as a historical/high sensitivity. This is cautious analyst shrinkage prompted by the missing dose and null-compatible longitudinal comparison, not an empirically estimated mapping. If “spillover household-years” instead denotes verified meaningful stress-resolution episodes, .005 might remain defensible conditionally—but the existing .5 household-year multiplier has not demonstrated that selection. Do not hide the distinction by relabeling every rent recipient a responder. No prices or physical-output priors were changed in this research asset.

## SF transfer, duration and overlap requirements

- Specify net rent savings, starting burden/income and actual change in harmful stress, crowding or displacement. Rent-controlled incumbents and financially comfortable renters may see little near-term health change. Price effects observed around new construction do not establish health effects for every nearby household.
- A stable rent reduction does not establish five years of constant utility improvement; adaptation, later income change and comparator housing supply can erase the health difference. Repeated state/health measurement is preferable to carrying a one-time symptom contrast across the building's life.
- Direct occupants and nonoccupant spillover households must be disjoint. Mental-health effects of food security, income, neighborhood safety and stability can describe the same person/state; a generic net utility term must not be added to each again.
- Moving may improve or worsen crowding, access, noise and support networks. Include origin-location outcomes and displaced residents; healthier city-average composition is not causal health improvement.
- These three sources cannot validate avoided eviction itself as a QALY: legal retention may not prevent a move. Major subsidized housing or neighborhood relocation cannot calibrate modest rent savings without a measured intermediate state.

Bottom line: housing remains a legitimate citywide SPUR mechanism, including spillovers. The evidence strengthens its plausibility but **does not strengthen the numerical utility calibration**. The recommended central-spillover shrinkage should be explicitly versioned if adopted; independent acceptance of arithmetic is not acceptance of these empirical priors.
