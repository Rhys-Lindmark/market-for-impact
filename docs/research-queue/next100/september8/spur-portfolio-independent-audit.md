## Material integration clarifications

1. **Make beneficiary geography an explicit prior, not a consequence of infrastructure location.** The tables label home/service outputs SF versus rest of Bay, but the headline promises *resident* QALYs. Nonoccupant housing spillovers, commuters and transit users cross jurisdictional boundaries. State that current cells assume beneficiary health geographically allocated as shown; that allocation is unverified. A future geographic transfer matrix should map project location to affected resident location. Do not simply label all SF service-hour benefit as SF-resident benefit or assume rest-of-Bay housing cannot affect SF rents. Retain the current totals as stated allocation judgments; Bay aggregation reduces, but does not eliminate, migration/out-of-region issues.

2. **Encode finite exposure precisely in JSON.** Six end-year credits at years 3–8 discounted 3% sum to **5.106222494**, not exactly five. The prose currently says “about five,” which is fine and not an arithmetic defect because H is directly stipulated in discounted home-years. To preserve H=1,000, define it as an already-integrated input; the “200 homes for five discounted years” explanation is approximate. Alternatively scale annual exposure to total exactly five. Do not recompute H from six full annual credits and retain the old aggregate outputs without acknowledging the change. Give pessimistic housing a finite calendar schedule too; “smaller, shorter acceleration” alone should not be the implemented time specification. Transit likewise needs its five-year aggregate convention preserved, not another five-year multiplier.

3. **Keep partial-health scope visible despite the whole-organization numerator.** It is a whole unrestricted-gift model with quantified housing, transport and clean heat, not an exhaustive welfare estimate. Fifteen percent of spending and potentially large nonhealth effects are unquantified. The current disclosure handles this properly; do not drop it in a headline calling the number SPUR's total societal value. Public operating/construction/implementation costs remain outside the donor-only numerator. The $1M stress is illustrative, not a costed resource estimate or a confidence bound.

## Causal and prior acceptance

The housing scale and contribution are defensible *coordinates for a disclosed judgment model*, not source-calibrated expected construction. Central gift-attributable SF output is 20 discounted home-years; favorable 250. Exposing those physical implications is much better than claiming a fraction of all city housing or assigning all of an enacted law. [Family Zoning's official implementation record](https://sfplanning.org/sf-family-zoning-plan) makes its adoption baseline. [Permitting Progress](https://www.spur.org/publications/spur-report/2026-05-27/permitting-progress) is real but includes charter-change mechanisms; prospective technical implementation must not be called an already operating administrative pilot.

The nonoccupant housing utility channel provides most central housing health (.00375 versus .003 direct QALY/home-year). Its effect magnitude is unvalidated, but not dimensionally incoherent: half a nonoccupant household-year × 1.5 adults × .005 utility. Keep “net” meaning the entire defined affected population's counterfactual benefit after ordinary harms, and retain potentially omitted harms outside those cells. Nearby rent effects do not empirically validate household stress, the .005 utility value or the statewide scope. This is the dominant health-bridge prior and deserves an independent zero-spillover sensitivity rather than being buried among minor parameters. Such a sensitivity does not replace the central judgment.

Transportation's service-hours → marginal trips → equivalent rider-years → net health is dimensionally coherent. Four hundred trips normalizes recurrent exposure; it is not 400 distinct people. [MTC's existing fare coordination](https://mtc.ca.gov/operations/transit-regional-network-management/transit-fare-coordination-integration) and public operations are baseline. The proposal correctly does not import a BayPass student ridership effect as SPUR health impact. Preserve competing walk/cycle/car/no-trip substitutions and possible negative injury/activity effects in the net prior. No primary source establishes the .005 utility-year gain, but that does not prohibit its explicit use as judgment.

[The authorized $590M transit bridge is a loan](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/02/19/governor-newsom-signs-legislation-authorizing-590-million-emergency-loan-to-bay-area-transit/), already part of baseline and not the current gift's numerator or impact. The proposal avoids directly crediting it. No political persuasion strategy or manufactured electoral success probability is needed.

## Duplication, nulls and decision

The declared ledger exclusions are necessary: new housing's travel/pollution changes cannot also appear in transit or heat; heat applies to existing buildings outside credited projects; governance support is not an extra outcome claim. In implementation, use net disjoint *benefits*, not blanket exclusion of every person who receives multiple distinct services. Multiple genuinely distinct improvements for one person are possible, but overlapping utility improvements cannot each be full additive gains.

Complete replacement null and independently donor-induced signed harm are correctly separate. Shared net utility priors need not have an additional arbitrary harm penalty; that would double-subtract. Favorable SF's $21,960 room beyond the donor budget is correctly calculated and fragile. Do not promote its sub-$100K donor result into a resource-adjusted recommendation.

**Bottom line:** accept central $6.57M SF / $1.44M Bay as broad provisional judgment estimates. Preserve source-versus-prior labeling, the beneficiary-geography assumption, finite integrated output years and the donor/resource distinction. No demand for certainty or a priced restricted offer should erase the useful whole-portfolio answer.
