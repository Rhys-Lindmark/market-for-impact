## Actual answer, with honest uncertainty

For one additional **$100,000 unrestricted gift**, my provisional central is **$6.57M per 10 incremental SF-resident QALYs**, or **$1.44M per 10 Bay-resident QALYs including SF**. Favorable joint stress: **$81,994 SF / $19,365 Bay**. Pessimistic positive: **$22.38B SF / $4.00B Bay**. Zero effect and net harm remain credible. These are analyst best-guess coordinates, not confidence intervals or a calibrated posterior mean. The favorable result is not enough for a donation recommendation.

Bay includes SF: never add the two totals. This incorporates housing spillovers and transport, rather than replacing the organization with clean heat. Fifteen percent of the budget has presently unquantified governance/economic-health benefits; setting those credits to zero is an explicit partial-health accounting convention, not proof those activities are valueless. The omitted effects could include harms as well as benefits.

## Shared budget and finite scope

One common allocation in every scenario: housing/planning $35,000; transportation $30,000; clean heat/environmental implementation $20,000; governance/procurement $10,000; economic security/downtown work $5,000. These are **marginal allocation judgments**, not reported SPUR spending shares or donor restrictions. Each amount includes its share of research, education, coordination, fundraising and administration; there is no separate overhead penalty or five duplicated $100,000 budgets. Actual unrestricted allocation may differ completely.

This gift is about 1.45% of the reported $6.917M FY25–26 expenses, but proportional budget is not proportional impact. A bounded two-year staff/implementation contribution may influence a larger policy; it cannot purchase that whole policy or its public implementation. Physical outputs below are conditional project/policy scopes multiplied by a single gift-versus-no-gift contribution. That contribution already includes funding substitution, implementation failure and collaborator credit: **do not multiply another success/additionality factor into housing or transit**.

Benefit windows stop after comparator catch-up. Housing and transport output-years are already discounted; illustrative central/favorable housing exposure spans years 3–8 after gift, giving about five discounted years at 3%, not permanent building lifetime. Pessimistic output may be a smaller, shorter acceleration. Transit service-hour totals accrue within five years and already incorporate ramp/catch-up/discounting. Heat uses its explicitly discounted effective impact-year convention. No infinite policy tail.

## Housing: completion, residents and citywide spillovers

Relevant actual work includes [Permitting Progress, May 2026](https://www.spur.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/SPUR_Permitting_Progress.pdf) and [Reinventing Downtown, February 4, 2026](https://www.spur.org/publications/policy-brief/2026-02-04/reinventing-downtown). These establish institutional fit, not a project pipeline. The latter proposes coordinated development/financing, not completed units. Existing city reforms and other advocates are baseline.

Define H as conditional **net additional or earlier occupied home-years**, not permits, zoning capacity or all new homes. Origin/destination changes must actually improve health; simple immigration of healthier people is not an outcome. Use separate SF and rest-of-Bay cells. Let a be the gift's net contribution to this conditional output. It is a judgment, not observed leverage.

Q_housing = H × a × [people × improved-state fraction × direct utility + spillover household-years × adults × spillover utility]. Central SF = 1,000 × .02 × (.003 + .00375) = **.135 QALY**; Bay total = **.54**. Favorable SF10.5/Bay42; pessimistic SF.00004/Bay.00016.

Scale defense: central conditional SF scope is roughly 200 net occupied units accelerated for five discounted years, rest-of-Bay 600; favorable 1,000 SF/3,000 elsewhere. SPUR's actual citywide/statewide planning work makes scopes larger than one building conceivable, but **none is verified in a funded pipeline**. Gift attribution reduces central expected SF output to four equivalent homes for five discounted years; favorable to50. This is the most important uncalibrated leverage prior, not a historical conversion rate. Favorable5% attribution to broad policy change is particularly demanding for a $35,000 component. If finance constraints prevent construction, H=0 regardless of policy adoption.

Health prior defense: most direct occupants receive no modeled harmful-state improvement; ordinary new-home occupancy is not treated as a move out of homelessness. Citywide spillovers are allowed for affected nonoccupants rather than all residents. The chosen small utility effects and spillover household counts are judgments, not derived from rent savings. [Asquith/Mast/Reed 2023](https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article/105/2/359/100977/Local-Effects-of-Large-New-Apartment-Buildings-in) supports nearby rent spillovers but its 5–7% local effect is **not** a citywide per-building coefficient. [Pennington 2021](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3867764) uses a specific relative displacement-risk proxy, not homelessness prevention. Neither identifies these utility values. Do not import Moving to Opportunity diabetes/obesity effects into this small housing-state bridge.

The utility priors are NET of ordinary construction/displacement burdens within these affected cells. If gross improvements are used later, add an explicit shared-harm subtraction instead. Spillover households exclude occupants/moving chains already counted. Overlapping catchments cannot each receive full stress relief. A source-calibrated rent-to-state model could materially raise or lower the estimate.

Scale defense: central expected SF100 service-hours over five years is a small efficiency/continuity gain for a $30,000 implementation contribution, not preservation of Muni. Favorable2,500 SF hours is much stronger; conditional scope50,000 hours is still a specific network slice, not all agency operations. None is measured SPUR production. Passenger trips are NEW versus baseline, not existing ridership multiplied by every hour; if only crowding changes with no net useful trips, this pathway must be remodeled.400 trips is an analyst recurrent-use normalization, not a source observation. The health fraction avoids assigning a health gain to every saved commute minute.

Health gain is a composite, net annual access/activity/injury judgment. It is deliberately not computed from the whole regional active-transport scenario or long-term1,100-step relocation association in Jason's audit. Injury exposure, pollution and delayed care could change its sign. Exclude housing-improved recipients and already counted housing-related travel gains from this transit slice. The .005/.01/.001 utility-year priors are not measured generic effects of transit.

For this $20,000 allocation, use F=.002/.01/.0002 (central/favorable/pessimistic), P=.5/.7/.2, A=.1/.2/.02, T=3/5/1, D=61/85/37 annual modeled deaths, L=8/12/3 QALYs per case, s=.10/.15/.05. All but the external modeled mortality range are judgments. Central BayQ=.1464, SFQ=.01464; favorable7.14/1.071; pessimistic.0000888/.00000444.

The F values are a proportional **planning sensitivity** to the prior $100,000 component scope, not an established linear funding-output law. This is weaker than a counted-installation forecast and must remain labeled. Retained primary model and duration/source caveats: `/private/tmp/mfi-spur-current-model-brief.md`. ## Full-resource comparison and key falsifiers

No complete induced-resource estimate exists. Public transit operations, permitting implementation, construction/renovation, appliances, financing timing and participant burdens cannot vanish because the gift pays only SPUR. Their incremental costs are versus comparator timing, not automatically every dollar of a new building or financing flow.

With an **additional $1M net induced-resource stress**, same health gives central $72.30M/10 SF Q and $15.80M/10 Bay Q; favorable $901,935 SF/$213,013 Bay. This is a stress allowance, not a source-costed resource model. At favorable SF health, threshold total cost is $121,960—only $21,960 above the donor grant; favorable Bay permits $516,400 total, or $416,400 additional. Thus the donor-only upside could disappear under realistic implementation costs. No fiscal or energy offsets are assumed.

Highest-priority evidence: marginal staff allocation, live financially feasible housing pipeline and comparator completion dates, changed rents/housing states outside occupants, causal useful transit service change, actual rider substitution, and shared public implementation cost. If these reveal little additional physical output, move the best estimate toward zero rather than preserve positive priors. The conditional estimate is a concrete answer for SPUR's portfolio, but current uncertainty is dominated by gift-to-output and output-to-health judgments, not arithmetic.
