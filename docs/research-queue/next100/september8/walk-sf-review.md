# Walk San Francisco — new organization, whole-gift conditional model

September 8, 2026. Research-only draft; independent acceptance pending. Model and executable self-check are `data/san-francisco/walk-sf-cea-v1.json` and `./walk-sf-self-check.mjs`. No Site edits, organization contact or publication.

## Discovery gate and result

Walk San Francisco is a distinct recipient absent from the inspected published charity-route roster. It was an outcome-first backlog lead. It merits further review because physical traffic-calming has controlled injury evidence, an SF implementation pathway and potentially broad reach from a comparatively small advocacy budget. Westside methadone continuity remains a competing lead, but its funded clinical baseline and overlap with existing opioid models make its marginal access question less distinctive. NICOS clinical delivery remained insufficiently established. This was a bounded screen, not an exhaustive search for the best new charity.

The provisional whole-gift central estimate is **$5.80M per 10 SF-resident QALYs**, or **$4.58M per 10 Bay-resident QALYs including SF**. A demanding favorable joint stress reaches **$31.7K/$28.3K donor cost**; that is **not** an evidence-backed confidence bound or demonstrated funding opportunity. In that favorable case, the matched public-capital timing perspective rises to $105.6K/$94.5K; charging expected early capital without later-build credit raises it to $384K/$344K. This illustrates possible donor leverage and its sensitivity to public resources—not a recommendation to donate on the favorable scenario.

## Recipient, actual portfolio and scope

The legal recipient is **Walk San Francisco Foundation, EIN 47-2000881**, verified in the [provider-posted IRS determination letter](https://walksf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/irs-determination-letter.pdf) dated February 26, 2015 and its [FY2024–25 Form 990](https://walksf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/wsff-24-25-taxfinal-990.public.pdf). This is a historical determination plus current filing, not fresh TEOS certification or tax advice. The [official impact page](https://walksf.org/about-us/our-impact/) supplies a giving route; no live restricted tranche or unused staff capacity was verified.

The [2026 Slow Our Streets campaign](https://walksf.org/slow-our-streets/) advocates additional traffic-calming and implementation, building on already installed cameras. The [2025 impact report](https://walksf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/walksf-impact-report-2026_final.pdf), posted June 2026, describes safe-intersection work, community organizing, survivor/family support, education and broader policy. This is the whole-organization map. The model does not advise candidate/party support or credit electoral persuasion; it considers non-electoral engineering implementation and accountability only.

One marginal unrestricted **$100K** gift is judgmentally allocated: $60K physical-street implementation work, $15K other policy/education, $15K survivor/family support, $10K shared reserve/other. Required staff, administration and fundraising within the physical stream are included in its $60K; the remainder is not a second overhead charge. All $100K is costed. Only physical-street injury benefits are quantified; unquantified streams may have meaningful welfare/health effects or harms.

The provider's 990 covers July 1, 2024–June 30, 2025. Page 1 and Part IX report expenses **$981,449**, comprising program services **$513,353**, management/general **$252,527** and fundraising **$215,569**; revenue **$894,475** and year-end net assets **$885,931**. The gift is about 10% of annual expenses, a material rather than infinitesimal margin. These figures are filed financial disclosures, **not audited statements or program-output prices**. They neither establish the marginal allocation nor prove the $86,974 shortfall is fundable room. Full expense proportions are not probabilities of impact.

## What a gift would have to change

The modeled pathway is a **hypothetical bundled physical traffic-calming package** brought forward in time through additional work funded by the gift. The package is not a verified offer, identified unfunded street list, or promise of construction. The critical missing deliverable is a named otherwise-delayed package with a real public-capital bottleneck and addressable risk.

The gift's $60K implementation allocation is assumed to generate a 10% central joint chance of meaningful acceleration; favorable 35%, pessimistic 1%. This probability includes failed advocacy, coalition contribution, fundraising replacement, public decisions and the possibility the same project happens anyway. It is explicitly an analyst prior, not measured organizational credit. Do not multiply by the 60% allocation or a second additionality factor again: this probability is already conditional on that allocated effort.

Central success affects a package containing 2% of SF's baseline fatal and serious-injury burden for two years before the no-gift comparator catches up. Favorable success affects 8% for five years; pessimistic 0.5% for one year. These are **risk-weighted exposure shares**, not population, street-length or nonprofit market shares. The favorable combination is ambitious: it implies a substantial multi-corridor implementation change, not a few speed humps or a successful meeting. Historical advocacy accomplishments establish plausibility of work at scale but do not estimate these priors.

The [SFMTA's 2024 High Injury Network update](https://www.sfmta.com/fr/node/45244) places 74% of serious/fatal injury on 13% of streets using 2020–24 data. This establishes concentration, not that Walk SF can access any stated fraction cheaply. Some major-road risk may not be amenable to the traffic-calmed residential zones in the external study. If target streets do not match the intervention, reduce the exposure share/effect or set them to zero.

## Primary risk and causal anchors

[SFMTA, January 8, 2026](https://www.sfmta.com/press-releases/press-release-san-francisco-traffic-fatalities-drop-nearly-half-2025) reports 25 traffic deaths in 2025 versus 43 in 2024. The model uses 25 as a constant central/favorable planning baseline, **not a forecast or causal effect of Walk SF**. The [SFDPH Community Health Assessment 2024, cleaned April 2025](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/San_Francisco_Population_Health_CHA_2024_2025-04.cleaned.pdf), printed page 58, reports approximately 550 severe traffic injuries annually since Vision Zero began in 2014. This is a historical average, **not 550 measured in 2025**. Combining it with 25 deaths is a constructed risk scenario; a current consistent injury table could change the result. The pessimistic baseline is judgmentally lower at 15 deaths and 300 nonfatal severe injuries. City events include nonresident road users.

[Grundy et al., BMJ 2009, full primary PDF](https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/4370/1/bmj.b4469.pdf) studied physically traffic-calmed London 20-mph zones using controlled interrupted time series. All-casualty reduction was 41.9% (95% CI 36.0–47.8); killed/seriously injured 46.3% (38.6–54.1); death-only 35.1% with **CI −1.9–72.0, including no effect**. Restriction to 2000–06 gave smaller all-casualty and KSI estimates, 22.7% and 28.4%. It was not randomized, did not measure QALYs and does not support assigning all-casualty reductions to deaths, signage, cameras or advocacy directly. The study's serious-injury definition also differs from modern SF clinical surveillance.

The model therefore uses separate judgmental fatal/severe reductions: central 10%/20%, favorable 35%/40%, pessimistic 2%/5%. These are not source point estimates or confidence limits. A death-effect-null sensitivity is retained because primary mortality evidence is imprecise. Positive effects assume net benefit after traffic diversion and other shared transport harms; a negative-risk-transfer scenario tests failure of that assumption.

## Finite health bridge — no invented measured QALYs

Every utility, survival and persistence input below is an **analyst judgment**, not a reported trial QALY result. Fatal loss is modeled with a finite survival schedule: baseline utility .80/.85/.65, annual competing hazard .02/.01/.05, horizon 30/40/15 years. At 3% discount, this gives **12.31/16.82/5.61 QALYs per death avoided**, not a full unqualified lifetime. Older adults are disproportionately harmed in SF; the favorable long survival is a joint favorable stress, not an age-matched estimate.

For nonfatal severe injuries, utility loss .10/.15/.03 for 3/5/1 years gives **.283/.687/.029 QALYs per injury avoided**. This intentionally simple finite trajectory does not establish case-mix accuracy. A person with several diagnoses is one injured person; do not multiply by lesions, admissions or diagnoses. A fatality is not also counted as a nonfatal severe injury. No additional caregiver, fear-of-walking, activity or pollution QALYs are added.

The package opens at the beginning of year 2 centrally/favorably, year 3 pessimistically. Only risk differences during years 2–3, 2–6 or year 3 are credited. Events are conservatively dated at year-end, followed by their finite health trajectory. Discount event timing once and the later health years once; these are different elapsed intervals, not double-discounting the same period. Implementation benefit stops when the comparator catches up, though injuries prevented before catch-up can have later health consequences.

## Geography and numeric scenarios

Central beneficiary-residence prior: 75% SF, 20% other Bay, 5% outside Bay. Favorable 85%/10%/5%; pessimistic 60%/25%/15%. These are not measured residence shares. SF location does not imply SF-resident health. Outside-Bay effects are disclosed but excluded from both local headlines; Bay includes SF and must not be added to it.

The core equation is `p × target risk share × discounted acceleration years × (fatal incidence × fatal reduction × finite fatal Q + nonfatal severe incidence × severe reduction × finite injury Q)`. Partition by residence and subtract any independent donor-specific harm. The entire gift is the numerator even when effects are zero. Independent harms are hypothetical gift-specific harms, not ordinary shared construction burden or already-priced money. No positive price is reported for zero or negative health.

Central funding threshold: with all other central assumptions, even p=1 gives only about 1.72 SF QALYs per gift, below the required >10 for $100K/10. Thus merely verifying some donor contribution cannot make the central package meet the target; package reach, effect, exposure duration or cost must materially differ. Favorable upside depends on jointly stronger values and should not be promoted as likely.

## Public funding, resource costs and disconfirmation

Existing cameras, daylighting, speed-limit changes and scheduled quick-build projects are baseline. The [SFMTA District 2 Safety Study page](https://www.sfmta.com/projects/district-2-safety-study) says the study is publicly funded; its timeline labels future 2027 implementation completed while elsewhere predicting 2027 completion, so do not infer actual completed construction from that field. The page is contrary to a claim that every local safety project is waiting for donations. The model does not assign any specific District 2 project to Walk SF. No historical citywide mortality decline or full past legislative win is credited.

The package capital allowances are **$1M central / $3M favorable / $300K pessimistic**, with annual maintenance $20K/$60K/$10K. No current construction quote is verified. [Historical SFMTA traffic-calming costs](https://www.sfmta.com/tl/media/2013/download?inline=) are only rough context and are too old to price a modern package; neither $7K humps nor outreach-only staff cost can represent a complete coordinated network.

Three cost perspectives remain distinct:

1. Donor: full $100K gift.
2. Matched-build timing: add `p × [capital PV if built early minus capital PV if built later + extra early maintenance]`. The no-gift package is assumed identical and delivered after the acceleration window. Central total $109,289; favorable $333,432; pessimistic $100,174. This limited comparison omits many societal costs and downstream savings; it is not a fully priced social CEA.
3. Gross forward-resource stress: charge expected early capital without credit for later baseline expenditure. Totals $200,803 / $1,212,790 / $102,919. It is an intentionally stricter spending envelope, not the incremental resource cost of pure acceleration.

Under p=0 both additional public-cost terms vanish while the gift remains spent. Under no injury effect, construction can still cost money. Staff/volunteer time outside the gift, displaced city projects, public financing, maintenance changes, implementation disruption and downstream medical savings remain incompletely priced. The shared capital construction ledger must be deduplicated with SPUR, public agencies and other advocates before portfolio aggregation. Distinct organizations are not automatically independent outcome claims.

## What would make this actionable

A named package and current injury/exposure data; a realistic no-gift timeline; public engineering/design and capital commitments; the exact work a gift changes; coalition attribution; residence/age/injury mix; complete net costs. Any of these can overturn the favorable possibility. Missing a quoted offer does not prohibit a conditional research model, but this packet is not a verified donation recommendation. Whole-organization scope is represented by a costed portfolio with explicitly unquantified streams, not by treating every dollar as speed-camera implementation.
