## Finite recurrent overdose survival
The unit is one distinct at-risk person with a supported witness/rescue network over the finite funded period. It is not a kit or reported reversal. Central50 nominal people at$300 become20 financially additional people. No conversion of the organization's reported dose count to unique recipients is assumed.

A [historical Medicaid study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10398609/) measured295 repeat overdoses per1,000 person-years and1,154 fatal opioid overdoses per100,000 person-years after nonfatal overdose. The [related all-cause cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6143082/) measured778.3 deaths per10,000 person-years. These are context, not current SF fentanyl-era hazards or a causal naloxone effect.

Central priors:0.5 overdose events/year,10% fatality without effective rescue,80% baseline effective rescue, and+10percentage points during one supported year. Effective rescue includes witnesses, medication availability, successful use, EMS and substitutes. Nonfatal events return the person to the alive state and can recur. Other mortality hazard.06 excludes modeled overdose mortality. Total baseline fatal hazard is.07; supported hazard.065 during the funded year. Both revert to.07 afterward.

For time t after service start, baseline survival is S0(t)=exp(−h0*t); supported survival is S1(t)=exp(−h0*t+b*min(t,C)). Here b is the fatal-hazard reduction and C the funded duration. Integrate .7*(S1−S0)/(1.03)^t to15years, then discount the.25-year gift delay once. One person's entire finite survival difference is counted, so recurrent reports cannot manufacture repeated lives. Central gain is0.02543946 QALY per additional person, totaling0.508789 US QALYs.

The simplified constant hazards do not reproduce acute post-overdose time-varying risk, ages or treatment transitions. Higher recurrent risk increases possible rescue benefit and future mortality together. Five-year horizon, quarter-year protection, negative rescue effect and independent-harm scenarios expose important limits. There is no extra MOUD/HCV or nonfatal-brain-injury tail on this cohort.
