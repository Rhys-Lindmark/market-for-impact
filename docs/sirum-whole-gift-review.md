# SIRUM whole-organization ordinary-gift review

Version sirum-whole-gift-v1; September 8, 2026. Research packet, not a verified funding offer.

## Outcome and confidence

For a hypothetical unrestricted $100,000 gift, central signed health is 7.634564 QALYs nationally, 0.229037 in Bay residents, and zero priced SF health. Donor costs per 10 QALYs are $130,983 national / $4,366,108 Bay / unpriced SF. Favorable joint assumptions produce 101.570526 QALYs nationally ($9,845/10Q); adverse assumptions are negative. These are uncalibrated scenarios, not a confidence interval or a threshold-tuned recommendation. The model's residual-drug prior supplies 6.012826 of 7.856149 gross clinical QALYs; setting it to zero reduces net health to 1.621738. The apparent near-threshold national result therefore is not a trial-derived estimate.

## Recipient, finance and operating premise

Exact recipient is Supporting Initiatives to Redistribute Unused Medicine–SIRUM, EIN 27-1103057. Its current appeal supports ordinary monetary donations and states $1 to deliver a prescription to a partner. This is a fundraising operating anchor, not an audited full marginal cost or promise of sustained therapy. Central assumes 70% of the whole gift supports this delivery activity; 30% remains in the denominator for other operations/expansion. No legislative or advocacy benefit is modeled.

Primary FY2024 Form 990: $32,354,215 total expense; pharmaceutical/medicine line $23,152,443; expense excluding that line $9,201,772, including depreciation $522,297. The $8,679,475 remainder excluding both is not a cash-flow calculation. Schedule M separately reports medicine received at FMV $23,093,298 and donated securities $981,890. Medicine receipts do not equal medicine expense; inventory movements and possible purchases have not been fully reconciled. Neither retail drug valuation nor aggregate expense/patients supplies a defensible marginal therapy cost. Cash plus temporary investments is $4,742,910; unrestricted net assets $16,056,256 are not an unfunded package. Schedule R's seven disregarded entities include Good Pill pharmacies, California operations and returns processors; their internal activities are not added a second time.

Current county pages verify Better Health Pharmacy is a public Santa Clara pharmacy working with SIRUM, not its subsidiary. Its formulary includes trial-relevant BP and statin medicines and multiple other classes. Stock continuity is expressly not guaranteed; a prescription is required. The county's 2023 operating-cost account is about $1m/year for 40k prescriptions, used only as an approximate outside dispensing planning anchor. Current open marginal capacity is not verified.

## Causal model and every prior's meaning

Prescription means a shipped fill-equivalent in the cost anchor; its duration is UNKNOWN. Central assumes one medication-month per shipped prescription and 70% eventually dispensed; three-month fills are a separate scenario. These are not patient counts. Total dispensed medication-months are allocated to mutually exclusive person cohorts: 20% high-risk BP, 15% high-risk statin, and 65% residual. A person receiving both BP and statin drugs must be assigned to one cohort before counting; all concurrent medicines necessary for that person are charged to that cohort. Central concurrent-drug divisors are 1.5, 1 and 2 respectively. Actual cohort routing and mix are unobserved priors, not a formulary frequency inference.

Funded years defaults to one. Increasing years divides the purchased medication-months across fewer complete courses; it does not create free renewals. Financing additionality 0.5 accounts for other donors/public supply replacing these fills. Treatment contrast 0.5 is the fraction of those patients with a meaningful newly sustained treatment contrast rather than already taking equivalent drugs through coverage, generics or another pharmacy. It is a cohort eligibility mixture, not a correction to published ITT trial adherence. Diagnostic and prescribing capacity remain necessary.

SHEP's older severe isolated-systolic-hypertension patients supply a five-year control stroke risk and ITT relative-rate anchor; HPS's high-risk patients supply a first-major-vascular-event anchor. Clinical transfer priors 0.8 BP and 0.35 statin attenuate the published relative reductions. HPS first-year benefit was not significant; its short-course transfer is particularly weak and has an explicit zero scenario. No per-protocol uplift, separate mortality benefit, recurrent event count, additive drug effect or additional revascularization/stroke count is used.

For each cardiovascular cohort, baseline hazard is -log(1-control five-year risk)/5, an explicit constant-hazard approximation. During funded coverage target hazard is h0×[1-(1-trialRR)×transfer]. After coverage ends, both hazards are h0. Event-free state probabilities are integrated over at most ten years with mortality 0.05 and discount 0.03, plus a 0.25-year discounted implementation delay. This captures finite earlier-care benefit and later first-event catch-up, not lifetime prevention. The utility gap between event-free and first-event states is 0.4 stroke / 0.18 mixed vascular, priors including fatal/nonfatal severity and recovery in a simplified absorbing state. HPS composite includes revascularizations; treating every event as fatal or permanently disabling would be wrong. For statins the entire event-state and medical-cost comparison is truncated at three years, not ten. The 0.18 mixed utility prior can be interpreted as roughly 10% fatal/severe loss0.75, 25% serious nonfatal loss0.35, and 65% short/mild mixed outcomes averaging0.025 across that bounded window (weighted0.17875, rounded0.18); these are uncertain severity/recovery priors, not observed HPS categories. A common post-state mortality hazard is a simplifying uncertain assumption. QALYs cannot exceed funded distinct people × horizon × one utility unit.

Residual expected morbidity is explicitly included rather than silently zero. Its class mix and average utility gains are all priors: psychiatric 20%×0.04, diabetes 15%×0.01, anti-infective 10%×0.05, asthma 15%×0.03, other 40%×0, weighted gain 0.019. Gains occur only during funded coverage, with the same additionality and treatment-contrast filters and competing mortality. No residual future survival or post-course carryover is claimed. These are average effects over all funded medication-months: psychiatric initiation delay/nonresponse, short clinically appropriate infection courses, spontaneous recovery and non-target/prophylactic use must already be averaged down within these gains. They are not a claim of continuous antibiotic benefit or full psychiatric benefit immediately. This uncertain whole-portfolio bridge is the dominant research question; null, favorable and harmful residual scenarios expose its leverage.

All newly exposed fills carry a small 0.00001-QALY medication/interruption harm allowance across all drug classes. It is scaled by financing additionality and treatment contrast, so identical-care financing replacement causes neither linked clinical benefit nor linked medication harm. Independent direct loss and displaced alternative health are separate gift-date PV inputs; central displaced health is 0.1 QALY. Neither independent loss disappears automatically when target efficacy/additionality is zero. They are not calibrated observed harms. Clinical benefit can be zero and net signed health negative.

## Geography and resources

US share1 is a domestic-service prior; Bay share0.03 is independent of the address and not estimated from county cumulative output; SF share0 because no SF-residence throughput is established. A conditional SF scenario is not a claim of exclusion or service absence. The same residency distribution for net benefit and harms is a simplifying prior. All regional prices use the entire $100,000 gift, never a geographically shrunken numerator. Nonpositive health returns null prices, not infinity or a positive recommendation.

Associated outside resources apply to ALL nominal dispensed fills, not merely high-risk successes: half at external pharmacies × $25 dispensing, plus $2 other clinical/participant resources and $0.25 medicine opportunity cost per fill. Internal Good Pill activity is excluded from that external fraction. The drug resource prior is not retail price: medicines otherwise destroyed can have low alternative value, while diverting medicine from another patient is costly and must affect both resources and alternative health. Public/private share70/30 is unmeasured. Gross associated resources equal gift + nominal outside resources + any positive modeled cardiovascular medical change; avoided medical costs are excluded from gross. Conditional net resources use 50% of outside resources as incremental and include the signed medical change. Event unit costs $25k stroke/$15k mixed vascular are planning priors. No patient savings are counted as additional health or a free resource offset.

Central nominal outside cost is $722,750, split $505,925 public/$216,825 private; modeled discounted medical change -$23,343. Gross associated total $822,750 and conditional net $438,032 imply national $1,077,665 and $573,749 per10 QALYs. These are incomplete societal accounts: residual medical-cost changes, unmodeled operational opportunity costs and general equilibrium drug effects remain unpriced. Changing financing additionality alone does not automatically delete associated nominal costs; outside incrementality is an independently exposed assumption.

## Acceptance and next evidence

The actual mechanism and entity pass feasibility. The donor unit-to-month and residual clinical effect do not yet support a strong recommendation. Cheapest decisive evidence: shipped/dispensed quantities in days by class, unique high-risk patients and concurrent drugs, actual stock interruptions, baseline alternative coverage, marginal cash expansion budget, outside pharmacy staffing capacity, and patient residency. No outreach occurred. No claimed verified open tranche.
